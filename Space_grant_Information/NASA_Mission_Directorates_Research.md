## 1. Science Mission Directorate (SMD)

### Mission Statement
NASA's Science Mission Directorate conducts scientific exploration that is enabled by access to space, projecting humankind's vantage point into space with observatories in Earth orbit and deep space, spacecraft visiting the Moon and other planetary bodies, and robotic landers, rovers, and sample return missions. From space, in space, and about space, NASA's science vision encompasses questions as practical as hurricane formation, as enticing as the prospect of lunar resources, as surprising as behavior in weightlessness, and as profound as the origin of the Universe.

### Strategic Priorities (2024-2026)

The 2025-2026 NASA Science Plan continues to build upon the Science 2020-2024 framework with focused priorities:

1. **Collaborative Culture**: Create a more collaborative culture within SMD and across the science community, encouraging diversity of thought, sharing best practices, and informed risk-taking to improve operations
2. **Innovation and Learning**: Continuous innovation and learning as cornerstones of scientific discovery
3. **Future Leaders**: Develop future leaders and inspire learners of all ages through new opportunities and hands-on experiences
4. **Open Science**: Seamless collaboration with ESDMD, SOMD, and STMD Mission Directorates

### Five Scientific Divisions

#### 1.1 Earth Science Division
**Mission**: Studies planet Earth from space to advance scientific understanding and meet societal needs.

**Key Focus Areas**:
- Monitoring global climate change through satellite data
- Understanding the carbon cycle
- Weather patterns and ocean dynamics
- Supporting disaster response efforts with real-time Earth observations
- Climate resilience, health, and ecological conservation

**Major Programs and Initiatives (2024)**:
- **Earth Action Initiative** (formerly Applications Program): User-centered strategies to address global challenges
- **Climate Adaptation Plan (2024-2027)**: Integrates climate risk across mission, operations, and asset management
- **Disaster Response Coordination System** (launched June 2024): Real-time information on fires, earthquakes, landslides, floods, tornadoes, hurricanes, and extreme events
- **ROSES-2024 A.42**: Earth Action: Disaster Risk Reduction, Recovery, and Resilience
- **Land-Cover and Land-Use Change (LCLUC) Program**

**Strategic Priorities**:
1. Innovate
2. Inform
3. Inspire
4. Partner

**Technology Needs**:
- Advanced Earth observation capabilities
- Satellite data processing and analysis
- Real-time disaster monitoring systems
- Climate modeling and prediction

#### 1.2 Planetary Science Division
**Mission**: Advances scientific knowledge of the origin and history of the solar system, the potential for life elsewhere, and the hazards and resources present as humans explore space.

**Key Focus Areas**:
- Understanding solar system history
- Distribution of life within the solar system
- Near-Earth object detection and planetary defense
- Robotic exploration of planets, moons, and asteroids

**Major Missions (2024)**:
- **Europa Clipper**: Launched October 14, 2024; will conduct 49 close flybys of Europa starting in 2030
- **Planetary Defense Program**: Tracking near-Earth objects; NEO Surveyor spacecraft in development
- **Asteroid Impact and Deflection Assessment**: Studying binary asteroid systems
- **Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS)**: Rapid acquisition of lunar delivery services

**CLPS Payload Programs**:
- **NPLP** (NASA Provided Lunar Payloads): 13 instruments selected, ready to fly
- **LSITP** (Lunar Surface Instrument and Technology Payloads): 12 small payloads selected in 2019
- **PRISM** (Payloads and Research Investigations on the Surface of the Moon): PI-led instrument suites
- **DALI** (Development and Advancement of Lunar Instrumentation): Develop instruments to TRL 6

**Technology Needs**:
- Planetary defense detection systems
- Sample collection and return technologies
- Lunar surface instrumentation
- Robotic exploration systems

#### 1.3 Astrophysics Division
**Mission**: Aims to expand humanity's understanding of how the universe began and evolved, how it works, and whether there are places beyond Earth where life might thrive.

**Key Focus Areas**:
- Origin, structure, evolution, and destiny of the universe
- Search for Earth-like planets
- Understanding dark matter and dark energy
- Discovering the nature of black holes and neutron stars

**Technology Needs**:
- Space-based telescopes and observatories
- Exoplanet detection and characterization
- High-precision astrometry
- Advanced spectroscopy

#### 1.4 Heliophysics Division
**Mission**: Studies the nature of the Sun, and how it influences the very nature of space – and, in turn, the atmospheres of planets and the technology that exists there.

**Key Focus Areas**:
- Understanding the Sun's influence on space environment
- Space weather prediction and protection
- Solar-terrestrial interactions
- Protecting technology and astronauts from space weather impacts

**Technology Needs**:
- Solar observation instruments
- Space weather monitoring systems
- Magnetosphere and ionosphere sensors
- Radiation detection and protection

#### 1.5 Biological and Physical Sciences (BPS) Division
**Mission**: Pioneers scientific discovery in and beyond low Earth orbit to drive advances in science, technology, and space exploration to enhance knowledge, education, innovation, and economic vitality.

**Note**: Administrative oversight transferred from HEOMD to SMD in 2020.

**Two Main Programs**:

**Space Biology Program**:
- Research on how gravity affects living organisms
- Understanding how biological systems accommodate spaceflight environments
- **TIDES** (Thrive In DEep Space): Determining effects of multiple deep-space stressors (radiation and reduced gravity) on model organisms

**Physical Sciences Program**:
- Investigating fundamental laws of physics using microgravity
- Mechanistic understanding of space exploration technologies:
  - Power generation and storage
  - Space propulsion
  - Life support systems
  - Environmental monitoring and control

**Technology Needs**:
- Microgravity research platforms
- Biological monitoring systems
- Physical sciences instruments
- Data analysis for open science repository

### SMD-Wide Initiatives

#### Open Science and Data
**Policy**: SPD-41 (August 2021) and SPD-41a (December 2022)

**Key Principles**:
- All SMD-funded data made publicly available without fees or restrictions
- Machine-readable data formats
- Robust, standards-compliant metadata
- No period of exclusive access to mission data
- Clear, open, and accessible data licenses

**Infrastructure**:
- Five scientific divisions maintain open data repositories
- **Open Science Data Repository (OSDR)**: One-stop repository for space data
- Science Data Portal for data discovery

#### Citizen Science Programs
**Purpose**: Form of open collaboration where individuals/organizations participate voluntarily in scientific process

**Funding Programs**:
1. **Citizen Science Seed Funding Program (CSSFP)**: For Astrophysics, BPS, Heliophysics, or Planetary Sciences
2. **Citizen Science for Earth Systems Program (CSESP)**: For Earth Science Division
3. **Heliophysics Citizen Science Investigations (HCSI)**: Up to three years of funding for heliophysics

## 2. Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD)

### Mission Statement
NASA's Space Technology Mission Directorate aims to transform future missions while ensuring American leadership in aerospace. STMD develops, demonstrates, and transfers new space technologies that benefit NASA, commercial, and other government missions.

### Strategic Priorities (2024-2026)

#### 2024 Civil Space Shortfall Ranking
**Purpose**: Better integrate the aerospace community's most pervasive technical challenges to guide NASA's space technology development and investments

**Process**:
- Spring 2024: Initiated new collaborative process
- July 2024: Released document with 187 shortfalls (technology areas requiring further development)
- Integrated input from:
  - NASA mission directorates and centers
  - Small and large industry organizations
  - Other government agencies
  - Academia
  - Interested individuals
- 2026 effort: Consolidating shortfalls into 32 broader, integrated categories

**Key Technology Shortfalls Identified**:
- Additive manufacturing
- Cryogenic fluid management
- In-situ resource utilization (ISRU)
- Power generation and storage
- Advanced propulsion
- Life support systems

#### Standing Announcement of Collaboration Opportunity (ACO)
**Status**: Released NNH25ZTR002O for five years
**Structure**: Umbrella opportunity for topic-specific appendix releases every 6-12 months to address evolving space technology needs

### Key Programs and Initiatives

#### 2.1 Lunar Surface Innovation Initiative (LSII)
**Mission**: Works across industry, academia, non-profits, and other government agencies to catalyze development of technology for robotic and human exploration of the Moon and future operations on Mars

**Focus Areas**:
- Lunar surface operations
- Technology demonstration
- Commercial partnerships
- Mars preparation

#### 2.2 NASA Innovative Advanced Concepts (NIAC)
**Mission**: Nurtures visionary ideas that could transform future NASA missions with breakthroughs—radically better or entirely new aerospace architectures, systems, or missions

**Characteristics**:
- Far-term, visionary concepts
- High-risk, high-reward
- Revolutionary approaches
- Early-stage innovation (typically TRL 1-3)

#### 2.3 Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) and Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR)
**Purpose**: Provide opportunities for small, high-tech companies and research institutions

**Benefits**:
- Commercial technology development
- Academic partnerships
- Innovation from small businesses
- Technology transfer

#### 2.4 Small Spacecraft Technology Program
**Mission**: Expands the ability to execute unique missions through rapid development and demonstration of capabilities for small spacecraft

**Characteristics**:
- Small, affordable, rapid, and transformative
- Applicable to exploration, science, and commercial space sector
- Technology Readiness Level (TRL) 5 or above for state-of-the-art
- "On the horizon" technologies in development

**State-of-the-Art Report 2024**:
- Published information current as of September 30, 2024
- Covers SmallSat and CubeSat technologies
- Identifies emerging capabilities

**Technology Categories**:
- Propulsion systems
- Power and energy storage
- Communications
- Attitude determination and control
- Structures and mechanisms
- Thermal control
- Command and data handling
- Software and autonomy

#### 2.5 Starling Mission
**Purpose**: Advancing the readiness of various technologies for cooperative groups of spacecraft (distributed missions, clusters, or swarms)

**Focus**:
- Autonomous coordination
- Multi-spacecraft operations
- Distributed sensing
- Formation flying

#### 2.6 Game Changing Development Program
**Mission**: Develops and demonstrates novel technologies for space applications

**BIG Idea 2024**:
- Focus on low Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP) inflatable technologies
- Structures and systems for lunar operations
- Innovative concepts with inflatable components

### Technology Development Framework

#### Technology Readiness Levels (TRLs)
**Origin**: Conceived at NASA in 1974, formally defined in 1989
**Scale**: Nine levels (TRL 1 lowest, TRL 9 highest)

**Early-Stage Innovation (Relevant for Undergraduate Research)**:
- **TRL 1**: Basic principles observed and reported; scientific research translating to future R&D
- **TRL 2**: Technology concept and/or application formulated; applied research focusing scientific principles on specific application area
- **TRL 3**: Analytical and experimental critical function and/or characteristic proof of concept

**Knowledge Transfer**: NASA transfers knowledge at all TRL levels, especially when working with industry and academia on collaborative projects

### Research Focus Areas

1. **In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU)**
   - Lunar South Pole focus (highland regolith and water/volatiles in shadowed regions)
   - Water/ice extraction from lunar regolith
   - Resource processing for life support and propulsion
   - Long-term investments in ISRU technology

   **Water Extraction Technologies**:
   - Drilling-based thermal methods
   - Auger-based systems with infrared heating
   - Planetary Volatiles Extractors (PVEx): "Sniffer," "Mobile In-Situ Water Extraction (MISWE)," "Corer"
   - Processing water into oxygen (breathing) and hydrogen (fuel)

2. **Advanced Propulsion**
   - Electric propulsion
   - Solar sails
   - Nuclear propulsion concepts
   - Chemical propulsion innovations

3. **Power Systems**
   - Solar power technologies
   - Nuclear power for deep space
   - Energy storage
   - Wireless power transmission

4. **Autonomous Systems**
   - Spacecraft autonomy
   - Robotic operations
   - AI/ML for space systems

5. **Advanced Manufacturing**
   - Additive manufacturing in space
   - In-space assembly
   - Novel materials
   - 3D printing

6. **Communications**
   - Small spacecraft communications
   - Deep space networks
   - Laser/optical communications
   - Miniaturized systems

## 3. Exploration Systems Development Mission Directorate (ESDMD)

### Mission Statement
The Exploration Systems Development Mission Directorate defines and manages systems development for programs critical to NASA's Artemis program and planning for NASA's Moon to Mars exploration approach. ESDMD manages human exploration system development for lunar orbital, lunar surface, and Mars exploration.

### Strategic Framework

#### Moon to Mars Program Office
**Responsibilities**:
- Hardware development
- Mission integration
- Risk management
- Oversight of:
  - Space Launch System (SLS) rocket
  - Orion spacecraft
  - Supporting ground systems
  - Human Landing Systems (HLS)
  - Spacesuits
  - Gateway
  - Deep space exploration systems

### Moon to Mars Objectives and Architecture

#### Strategic Approach
**Framework**: Objectives-based (not capabilities-based) approach to human exploration

**2024 Architecture Update**: Incorporates feedback from U.S. industry, academia, international partners, and NASA workforce

#### Core Objectives

**Lunar Infrastructure Goal**: Create an interoperable global lunar utilization infrastructure where U.S. industry and international partners can maintain continuous robotic and human presence on the lunar surface for a robust lunar economy without NASA as the sole user, while accomplishing science objectives and testing for Mars.

**Mars Infrastructure Goal**: Create essential infrastructure to support initial human Mars exploration campaign.

### Major Programs and Systems

#### 3.1 Artemis Program
**Mission**: Return humans to the Moon and establish sustainable lunar presence as preparation for Mars

**Timeline**:
- Artemis II: Crewed lunar flyby
- Artemis III: First crewed lunar landing of the program
- Beyond: Sustained lunar operations

**Recent Hardware Progress (2024)**:
- Multi-mission hardware transported on NASA's Pegasus barge:
  - Artemis II launch vehicle stage adapter
  - "Boat-tail" of core stage for Artemis III
  - Core stage engine section for Artemis IV
  - Ground support equipment

#### 3.2 Gateway (Lunar Outpost)
**Purpose**: Critical element of deep space infrastructure; permanent U.S.-led outpost in cislunar space

**Timeline**: NASA committed to launching Gateway initial capability by December 2027

**Budget**: $5.3 billion for initial capability

**Initial Components**:
- **Power and Propulsion Element (PPE)**
- **Habitation and Logistics Outpost (HALO)**

**Functions**:
- Empowering Artemis program
- Stepping-stone to Mars
- Communications relay
- Science platform
- Staging point for lunar surface operations

#### 3.3 Space Launch System (SLS)
**Purpose**: Heavy-lift launch vehicle for deep space missions

**Capabilities**:
- Launching Orion spacecraft
- Delivering large payloads to lunar vicinity
- Enabling crewed missions beyond LEO

#### 3.4 Orion Spacecraft
**Purpose**: Crew vehicle for deep space exploration

**Capabilities**:
- Crew transport to/from lunar vicinity
- Life support for extended missions
- Re-entry and recovery

#### 3.5 Human Landing Systems (HLS)
**Purpose**: Transport crew between lunar orbit and surface

**Development**: Commercial partnerships for lander development

#### 3.6 Lunar Terrain Vehicle (LTV)
**Requirements** (under development):
- Traverse from one landing zone to another
- Increase exploration range beyond maximum suited walking distance
- Reusable and rechargeable
- Approximately 10-year service life

#### 3.7 Advanced Spacesuits
**Purpose**: Next-generation suits for lunar surface operations

**Capabilities**:
- Enhanced mobility
- Extended duration
- Improved safety
- Lunar dust mitigation

### 2024 Technology Gaps

**Architecture-Driven Gaps**: NASA applied rigorous systems engineering processes to identify and prioritize technology gaps

**Initial Assessment**: 56 total gaps identified; list changes annually

**Five High-Priority Technology Gaps** (2024):
*Specific gaps detailed in 2024 Moon to Mars Architecture Concept Review document*

**Investment Strategy**: Technology gaps inform STMD and ESDMD development investments

### Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS)

**ESDMD Role**:
- "CX-#" designation for ESDMD-led deliveries
- Integration with science payloads

**Delivery Cadence**: Expected two CLPS deliveries per year to lunar surface

## 4. Space Operations Mission Directorate (SOMD)

### Mission Statement
SOMD's goals include enabling sustained human exploration missions, scientific discovery and advanced operations in the solar system; managing NASA crewed space operations in low-Earth orbit (LEO) and commercial launch services; and performing scientific research to enable sustained human exploration and solve health challenges for humans on Earth.

### Core Responsibilities

SOMD manages NASA's current and future space operations in and beyond low-Earth orbit, including:
- International Space Station operations
- Commercial launch services to ISS
- Exploration systems operations and maintenance
- Space transportation systems development and operation
- Space transportation services for NASA and NASA-sponsored payloads requiring orbital launch
- Space communications and navigation services supporting all NASA space systems

### Strategic Priorities (2024-2026)

1. Maintain human presence in low-Earth orbit
2. Support ISS operations and research
3. Enable commercial space transportation
4. Provide communications and navigation services for all NASA missions
5. Transition to commercial LEO destinations

### Key Programs

#### 4.1 International Space Station (ISS)
**Status**: Operating through approximately 2030

**Functions**:
- Microgravity research platform
- Technology demonstration
- Crew operations and training
- International collaboration
- Commercial partnerships

**Research Priorities**:
- Approximately 200 scientific investigations annually
- Permanent presence with at least two crew members
- Biological and physical sciences
- Human health and performance
- Technology development
- Earth observation

#### 4.2 ISS National Laboratory
**Purpose**: U.S. portion of ISS available for research by academia, industry, and government

**Research Focus Areas** (2024):
- Regenerative medicine
- Microgravity effects on bone loss
- Orbital data processing capabilities
- Commercial applications for improving life on Earth
- In-space production applications
- Advanced materials and manufacturing

**Funding Opportunities**:
- In-Space Production Applications (released 2024)
- Various research solicitations throughout the year

#### 4.3 Commercial Crew Program
**Purpose**: Transport astronauts to and from ISS using commercial partners

**Partners**:
- **SpaceX**: Crew Dragon
- **Boeing**: Starliner

**Recent Missions (2024)**:
- **Crew-8**: Launched March 1, 2024 (SpaceX Falcon 9)
  - Crew: Matthew Dominick, Michael Barratt, Jeanette Epps (NASA); Alexander Grebenkin (Roscosmos)
  - Multiple ISS National Lab-sponsored investigations

- **Starliner Crew Flight Test**: May 2024 (Butch Wilmore, Suni Williams)

**Impact**:
- Significantly increased crew time available for research
- Expanded potential for commercial use of ISS
- More crew members = more time for scientific research and technology demonstrations

#### 4.4 Commercial Cargo Program
**Purpose**: Deliver supplies, equipment, and research payloads to ISS

**Partners and Status (2024)**:
- **SpaceX**: 30 commercial resupply services missions by March 2024
- **Northrop Grumman**: 20 missions by January 2024

**Capabilities**:
- Cargo delivery
- Return capability
- Express rack integration
- Science payload accommodation

#### 4.5 Commercial LEO Development (CLD) Program
**Purpose**: Support development of commercially owned and operated space stations in LEO from which NASA and other customers can purchase services

**Strategic Goal**: Stimulate growth of commercial activities in microgravity

**Timeline**: ISS transition to commercial stations around 2030

**Phase 1 Partners**:
1. **Blue Origin** (partnership with Sierra Space)
2. **Starlab Space** (joint venture between Voyager Space and Airbus)
3. **Axiom Space** (firm fixed price contract):
   - Design and develop commercial modules
   - Initially attach to ISS
   - Detach prior to ISS retirement

**Service Requirements**:
- Maintain NASA's ability for permanent LEO presence
- Support at least two crew members
- Approximately 200 scientific investigations annually
- Ensure continuity of research capabilities

**Challenge**: Potential gap between ISS end-of-operations and commercial station availability

#### 4.6 Space Communications and Navigation (SCaN)
**Purpose**: Provide communications and navigation services for all NASA missions

**Major Technology Development**:

**Deep Space Optical Communications (DSOC)**:
- Managed by JPL under STMD and SOMD SCaN program
- Laser-based communications demonstration

**2024 Achievements**:
- **April 2024**: Transmitted engineering data from 140 million miles at max rate of 25 Mbps
- **June 2024**: Sustained downlink of 6.25 Mbps (max 8.3 Mbps) from 240 million miles
- **July 29, 2024**: Successfully commanded flight instrument from 288 million miles; completed Phase 1
- **December 3, 2024**: Record-breaking 307 million miles downlink (farther than average Earth-Mars distance)
- **Summer 2024**: Laser signal sent to Psyche spacecraft at 290 million miles

**Technology Impact**:
- Lasers transport data at rates up to 100 times higher than radio frequencies
- Enables complex scientific information transmission
- High-definition imagery and video
- Critical for future human Mars missions

**Services**:
- Near-Earth Network
- Space Network
- Deep Space Network
- Lunar communications
- Optical/laser communications

## 5. Aeronautics Research Mission Directorate (ARMD)

### Mission Statement
ARMD's vision is for future air transportation systems that are safe and sustainable, increase personal mobility, and enhance U.S. competitiveness in the global aviation sector. The directorate encompasses a broad range of technologies to meet future needs of the aviation community, the nation, and the world for safe, efficient, flexible, and environmentally sustainable air transportation.

### Strategic Implementation Plan
**Latest Update**: 2023 (renewed focus on next 25 years and beyond)

**Primary Focus**: Enabling and innovating sustainable aviation using green technologies, working with partners in government, industry, and academia to enable dramatic reductions in fuel use and emissions in future airliner designs.

### Six Strategic Thrusts (Research Portfolio Organization)

#### 5.1 Safe, Efficient Growth in Global Operations
**Focus**: Supporting continued growth in air traffic while maintaining or improving safety

**Research Areas**:
- Air traffic management
- NextGen evolution
- System-wide safety
- Operational efficiency
- Capacity enhancement

#### 5.2 Innovation in Commercial Supersonic Aircraft
**Focus**: Enabling return of commercial supersonic flight

**Research Areas**:
- Sonic boom reduction
- Environmental impact mitigation
- Efficient supersonic propulsion
- Materials for high-speed flight

#### 5.3 Ultra-Efficient Commercial Vehicles
**Focus**: Dramatic improvements in aircraft fuel efficiency

**Research Areas**:
- Advanced aerodynamics
- Lightweight structures
- Efficient propulsion integration
- Novel configurations

#### 5.4 Transition to Low-Carbon Propulsion
**Focus**: Reducing or eliminating aviation carbon emissions

**Priority Research Areas**:

**Electrified Aircraft Propulsion**:
- **Electrified Powertrain Flight Demonstration (EPFD)**: Ground and flight tests of electrified aircraft propulsion technologies
- Goal: Enable new generation of hybrid electric-powered aircraft
- Commitment: Support U.S. goal of net-zero greenhouse gas emissions from aviation by 2050

### Proposal Alignment Strategy

When aligning undergraduate research proposals with NASA Mission Directorates:

1. **Identify Primary Directorate**: Determine which directorate's mission best aligns with research focus
2. **Find Supporting Directorates**: Identify cross-cutting applications to other directorates
3. **Use Keywords**: Incorporate directorate-specific terminology and priorities
4. **Address Strategic Goals**: Explicitly connect to 2024-2026 priorities and strategic thrusts
5. **Demonstrate Feasibility**: Match scope to undergraduate capabilities (early TRL, component-level, etc.)
6. **Show Pathway to Impact**: Explain how research contributes to larger NASA objectives
7. **Emphasize Training**: Highlight workforce development and STEM engagement aspects
8. **Include Partnerships**: Identify potential NASA center, industry, or institutional collaborations
9. **Reference Specific Programs**: Connect to named initiatives (Artemis, CLPS, AAM, etc.)
10. **Address Technology Gaps**: Link research to identified needs in relevant directorate
