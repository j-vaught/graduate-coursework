Developing and validating radar detection and tracking algorithms for maritime applications requires large volumes of labeled data with diverse target and clutter scenarios—data that is expensive to collect and difficult to annotate at scale. We present a multi-fidelity simulation framework for X-band marine radar that spans three computational tiers: (1) a high-fidelity electromagnetic ray-tracing engine operating on fully rendered 3D world geometry with material-dependent scattering, (2) a medium-fidelity approximation layer using analytical radar cross-section models and statistical clutter synthesis, and (3) a low-fidelity data-driven compositor that manipulates real radar recordings to generate novel scenarios. The ray-tracing tier provides physics-grounded ground truth for algorithm validation, while the lighter tiers enable rapid dataset augmentation and real-time hardware-in-the-loop testing. We describe the architecture of each simulation mode, their computational trade-offs, and demonstrate their application to training and benchmarking detection algorithms for small maritime targets. The framework is designed for extensibility across radar bands, platform configurations, and environmental conditions.
