Small unmanned systems performing maritime domain awareness, search-and-rescue, and coastal monitoring require robust on-board radar detection under tight size, weight, power, and cost constraints. Tuning Constant False Alarm Rate (CFAR) detectors for littoral scenes is notoriously difficult due to heavy-tailed sea clutter, land/sea transitions, and shoreline multipath, yet remains essential for low-latency situational awareness. We present a human-supervised methodology for tuning CFAR parameters in coastal radar scenes with stationary or persistent targets. A practitioner provides bounding boxes around reference objects (e.g., navigation aids, towers, shoreline structures) on one or more frames; the system then (i) propagates those regions across a frame sequence, (ii) sweeps the parameter spaces of multiple CFAR variants (cell-averaging, order-statistic, censored, and others), and (iii) solves a multi-objective optimization maximizing true detections inside annotated regions while minimizing false alarms elsewhere under an explicit PFA constraint. The outcome is a scene- and sensor-tailored algorithm/parameter configuration. We validate on synthetic sea-clutter (K/Pareto distributions) and real coastal radar data, targeting improved persistent detection of operator-specified objects with reduced background false alarms compared to single-algorithm default tuning.
