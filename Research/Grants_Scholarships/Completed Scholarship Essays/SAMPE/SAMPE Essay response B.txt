Essay response B: If you could design a college curriculum, what would it be or look like?

When tasked with designing a college curriculum, my focus would be on creating a comprehensive Mechatronics/Robotics Engineering Degree. This proposal centers around a single major and assumes that finding professors with a pure robotics focus may be challenging. Therefore, the number of dedicated robotics courses is limited. I had designed this curriculum during a trip to Disney World a few months ago when I was bored and was unmotivated to do some other homework.

My curriculum is designed to balance comprehensiveness and workload, totaling 130 credit hours. Additionally, I've compiled a list of electives that would complement the degree well.

The proposed curriculum unfolds as follows:

First Semester: This foundational semester focuses on general electives including history, English, Calculus I, Chemistry I, and an introduction to Mechatronics Engineering.

Second Semester: Students are introduced to Computer-Aided Design (CAD) and Simulation Program with Integrated Circuit Emphasis (SPICE), alongside basic Direct Current (DC) circuit analysis.

Third Semester: This phase delves into Alternating Current (AC) circuit analysis and Mechanical Engineering statics, supplemented with Physics, Math, and an elective.

Fourth Semester: This challenging semester covers Signals and Systems, introductory Electrical Engineering lab work, an introduction to Algorithmic Design (via Java programming), and Solid Mechanics.

Fifth Semester: This is a demanding period that would witness the most dropouts. Students engage in an intensive Electronics course, paired with a 30-hour per week Electronics Lab. Concurrently, they tackle Dynamics, Linear Algebra, and additional programming classes.

Sixth Semester: Though difficult, this term is less time-consuming, featuring coursework in C++ programming, Field-Programmable Gate Array (FPGA) design, advanced Solid Mechanics (focusing on Mechanical Machine Design), a Mechatronics Lab (centering around Arduino), and Digital Signal Processing.

Seventh & Eighth Semesters: These final phases are less strenuous. In the seventh semester, students study Kinematics, delve into Robotics in Mechanical Engineering, learn Control Theory, undertake their first Capstone project, and choose a Humanities elective. The final eighth semester comprises three Mechatronics electives, a Humanities elective, and the second part of the Capstone project.

When considering electives, I included at least one from each contributing major:

CAD-related Electives: Finite Element Analysis, Advanced CAD

Electrical Engineering Electives: Two Controls courses, two Power Systems courses, a Communications Systems course, and a System Health Management course.

Computer Science Electives: Three options for FPGA Design, two options for Machine Learning, a Robotics course, a Human-Machine Interaction course, and a System Simulations class.

In summary, I believe this comprehensive Mechatronics/Robotics curriculum provides a balance of theoretical and practical education, blending broad foundations with specialized courses and hands-on labs.
