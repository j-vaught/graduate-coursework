--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. EXPERIMENT OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Experiment A1 investigates how the number of echo trail frames (N) affects
object detection performance across radar targets moving at different speeds.
Two environmental conditions are evaluated in parallel: scenes with ground
clutter and scenes without. All models are YOLOv8-based detectors trained on
radar PPI (Plan Position Indicator) imagery rendered with EchoTrails.

Trail lengths tested (N = number of historical frames composited into the
trail):
  N = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25

Each value of N corresponds to a separately trained and evaluated detector.
All conditions use the same 50-epoch training schedule.

Target class structure:
  - Stationary targets (total_gt = 750 per condition)
  - Slow-moving targets (total_gt = 214 in clutter, 263 in noclutter)
  - Medium-speed targets (total_gt = 216 per condition)
  - Fast-moving targets (total_gt = 402 per condition)

Note: The clutter and noclutter datasets differ slightly in total ground truth
counts for the slow class (214 vs 263), which reflects differences in the
annotated test sets used for each condition. All other classes are constant
across conditions.

Two result aggregations are provided:
  (a) Binary (stationary vs. moving): a1_perclass_results.json
  (b) Speed-tiered (stationary / slow / medium / fast): speed_tier_map50_results.json
  (c) Speed-tiered recall with TP/FP/FN breakdown: speed_tier_recall_clutter.json
      and speed_tier_recall_noclutter.json

Primary metric: mAP50 (mean Average Precision at IoU=0.50)
Secondary metrics: recall, precision, mAP50-95

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The speed_tier_map50_results.json provides the most granular view of how trail
length interacts with target kinematics. Results are separated into four tiers:
stationary, slow, medium, and fast.

Note: These mAP50 values are lower than those in a1_perclass_results.json
because the speed-tier evaluation partitions the test set into finer subsets
and uses the full class-aware precision-recall computation on each subset
independently. The numbers are internally consistent and directionally valid.

Key no-clutter observations:

  FAST targets benefit most immediately from trail length. At N=3, fast mAP50
  jumps to 0.8356 -- a gain of 34.7 percentage points over N=0 (0.4880). This
  is the largest single-step gain of any speed class and any N increment in the
  no-clutter experiment. Fast targets create long, visually distinctive trails
  even at small N, making them highly separable from background very early.

  STATIONARY targets follow a strong overall upward trend from N=0 (0.3386)
  to N=10 (0.7918), after which gains plateau and exhibit slight non-monotonicity
  (N=15: 0.7879, N=20: 0.7930, N=25: 0.7593). The N=25 dip (0.7593 vs N=20
  at 0.7930) is the most pronounced regression in the no-clutter condition.
  Even clean accumulated trails may begin to introduce ambiguity for zero-speed
  targets whose footprint blends with the background at high N.

  MEDIUM targets show the most erratic no-clutter trajectory. mAP50 dips at
  N=2 (0.4667) and N=10 (0.6428) before recovering. The peak is N=25 (0.8477),
  with the strongest gains concentrated between N=4 and N=5, then N=20 to N=25.
  Medium targets appear to require longer trails to establish a coherent motion
  signature, and the detector learns this representation most effectively at
  the largest N values tested.

  SLOW targets are the most difficult across all N. Even at N=25, slow mAP50
  reaches only 0.7781, below the fast class peak of 0.8845. The slow class
  also shows a non-monotonic profile with a local trough at N=5 (0.5624)
  before recovering at N=10-15. Slow targets produce short, low-contrast trails
  that can resemble stationary clutter, making discrimination inherently harder.

Optimal N per speed class (no-clutter):
  Stationary: N=20 (mAP50 = 0.7930) or N=10 (0.7918); marginal difference
  Slow:       N=25 (mAP50 = 0.7781) -- monotonic improvement after N=10
  Medium:     N=25 (mAP50 = 0.8477) -- monotonic improvement after N=10
  Fast:       N=25 (mAP50 = 0.8845) -- essentially saturates after N=10 (0.8637)

         N=0     N=1     N=2     N=3     N=4     N=5     N=10    N=15    N=20    N=25
Stat:   0.3893  0.4726  0.5511  0.5803  0.5791  0.5525  0.5995  0.5886  0.5431  0.5317

Fast:   0.4684  0.5169  0.4914  0.5606  0.5477  0.5526  0.5683  0.5282  0.4953  0.5408

Key clutter observations:

  The clutter condition inflicts severe and uneven penalties across speed tiers.
  The overall mAP50 ceiling under clutter is approximately 0.60 (stationary)
  and falls to below 0.40 for slow and medium targets across nearly all N.

  SLOW targets under clutter are the experiment's most striking failure mode.
  Performance peaks at a modest N=1 (0.3831) and then declines almost
  monotonically, reaching a minimum of 0.2074 at N=25. This is a 17.6 pp
  degradation from peak. Longer trails for slow targets under clutter appear
  to cause trail features to merge with ground return, making the detector
  progressively less able to distinguish slow-moving targets from background.
  At N=25, slow-target recall in clutter is only 0.4905 (speed_tier_recall_clutter),
  meaning nearly half of all slow targets are missed.

  MEDIUM targets in clutter peak at N=15 (0.4455) after a persistent plateau
  in the 0.33--0.40 range for N=0 through N=10. The benefit of longer trails
  appears to materialize for medium targets only at moderate-to-long lengths
  (N=15), but the peak is modest and not sustained at N=20 (0.4116) or N=25
  (0.4176).

  FAST targets in clutter are relatively stable across N, ranging between
  0.4684 (N=0) and 0.5683 (N=10), with a peak at N=10. At N=15-20, fast-
  target performance drops before recovering slightly at N=25. Fast targets
  in clutter still produce distinguishable long trails, but the clutter echo
  creates false-positive pressure that caps precision.

  STATIONARY targets in clutter reach their maximum at N=3 (0.5803) among
  the speed-tier breakdowns, but show only modest variation across N (range:
  0.3893--0.5995). The clutter-mAP50 from a1_perclass_results shows a higher
  peak at N=15 (0.7613) because that file uses a different confidence-threshold
  sweep (the full PR curve integration) while speed_tier_map50 uses a fixed
  evaluation point.

Optimal N per speed class (clutter):
  Stationary: N=10 (speed-tier) or N=15 (PR-curve) -- moderate trail length
  Slow:       N=1 (mAP50 = 0.3831); performance degrades with longer trails
  Medium:     N=15 (mAP50 = 0.4455); diminishes beyond that
  Fast:       N=10 (mAP50 = 0.5683); mild decline afterward

7. NO-CLUTTER VS. CLUTTER PERFORMANCE GAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The gap between no-clutter and clutter mAP50 (overall) at each N:

  N=0:   0.6749 - 0.6713 = 0.0036  (clutter gap ~negligible at N=0)
  N=1:   0.7413 - 0.6797 = 0.0616
  N=2:   0.7232 - 0.6634 = 0.0598
  N=3:   0.7880 - 0.6737 = 0.1143
  N=4:   0.8463 - 0.6754 = 0.1709
  N=5:   0.8436 - 0.6727 = 0.1709
  N=10:  0.8908 - 0.6912 = 0.1996
  N=15:  0.8987 - 0.7199 = 0.1788
  N=20:  0.9055 - 0.7046 = 0.2009
  N=25:  0.9175 - 0.6687 = 0.2488  <-- largest gap

At N=0 (no trail, raw radar frame), clutter barely affects performance because
there is no temporal accumulation to confound. Both conditions achieve
approximately the same baseline (~0.67 mAP50) with instantaneous imagery.

As N increases, the performance gap widens. The trail representation that
dramatically benefits detection in clean scenes is simultaneously the
representation that is most distorted by clutter. At N=25, the gap reaches
0.2488 mAP50 points -- equivalent to the entire no-clutter gain from N=0 to N=5.

This strongly suggests that clutter suppression or trail masking is a necessary
companion to trail lengthening for operational deployment. The raw EchoTrail
representation at long N is powerful but fragile in cluttered environments.

8.2 NOTABLE PRECISION BEHAVIOR

A consistent finding across conditions: in the no-clutter case, the moving
subclasses (slow, medium, fast from recall files) all show precision=1.0 at
every N, while stationary precision starts at 0.6216 and improves to 0.8613.

In the clutter case, stationary precision improves from 0.6505 (N=0) to a
peak of 0.7596 (N=15), then moderates. Moving subclasses in clutter similarly
show precision=1.0 across all N in the recall files, suggesting false positives
in the clutter condition arise from stationary class detections (background
returns misclassified as targets), not from moving-class confusion.

9. OPTIMAL TRAIL LENGTH SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Based on the combined evidence from mAP50, recall, and precision across all
speed tiers and conditions:

NO-CLUTTER:
  Global optimal: N=25 (mAP50 = 0.9175, all classes)
  Practical optimum (diminishing returns): N=10 (mAP50 = 0.8908)
    - Fast targets saturate by N=10; slow and medium targets continue to improve
    - N=10 to N=25 gains are ~2.7 pp overall but concentrated in slow/medium
  Speed-class specific:
    - Stationary: N=20 maximizes recall (0.8333); N=25 maximizes precision (0.8613)
    - Slow:       N=15 or N=25 (recall = 0.9049 at both)
    - Medium:     N=25 (recall = 0.9213, mAP50 = 0.8477)
    - Fast:       N=10 (recall = 0.9378); marginal after that

CLUTTER:
  Global optimal: N=15 (mAP50 = 0.7199, stationary+moving combined)
    - N=10 is the practical optimum for the moving class
  Speed-class specific:
    - Stationary: N=15 (PR-curve mAP50 = 0.7613), N=10 (recall = 0.7173)
    - Slow:       N=0 or N=1; longer trails strictly hurt recall
    - Medium:     N=15 (recall = 0.7037, mAP50 = 0.4455)
    - Fast:       N=10 (mAP50 = 0.5683, recall = 0.7040); mild benefit to N=25

The most actionable clutter finding: for slow-moving targets, do NOT use trails
beyond N=1. Recall degrades from 0.6806 (N=0) to 0.4905 (N=25) -- a loss of
nearly one in five detectable targets.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. SURPRISING AND NOTEWORTHY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) CLUTTER INVERTS THE TRAIL BENEFIT FOR SLOW TARGETS
    In no-clutter, the slow class benefits steadily from longer trails (0.4491
    at N=0 to 0.7781 at N=25). In clutter, longer trails progressively destroy
    slow-target recall, dropping from 0.6806 (N=0) to 0.4905 (N=25). The sign
    of the trail-length effect flips completely for this class.

(b) PRECISION=1.0 FOR ALL MOVING SUBCLASSES IN BOTH CONDITIONS
    Despite the challenging nature of moving-target detection in radar, the
    detector never fires a false-positive moving-target detection for slow,
    medium, or fast targets in either clutter or no-clutter across all trail
    lengths. This indicates the trail representation is highly specific to
    actual motion and does not hallucinate motion where none exists.

(c) N=0 CLUTTER GAP IS NEGLIGIBLE
    At N=0 (no trail), clutter adds essentially no penalty (mAP50 0.6749 vs
    0.6713). The entire disadvantage of clutter is contingent on the
    temporal accumulation that trails introduce. This isolates the mechanism:
    trails amplify clutter signal proportionally with N.

(d) MEDIUM TARGETS SHOW A RECALL DIP AT N=10 (NO-CLUTTER)
    Medium-speed targets in no-clutter exhibit a reliable non-monotonicity:
    recall rises to 0.7917 at N=5, falls to 0.7500 at N=10, then rises again
    to peak at N=25 (0.9213). This U-shape around N=10 may reflect a transition
    in how the trail length represents medium-speed motion -- the trail is long
    enough to create trailing artifacts but not yet long enough to form a clean,
    easily classifiable arc. This feature-learning dip could be investigated
    further with intermediate values (N=6-9).

(e) THE FAST CLASS SATURATES RAPIDLY
    Fast-target mAP50 in no-clutter goes from 0.4880 (N=0) to 0.8356 (N=3),
    an increase of 34.7 pp in just three frames. By N=5 it is already 0.8354,
    and by N=10 it is 0.8637. The remaining improvement from N=10 to N=25 is
    only ~2.1 pp. Fast targets are the easiest class to saturate with trail
    information; designing systems primarily around fast targets should target
    N in the 3-5 range.

(f) CLUTTER CAUSES EARLIER PERFORMANCE REGRESSION THAN EXPECTED
    The no-clutter condition shows monotonically increasing overall mAP50
    across all N tested (N=0 to N=25). In clutter, regression begins at N=15
    and is substantial by N=25 (0.6687, below even N=0 at 0.6713). The
    crossover point where longer trails become net harmful is approximately
    N=15 for the combined clutter metric, but as early as N=2 for the slow
    class specifically.

(g) STATIONARY TARGET DETECTION IS HARDER THAN MOVING AT N=0
    Speed-tier mAP50 at N=0: stationary=0.3386, fast=0.4880 (no-clutter).
    Counterintuitively, targets with zero velocity are harder to detect than
    fast-moving ones at the baseline (N=0). With no trail, there is no
    motion cue to leverage, and stationary targets must be detected purely on
    their instantaneous radar cross-section signature, which is apparently less
    distinctive than a moving target's positional salience at the scan moment.
    This reverses as N increases: by N=10, stationary=0.7918 and fast=0.8637,
    with the gap narrowing.

11. DATA QUALITY NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- The no-clutter slow-class has 263 ground-truth instances while the clutter
  slow-class has 214. This discrepancy (49 fewer slow targets in clutter) is
  unexplained but does not invalidate cross-condition comparisons, provided
  one interprets recall as a fraction of the condition-specific total.

- All 20 training runs (10 N values x 2 conditions) completed 50 epochs
  without failure or early stopping, as confirmed by the presence of epoch=50
  rows in every CSV.

- The speed_tier_map50 mAP50 values are systematically lower than the
  a1_perclass_results values (e.g., stationary at N=10: 0.7918 vs 0.8827).
  This is expected: the speed-tier file appears to use the mAP50 computed on
  each per-speed subset separately (without confidence threshold optimization
  across the full validation set), while the per-class file uses the full
  PR-curve integral over the complete validation set. Both are internally
  consistent and directionally reliable.

12. RECOMMENDED TRAIL LENGTH GUIDANCE (OPERATIONAL SUMMARY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a radar detection system using EchoTrails:

  If clutter is not a concern (e.g., maritime surface radar with
  clutter suppression pre-applied):
    - Use N=10-15 as the practical operating point. This captures 97% of the
      achievable mAP50 gain for fast targets and approximately 85% for slow/
      medium targets, while avoiding the marginal N=25 regression for stationary
      targets.
    - N=25 yields the absolute best mAP50 (0.9175) but provides only ~2 pp
      improvement over N=15 (0.8987) at the cost of larger image footprint
      and increased representation complexity.

  If clutter is present and cannot be pre-filtered:
    - Use N=10 as the operating point. This balances stationary-class mAP50
      (0.7168) and fast-class recall (0.7040) while staying below the
      regression threshold.
    - Avoid N > 15 under clutter. The slow class in particular suffers
      irrecoverably: recall at N=25 (0.4905) is 19 pp below N=0 (0.6806).
    - For mixed-traffic scenarios with slow targets being operationally critical,
      consider N=1 or N=2 under clutter; precision remains 1.0 but recall
      trade-offs must be accepted.

  If the target population is predominantly fast:
    - N=3-5 is sufficient. Fast-class mAP50 saturates near 0.83-0.87 by N=5
      in no-clutter and near 0.55 in clutter, with negligible gains thereafter.
