--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. EXPERIMENT OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Experiment A4 evaluates how the choice of color mapping scheme for rendering
EchoTrail images affects downstream object detection performance. Four color
mapping strategies are compared across two environmental conditions, yielding
eight total experimental conditions.

COLOR MAPPING SCHEMES TESTED:
  - mono:       Single-channel grayscale. Trail history rendered in a single hue
                with no explicit color encoding of temporal or intensity
                information.
  - twotone:    Two-color scheme. Uses a binary color split to visually separate
                some property (likely current return vs. historical echo, or
                foreground vs. background).
  - gradient:   Multi-step color gradient. Encodes either trail age or radar
                reflectivity along a continuous color ramp, giving the detector
                smooth perceptual cues about temporal ordering or signal strength.
  - intensity:  Color encoding tied directly to radar return intensity (dBZ or
                equivalent). The hue or brightness of each pixel reflects the
                strength of the radar echo, not merely its age.

CLUTTER CONDITIONS:
  - noclutter:  Clean radar imagery; ground clutter suppression applied or
                scenarios with minimal clutter sources.
  - clutter:    Radar imagery with realistic ground clutter present; tests
                robustness of each color scheme when the scene contains false
                targets and interference.

DETECTION TARGET CLASSES:
  Two semantic classes -- stationary and moving -- plus speed tiers:
  - slow:   263 ground-truth instances
  - medium: 216 ground-truth instances
  - fast:   402 ground-truth instances

GROUND TRUTH COUNTS are identical across all color schemes within the same
clutter condition, confirming a controlled, identical test set. Total annotated
instances per clutter condition: 881 (263 + 216 + 402).

DETECTOR: YOLOv8 or equivalent single-stage detector trained for 50 epochs.
METRICS: mAP50 (primary), mAP50-95 (strict localization), precision, recall.

3. BEST PERFORMING SCHEME -- OVERALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

NO-CLUTTER: gradient wins clearly.
  gradient mAP50 = 0.8710 vs. twotone 0.8372 vs. intensity 0.8099 vs. mono 0.7364.
  The gradient scheme outperforms the second-best (twotone) by 3.38 percentage
  points in mAP50 and only 0.25 percentage points in mAP50-95 (0.6340 vs.
  0.6315). Against mono (the simplest scheme), gradient gains +13.46 mAP50
  points -- a 18.3% relative improvement.

CLUTTER: intensity is the narrow winner.
  intensity mAP50 = 0.6703 vs. gradient 0.6668 vs. twotone 0.6567 vs. mono 0.5760.
  The margin between intensity and gradient is only 0.35 mAP50 points (< 1%
  relative difference), making them effectively tied for first under clutter.
  Both outperform mono by roughly 9 percentage points.

STRICT LOCALIZATION (mAP50-95):
  No-clutter: gradient (0.6340) > twotone (0.6315) > intensity (0.5498) > mono (0.4603).
  Clutter:    twotone (0.4824) > gradient (0.4620) > intensity (0.4446) > mono (0.3473).
  Under strict localization in clutter, twotone edges ahead of gradient, which
  is a reversal of the mAP50 ranking and indicates gradient boxes may be
  slightly less precisely localized under cluttered conditions.

5. STATIONARY VS. MOVING TARGET BREAKDOWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--- NO-CLUTTER ---

Class       gradient   twotone    intensity   mono
-----------  --------   --------   ---------   ------
Stationary   0.8379     0.8321     0.8182      0.7152
Moving       0.9041     0.8422     0.8017      0.7576

Recall (stationary):   0.868   0.872   0.875   0.775
Recall (moving):       0.936   0.897   0.880   0.891
Precision (stationary): 0.458  0.470   0.315   0.381
Precision (moving):     0.370  0.375   0.203   0.230

Observations:
- gradient achieves the highest moving mAP50 (0.9041) in no-clutter,
  representing a +6.19 point gap over twotone and a +10.24 point gap over mono.
  The temporal color encoding of gradient appears especially valuable for
  detecting objects with a discernible trail history.
- gradient moving recall of 0.936 is the highest across all conditions and
  classes, suggesting gradient minimizes missed detections on moving targets.
- Stationary target performance is more compressed: gradient (0.8379) only
  marginally exceeds twotone (0.8321) by 0.58 points. The color scheme matters
  less for stationary targets in clean conditions.
- Precision values are universally low (0.20 -- 0.47), implying significant
  false positive rates across all color schemes and conditions. This is likely
  a dataset-level characteristic rather than a scheme-specific phenomenon.
- intensity has the highest stationary recall (0.875) but lowest stationary
  precision (0.315), suggesting it fires on stationary targets aggressively
  at the cost of many false alarms.

--- CLUTTER ---

Class       gradient   twotone    intensity   mono
-----------  --------   --------   ---------   ------
Stationary   0.6807     0.6811     0.7386      0.6490
Moving       0.6529     0.6323     0.6020      0.5030

Recall (stationary):   0.744   0.788   0.841   0.741
Recall (moving):       0.875   0.828   0.798   0.791
Precision (stationary): 0.289  0.274   0.222   0.255
Precision (moving):     0.138  0.159   0.108   0.082

Observations:
- Under clutter, intensity reverses its rank ordering and becomes the BEST
  scheme for stationary targets (mAP50 = 0.7386), outperforming all others by
  5+ points. This is the single most counterintuitive finding in the experiment.
  Encoding radar return strength may help distinguish persistent stationary
  returns from transient clutter, since real stationary targets (buildings,
  parked vehicles) often produce strong, consistent echoes.
- Moving target performance in clutter drops sharply across all schemes. Mono
  is worst (0.5030); gradient is best (0.6529) with a +15.0 point margin over
  mono. The temporal color cue of gradient thus remains the most useful encoding
  for tracking moving targets even when clutter is present.
- The clutter condition inverts the stationary vs. moving advantage seen in
  no-clutter. In no-clutter, moving > stationary for gradient/twotone/intensity.
  In clutter, stationary > moving for all four schemes. Moving targets become
  harder to detect than stationary in cluttered environments, likely because
  trail features are contaminated by ground returns.
- Precision for moving targets in clutter degrades to the range 0.08 -- 0.16
  across all schemes, indicating extreme false positive rates. The clutter
  environment makes moving-class predictions highly unreliable regardless of
  color encoding.

KEY INVERSION: gradient is best for moving targets in clutter (0.6529),
while intensity is best for stationary targets in clutter (0.7386). No single
scheme dominates all classes under clutter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Condition            Scheme        Peak mAP50   Epoch of Peak
-------------------  -----------   ----------   -------------
noclutter            gradient      0.9179       30
noclutter            twotone       0.9231       29

clutter              gradient      0.7219       37
clutter              intensity     0.7341       38
clutter              mono          0.6092       43
clutter              twotone       0.6992       42

Key observations from training dynamics:

1. Convergence speed. In no-clutter, gradient and twotone peak by epoch 29-30,
   indicating rapid early learning. Mono requires until epoch 44 to peak, a
   roughly 50% longer training duration, suggesting the sparse visual signal
   of a single color channel is harder for the model to learn from quickly.
   At epoch 10, gradient noclutter mAP50 = 0.8458 vs. mono noclutter = 0.7015,
   already a 14-point gap at less than one-quarter of training.

2. Post-peak degradation. Training peak values are substantially higher than
   final (epoch 50) values for no-clutter runs:
   - noclutter_gradient:  peak 0.9179 vs. final epoch 50: 0.9003 (delta -1.76)
   - noclutter_twotone:   peak 0.9231 vs. final epoch 50: 0.8911 (delta -3.20)
   This overfitting tail is most severe for twotone (which actually peaks highest
   but ends lower), suggesting twotone is more prone to overfitting than gradient.
   The JSON evaluation numbers (mAP50 = 0.8710 gradient, 0.8372 twotone) likely
   represent best-weights checkpoint evaluations rather than the epoch-50 weights.

3. Late-stage stability (last 10 epochs std of validation mAP50):
   - noclutter_gradient:  std = 0.0078  (most stable no-clutter run)
   - noclutter_twotone:   std = 0.0072  (comparably stable)
   - noclutter_intensity: std = 0.0055  (most stable overall)
   - noclutter_mono:      std = 0.0094
   - clutter_intensity:   std = 0.0067  (most stable clutter run)
   - clutter_gradient:    std = 0.0115
   - clutter_mono:        std = 0.0152  (least stable)
   - clutter_twotone:     std = 0.0167  (least stable in clutter)
   Twotone shows the most volatile late-stage training in clutter, consistent
   with its lower mAP50-95 relative to its mAP50 -- the model is less
   confident in bounding box localization for twotone in clutter.

4. Validation loss at epoch 50:
   - Lowest val_box_loss: noclutter_twotone (0.8713), noclutter_gradient (0.9271)
   - Highest val_box_loss: clutter_mono (1.3916), clutter_intensity (1.1620)
   - Lowest val_cls_loss: noclutter_gradient (0.5819), noclutter_twotone (0.6248)
   - Highest val_cls_loss: clutter_mono (1.0490)
   Gradient and twotone consistently produce the lowest validation losses in
   no-clutter, correlating with their superior mAP50 outcomes.

7. CLUTTER DEGRADATION ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The performance gap between clutter and no-clutter conditions quantifies each
scheme's robustness to environmental interference.

Scheme      noclutter mAP50   clutter mAP50   Absolute Drop   Relative Drop
----------  ----------------  ---------------  --------------  -------------
gradient    0.8710            0.6668           0.2042          23.4%
twotone     0.8372            0.6567           0.1805          21.6%
intensity   0.8099            0.6703           0.1396          17.2%
mono        0.7364            0.5760           0.1604          21.8%

CRITICAL FINDING: intensity is the most clutter-robust scheme.

Although intensity ranks third in no-clutter mAP50, it exhibits the smallest
absolute degradation (0.1396 points, 17.2% relative drop) when clutter is
introduced. Gradient suffers the largest absolute degradation (0.2042 points)
and the second-largest relative drop (23.4%). The no-clutter advantage of
gradient is substantially eroded under clutter.

Stationary class clutter degradation:
  gradient:  0.8379 -> 0.6807 = -0.1572 (-18.8%)
  twotone:   0.8321 -> 0.6811 = -0.1510 (-18.1%)
  intensity: 0.8182 -> 0.7386 = -0.0796 ( -9.7%)  <-- most robust
  mono:      0.7152 -> 0.6490 = -0.0662 ( -9.3%)  <-- also robust

Moving class clutter degradation:
  gradient:  0.9041 -> 0.6529 = -0.2512 (-27.8%)
  twotone:   0.8422 -> 0.6323 = -0.2099 (-24.9%)
  intensity: 0.8017 -> 0.6020 = -0.1997 (-24.9%)
  mono:      0.7576 -> 0.5030 = -0.2546 (-33.6%)  <-- worst degradation

Moving targets are hurt more by clutter than stationary targets regardless
of color scheme. Gradient sustains moving-target detection better than any
other scheme (-27.8%) despite the absolute loss, while mono suffers the most
catastrophic moving-class degradation (-33.6%).

OVERALL VERDICT:
  - gradient is the best single scheme if clutter is not a primary concern
    (highest no-clutter performance: mAP50 = 0.8710, moving mAP50 = 0.9041).

    conditions, confirming that color encoding -- whatever its form -- provides
    meaningful discriminative value beyond grayscale intensity alone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. SURPRISING AND NOTEWORTHY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. INTENSITY WINS IN CLUTTER FOR STATIONARY TARGETS.
   The most counterintuitive result: intensity, which ranks 3rd in clean
   conditions, becomes the best scheme for stationary detection under clutter
   (mAP50 = 0.7386, vs. gradient 0.6807). This suggests radar return strength
   is a stable distinguishing feature for persistent targets (buildings, parked
   vehicles) amidst clutter, since real stationary targets produce strong,
   consistent, high-intensity returns that ground clutter does not replicate.

2. GRADIENT MAINTAINS MOVING-TARGET ADVANTAGE UNDER CLUTTER.
   Despite suffering the largest absolute mAP50 drop overall, gradient retains
   the top rank for moving targets even in clutter (0.6529). The temporal color
   encoding appears to carry enough information to discriminate genuine moving
   echoes from interference even when the scene is contaminated.

3. MEDIUM SPEED TIER IS THE CONSISTENT WEAK POINT.
   Across all eight conditions, medium-speed targets score lower than fast and
   are often lower than slow. This is unexpected: medium targets have more
   motion than slow but less than fast, so they should be intermediate. The
   pattern suggests medium-speed objects may produce trail signatures that are
   ambiguous -- too short to be clearly directional but too displacement-shifted
   to be confused with stationary returns. The dataset has the smallest count
   for medium (216 vs. 263 slow and 402 fast), which may compound this.

4. MONO'S MEDIUM-TIER ANOMALY.
   Despite being the worst scheme overall, mono is the unexpected leader on
   medium-speed targets in both clutter (0.2990) and no-clutter (0.3429)
   conditions. This is replicated consistently across both environmental
   conditions and cannot be a statistical fluke. One hypothesis: the single
   grayscale channel may reduce distraction from complex color patterns at
   medium speeds where trails are short and the color encoding provides
   insufficient context.

5. TWOTONE PEAKS HIGHEST BUT FINISHES LOWER THAN GRADIENT.
   During training, noclutter_twotone achieves the highest peak mAP50 of any
   run (0.9231 at epoch 29) -- higher than gradient's peak (0.9179 at epoch 30).
   Yet the final test-set evaluation gives gradient the win (0.8710 vs. 0.8372).
   This discrepancy indicates twotone overfits more severely after its peak and
   that gradient weights generalize better at the point of checkpoint selection.
   From a deployment standpoint, gradient is more reliable.

6. CLUTTER INVERTS THE STATIONARY/MOVING PERFORMANCE RELATIONSHIP.
   In no-clutter, all three multi-color schemes show moving mAP50 > stationary
   mAP50 (e.g., gradient: 0.9041 moving vs. 0.8379 stationary). In clutter,
   this relationship flips: all schemes show stationary mAP50 > moving mAP50
   (e.g., gradient: 0.6807 stationary vs. 0.6529 moving). This inversion
   reveals that clutter disproportionately degrades moving-target detection,
   perhaps because clutter echoes contaminate the motion history encoded in
   the trails.

7. PRECISION IS GLOBALLY LOW.
   Across all 16 class-condition-scheme combinations, precision does not exceed
   0.470 and in some cases falls as low as 0.082 (mono moving in clutter).
   This system-wide low precision (high false positive rate) is independent of
   color scheme choice and points to a dataset-level or annotation-level
   characteristic -- likely a large number of low-confidence predictions that
   the model generates for ambiguous radar returns. The color scheme choice
   primarily affects recall and mAP50, not precision.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. RECOMMENDATIONS FOR THE PAPER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Use gradient as the default/recommended color mapping scheme, citing its
   superior overall mAP50 (0.8710 no-clutter, 0.6668 clutter) and particularly
   strong moving-target performance (0.9041 no-clutter moving mAP50).

2. Report intensity as the recommended scheme for clutter-limited environments,
   supported by the stationary-class advantage (0.7386 in clutter) and the
   smallest degradation factor (17.2% relative drop vs. 23.4% for gradient).

3. The mono vs. multi-color comparison is the clearest ablation result: any
   form of color encoding (twotone, gradient, or intensity) outperforms
   grayscale mono by 9-13+ mAP50 points. This is the core evidence that color
   mapping is not cosmetic but functionally meaningful for the detector.

4. The medium-speed anomaly and mono's unexpected medium-tier performance are
   worth flagging as open questions for future work (the cause is not clear
   from these data alone).

5. The clutter degradation table (section 7) provides a clean comparison for
   a robustness discussion in the paper.
