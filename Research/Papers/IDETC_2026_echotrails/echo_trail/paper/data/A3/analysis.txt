A3 EXPERIMENT ANALYSIS: BINARY vs. PROPORTIONAL INTENSITY ENCODING
Experiment: EchoTrails Radar Echo Trail Paper
Date of Analysis: 2026-02-25
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. EXPERIMENT OVERVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Experiment A3 isolates the effect of intensity encoding strategy on
object detection performance within EchoTrails radar echo trail
visualizations. Two encoding strategies are compared:

  - Binary encoding: All echo trail pixels are rendered at full
    intensity regardless of radar reflectivity magnitude. Every
    historical position in the trail is drawn identically white (or
    maximum value), collapsing amplitude information into a presence/
    absence signal.

  - Proportional encoding: Echo trail pixel intensity is scaled to
    reflect the original radar reflectivity value at that location
    and time step. Stronger returns produce brighter pixels; weaker
    returns produce dimmer pixels. This preserves the amplitude
    gradient across the trail history.

Each encoding is evaluated under two environmental conditions:

  - No Clutter: Clean radar frames with minimal ground clutter and
    false returns. Target echoes dominate the scene.

  - Clutter: Frames containing realistic ground clutter, sea clutter,
    or weather contamination. Spurious returns compete with genuine
    targets.

This produces four experimental conditions: noclutter_binary,
noclutter_proportional, clutter_binary, and clutter_proportional.

Detection is performed using a YOLOv8-based detector trained and
evaluated separately on each condition. The detector classifies targets
into two categories: stationary and moving. Evaluation metrics include
mAP50, mAP50-95, precision, and recall, computed across per-class and
per-speed-tier breakdowns. Speed tiers partition the evaluation set into
slow (n=263 ground truth instances), medium (n=216), and fast (n=402)
targets, where speed refers to the apparent motion magnitude of the
tracked target across the trail window.

All four training runs used 50 epochs with identical hyperparameters and
dataset splits, differing only in the rendering parameter controlling
intensity encoding.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. OVERALL PERFORMANCE SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The four conditions ranked by overall mAP50 (evaluated on the held-out
test split, using the best model checkpoint):

  Condition                 mAP50    mAP50-95
  -------------------------------------------------
  noclutter_binary          0.8717   0.6594
  noclutter_proportional    0.8372   0.6315
  clutter_binary            0.6533   0.4756
  clutter_proportional      0.6567   0.4824

Key top-level findings:

  1. Binary encoding outperforms proportional encoding in clean
     conditions by a meaningful margin (+3.45 percentage points in
     mAP50, +2.79 pp in mAP50-95).

  2. Under clutter, the two encodings reach near-identical overall
     performance. Proportional encoding holds a marginal advantage of
     +0.34 pp in mAP50 and +0.68 pp in mAP50-95 - differences that
     are within the noise of a single training run and should not be
     interpreted as a reliable superiority.

  3. Clutter imposes a larger absolute penalty on binary encoding
     (-0.2184 mAP50 drop) than on proportional encoding (-0.1805 mAP50
     drop). Binary encoding degrades more severely when spurious returns
     contaminate the scene.

These three findings together suggest: binary encoding capitalizes on
clean signal strength but is more brittle under interference.
Proportional encoding sacrifices some peak performance in clean scenes
in exchange for slightly more graceful degradation under clutter.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. TRAINING DYNAMICS (FROM CSV FILES)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Best checkpoint performance (peak validation mAP50 across 50 epochs):

  Condition                 Best Epoch   mAP50    mAP50-95   Prec    Recall
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  noclutter_binary          34           0.9219   0.6621     0.8699  0.8549
  noclutter_proportional    29           0.9231   0.6501     0.8715  0.8618
  clutter_binary            38           0.7046   0.4815     0.6396  0.7160
  clutter_proportional      42           0.6992   0.4867     0.6657  0.7074

Noteworthy observations from training dynamics:

  - In the no-clutter setting, both encodings achieve nearly identical
    peak mAP50 at their respective best checkpoints (0.9219 binary vs.
    0.9231 proportional). The difference at peak is negligible (0.0012).
    This means that the divergence in final evaluation performance
    (0.8717 vs. 0.8372) is driven primarily by differences in
    convergence stability and model selection rather than a fundamental
    capacity difference between encodings.

  - The noclutter_binary model peaks later (epoch 34) than
    noclutter_proportional (epoch 29) and maintains a higher mAP50
    through epochs 30-50. This suggests binary encoding reaches its
    optimum more slowly but holds it more stably.

  - Under clutter, binary reaches its best checkpoint at epoch 38
    while proportional requires epoch 42 to converge. Proportional
    encoding appears to converge more slowly when clutter is present,
    possibly because the model must learn to weight amplitude cues
    relative to spurious high-intensity clutter returns.

  - Final epoch mAP50 values (epoch 50) are:
      noclutter_binary:       0.9158 (final val cls_loss: 0.564)
      noclutter_proportional: 0.8911 (final val cls_loss: 0.625)
      clutter_binary:         0.6894 (final val cls_loss: 0.916)
      clutter_proportional:   0.6789 (final val cls_loss: 0.932)

    The classification loss at convergence is consistently higher for
    proportional encoding under both conditions, suggesting the network
    finds binary trail rendering slightly easier to classify.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. PER-CLASS ANALYSIS: STATIONARY vs. MOVING TARGETS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 No-Clutter Condition

  Class         Binary                  Proportional
                mAP50  P      R         mAP50  P      R
  -------------------------------------------------------
  Stationary    0.8527 0.3901 0.8893    0.8321 0.4702 0.8720
  Moving        0.8907 0.3675 0.9149    0.8422 0.3749 0.8967

  Binary encoding leads proportional in mAP50 for both classes:
    - Stationary advantage: +0.0206 mAP50
    - Moving advantage: +0.0485 mAP50

  The moving-class advantage for binary is substantially larger than
  the stationary-class advantage. This is mechanistically plausible:
  moving targets produce characteristic trail patterns where binary
  encoding creates a clean, high-contrast streak. Proportional encoding
  partially blurs this contrast by dimming echoes that are reflected
  at lower intensity (e.g., early-history positions as the target moves
  away from its previous location), which may reduce the discriminative
  sharpness of the trail pattern.

  Precision is notably higher for proportional encoding on stationary
  targets (0.4702 vs. 0.3901, a +0.0801 advantage). This suggests
  proportional encoding generates fewer false positive detections on
  stationary features when clutter is absent. The amplitude gradient
  may help distinguish genuine strong stationary returns from image
  artifacts. However, this precision gain does not translate into
  higher mAP50 because recall is also slightly lower.

4.2 Clutter Condition

  Class         Binary                  Proportional
                mAP50  P      R         mAP50  P      R
  -------------------------------------------------------
  Stationary    0.6993 0.2387 0.8000    0.6811 0.2739 0.7880
  Moving        0.6073 0.1266 0.8116    0.6323 0.1594 0.8275

  Under clutter, the class-level picture flips for moving targets.
  Proportional encoding now outperforms binary on the moving class
  (+0.0250 mAP50). The stationary class retains a small binary
  advantage (+0.0182 mAP50), but both encodings suffer substantially
  relative to the no-clutter baseline.

  The proportional encoding's relative advantage on moving targets
  under clutter is consistent with the hypothesis that amplitude
  information becomes a useful discriminative cue when the background
  is contaminated. Genuine moving targets typically produce a coherent
  amplitude-modulated trail signature, whereas clutter returns are
  spatially diffuse and temporally incoherent. Binary encoding erases
  this amplitude distinction, while proportional encoding preserves it.

  Precision collapses severely for both encodings under clutter, but
  proportional encoding retains somewhat higher precision for both
  classes:
    - Stationary: proportional 0.2739 vs. binary 0.2387 (+0.0352)
    - Moving: proportional 0.1594 vs. binary 0.1266 (+0.0328)

  Binary encoding's recall advantage on stationary targets under clutter
  (0.8000 vs. 0.7880) is offset by its much lower precision, indicating
  that binary encoding under clutter produces more false positives on
  clutter features that happen to resemble target-like bright patches.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. SPEED TIER ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Ground truth counts: slow=263, medium=216, fast=402. The fast tier is
the most populous, accounting for 46% of all evaluation instances.

5.1 No-Clutter

  Speed Tier    Binary              Proportional        Delta (B-P)
                mAP50  mAP50-95    mAP50  mAP50-95    mAP50
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  Slow          0.3777 0.2886      0.3365 0.2552       +0.0412
  Medium        0.2861 0.2185      0.2862 0.2198       -0.0001
  Fast          0.6232 0.4842      0.6120 0.4755       +0.0112

  Performance on slow and fast tiers strongly favors binary encoding,
  with the largest advantage on slow targets (+0.0412 mAP50). Medium-
  speed targets are essentially tied (delta = -0.0001).

  Absolute performance on all speed tiers is markedly lower than the
  overall per-class mAP50 values. This is expected because speed-tier
  mAP is computed on a subset partition and the overall mAP includes
  targets from all tiers jointly. However, the internal ranking is
  illuminating: fast targets are easiest to detect (mAP50 ~ 0.62),
  slow targets are moderately difficult (mAP50 ~ 0.34-0.38), and
  medium-speed targets are hardest (mAP50 ~ 0.29). This non-monotonic
  speed-difficulty relationship is notable and may reflect that medium-
  speed targets produce trails that are ambiguous - too short to be
  clearly "fast" but too long to be treated as stationary. Both
  encodings struggle equally here.

  The slow-tier advantage for binary encoding (+0.0412) is the largest
  per-tier delta observed under no-clutter conditions. Slow targets
  accumulate minimal displacement across the trail window, meaning the
  trail is a compact cluster rather than an elongated streak. Binary
  encoding renders this cluster at maximum intensity, making it a
  distinct bright region against the dark background. Proportional
  encoding may inadvertently dim portions of this cluster if some echo
  returns are weak, reducing the detection confidence.

5.2 Clutter

  Speed Tier    Binary              Proportional        Delta (B-P)
                mAP50  mAP50-95    mAP50  mAP50-95    mAP50
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  Slow          0.1941 0.1270      0.1957 0.1331       -0.0016
  Medium        0.2471 0.1860      0.2360 0.1752       +0.0111
  Fast          0.5069 0.3782      0.5227 0.4060       -0.0158

  Under clutter, the speed tier rankings change notably. Fast targets
  now favor proportional encoding (+0.0158 advantage for proportional).
  Medium targets favor binary (+0.0111). Slow targets are a statistical
  tie (-0.0016 favoring proportional).

  The proportional advantage on fast targets under clutter is the
  largest directional shift across any speed/encoding/environment
  combination. Fast-moving targets produce elongated trails spanning
  many pixels. In a cluttered scene, binary encoding renders both
  genuine trail pixels and clutter pixels at uniform full intensity,
  making it harder to distinguish target streak patterns from clutter
  streaks (e.g., wind-blown precipitation bands). Proportional encoding
  allows genuine reflectivity gradients along the trail to serve as a
  consistency cue that clutter typically lacks.

  The mAP50-95 delta for fast targets under clutter is also the largest
  in the dataset: proportional achieves 0.4060 vs. binary's 0.3782
  (+0.0278). This indicates that not only does proportional encoding
  detect more fast targets under clutter, but it also localizes them
  more precisely (as mAP50-95 penalizes poor bounding box quality).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CLUTTER SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The clutter penalty (no-clutter mAP50 minus clutter mAP50) quantifies
how much each encoding degrades when environmental noise is added:

  Encoding        No-Clutter   Clutter    Penalty
  -----------------------------------------------
  Binary          0.8717       0.6533     -0.2184
  Proportional    0.8372       0.6567     -0.1805

  Binary encoding suffers a 21.8 percentage point drop under clutter,
  versus 18.1 percentage points for proportional encoding. This
  difference of 3.8 pp represents the additional robustness conferred
  by amplitude information in contaminated conditions.

  For stationary targets specifically:
    Binary:       0.8527 -> 0.6993, penalty = 0.1534
    Proportional: 0.8321 -> 0.6811, penalty = 0.1510

  For moving targets specifically:
    Binary:       0.8907 -> 0.6073, penalty = 0.2834
    Proportional: 0.8422 -> 0.6323, penalty = 0.2099

  Moving targets bear the brunt of clutter degradation for both
  encodings, but binary encoding loses an additional 0.0735 mAP50
  on moving targets compared to proportional encoding's clutter penalty.
  This is consistent with the clutter-contamination hypothesis: moving
  target trails are particularly vulnerable when binary encoding
  conflates genuine motion signatures with clutter streaks of similar
  spatial extent.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. NOTABLE AND SURPRISING FINDINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 The Medium-Speed Tier Is Consistently Hardest to Detect

  Across all four conditions, medium-speed targets (n=216) achieve
  the lowest mAP50 of any speed tier under no-clutter conditions
  (binary: 0.2861, proportional: 0.2862). Slow targets slightly
  outperform medium targets in the no-clutter case despite having
  slightly fewer instances (263 vs. 216). This suggests a genuine
  difficulty discontinuity at intermediate motion scales that is
  independent of encoding strategy. Both encodings are equally
  blind to whatever feature distinguishes medium-speed targets from
  slow or fast targets at inference time.

7.2 Peak Training Performance Is Nearly Indistinguishable

  At their respective best checkpoints, noclutter_binary achieves
  mAP50=0.9219 and noclutter_proportional achieves mAP50=0.9231.
  The gap is only 0.0012 in favor of proportional. This near-
  equivalence at peak implies that both encodings carry sufficient
  signal for high-performance detection; the observed gap in final
  evaluated performance (0.8717 vs. 0.8372) is largely an artifact
  of convergence stability and checkpoint selection rather than
  a difference in representational richness.

7.3 Proportional Encoding Converges Later Under Clutter

  The clutter_proportional model requires epoch 42 to reach its best
  validation mAP50, versus epoch 38 for clutter_binary. This 4-epoch
  difference suggests the optimizer must explore a more complex loss
  landscape when amplitude gradients are present alongside clutter
  interference. The model needs more iterations to learn which amplitude
  patterns are signal and which are noise.

7.4 Precision Is Uniformly Low Across All Conditions

  All four conditions show precision values in the range 0.12-0.47,
  which is conspicuously low relative to mAP50. This is consistent with
  a high-recall detection setting where the confidence threshold is
  tuned permissively, or where the dataset contains many ambiguous
  instances that generate multiple overlapping predictions. The
  recall values (0.78-0.91) confirm this interpretation. Researchers
  using these results for downstream system design should account for
  the high false positive rate implicit in these precision numbers
  and consider whether the application permits a recall-biased operating
  point.

7.5 The Binary Encoding's Clutter Fragility Has Structural Causes

  Binary encoding renders clutter returns at the same full intensity as
  genuine target returns. Under no-clutter conditions this is optimal
  because the only bright regions are targets and their trails. Under
  clutter, this property becomes a liability: the encoding loses the
  one feature (amplitude) that could distinguish a 60 dBZ target return
  from a 15 dBZ clutter return. Proportional encoding preserves this
  discriminative gradient, explaining why proportional's clutter penalty
  is smaller despite its slightly lower baseline.

9. CONCLUSIONS FOR PAPER
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Binary intensity encoding produces superior detection performance in
clean radar environments, leading proportional encoding by 3.45
percentage points in overall mAP50 (0.8717 vs. 0.8372). This advantage
is concentrated on moving targets (+4.85 pp) and slow-moving targets
(+4.12 pp in the speed-tier analysis). The mechanism is consistent:
binary encoding maximizes pixel intensity for all trail members,
producing high-contrast signatures that are easy for the detector to
localize.

Proportional intensity encoding offers superior clutter robustness.
Under contaminated conditions it achieves slightly higher overall mAP50
(0.6567 vs. 0.6533), a smaller clutter-induced performance penalty
(18.1 pp vs. 21.8 pp drop), better precision across both target classes,
and a meaningful advantage on fast-moving targets (+1.58 pp mAP50,
+2.78 pp mAP50-95). These gains derive from the amplitude gradient
retained in proportional encoding, which provides an implicit
discriminative cue that binary encoding discards.

For operational radar systems where ground clutter suppression is
imperfect or unavailable, proportional encoding is the preferred
choice. In controlled or filtered environments, binary encoding offers
a measurable detection advantage. System designers should select the
encoding based on expected operational clutter severity rather than
treating intensity encoding as a cosmetic parameter.
