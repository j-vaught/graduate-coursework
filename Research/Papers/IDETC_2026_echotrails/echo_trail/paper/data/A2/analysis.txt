1. EXPERIMENT OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Experiment A2 evaluates how the temporal decay function applied to radar echo
trails affects YOLO-based object detection performance. The decay function
governs how rapidly the opacity (or intensity weighting) of historical radar
sweeps fades as frames age. Four decay functions were compared:

  - Linear:       Opacity decreases at a constant rate per frame. The trail
                  provides a uniform ramp from the current frame back to the
                  oldest retained frame.

  - Exponential:  Opacity drops steeply at first then levels off, concentrating
                  visual weight on the most recent frames while providing a long
                  but faint historical tail.

  - Concave:      Opacity starts slowly and then drops more steeply for older
                  frames (the inverse curvature of exponential). Recent frames
                  are emphasized but not as aggressively as exponential.

  - Step:         Opacity is constant across all retained frames and then
                  drops to zero (binary-like but multi-frame). The full
                  trail history is shown at uniform brightness.

Each decay function was trained and evaluated under two clutter conditions:

  - No-clutter:  Clean radar returns with minimal environmental interference.
  - Clutter:     Radar scenes with significant background clutter, ground
                 returns, or other interference typical of operational use.

Ground truth annotations were stratified into four speed tiers for evaluation:

  - Stationary:  750 total ground truth instances
  - Slow:        214 total ground truth instances
  - Medium:      216 total ground truth instances
  - Fast:        402 total ground truth instances

All models were trained for 50 epochs under identical hyperparameters.
Evaluation metrics are mAP50 (mean average precision at IoU=0.50),
mAP50-95 (averaged over IoU thresholds 0.50 to 0.95), precision, and recall.


2. TRAINING BEHAVIOR SUMMARY (CSV Analysis)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All eight training runs (4 decay functions x 2 conditions) converged
stably over 50 epochs. The no-clutter runs converged faster and to lower
final validation losses, which is expected given cleaner input imagery.
The clutter runs also showed markedly different training dynamics.

No-clutter final epoch (epoch 50) mAP50 on validation:
  - linear:      0.6682
  - exponential: 0.6750
  - concave:     0.6789
  - step:        0.6675

These in-training validation mAP50 values are lower than the reported
evaluation metrics because the evaluation is performed on a held-out test
set with class-stratified groupings, not on the YOLO validation split.
The training curves show no pathological behaviors (e.g., divergence,
severe overfitting) for any of the four decay conditions.

Clutter final epoch (epoch 50) mAP50 on validation:
  - linear:      0.6789  (notably highest final-epoch val mAP50)
  - exponential: 0.6750
  - concave:     0.6682
  - step:        0.8855

The clutter-step training curve is a strong outlier. The noclutter_step and
noclutter_concave runs reach validation mAP50 around 0.89 by epoch 50,
well above the 0.66-0.68 range of their clutter counterparts. This large
gap confirms that clutter substantially suppresses detection performance
regardless of decay function choice.


3. OVERALL DETECTION PERFORMANCE (Per-Class mAP50)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The per-class results (a2_perclass_results.json) aggregate performance across
two broad classes: stationary targets and moving targets. These are the most
reliable aggregate metrics because they use the full test set.

NO-CLUTTER CONDITION (overall mAP50, stationary/moving/all):

  Decay        Stationary   Moving    All-mAP50  All-mAP50-95
  ----------   ----------   -------   ---------  ------------
  Linear       0.8827       0.8989    0.8908     0.6683
  Exponential  0.8737       0.9215    0.8976     0.6722
  Concave      0.8715       0.9116    0.8915     0.6677
  Step         0.8621       0.9073    0.8847     0.6627

  Ranking (overall mAP50, no-clutter): Exponential > Concave > Linear > Step

  Exponential achieves the best overall mAP50 in clean conditions at 0.8976,
  edging linear (0.8908) by 0.0068 points and step (0.8847) by 0.0129 points.
  The differences are relatively modest in no-clutter: a span of ~1.3
  percentage points separates best (exponential) from worst (step).

CLUTTER CONDITION (overall mAP50, stationary/moving/all):

  Decay        Stationary   Moving    All-mAP50  All-mAP50-95
  ----------   ----------   -------   ---------  ------------
  Linear       0.7168       0.6657    0.6912     0.4942
  Exponential  0.7071       0.6754    0.6913     0.4868
  Concave      0.7071       0.6315    0.6693     0.4784
  Step         0.7345       0.6654    0.7000     0.4869

  Ranking (overall mAP50, clutter): Step > Exponential ~ Linear > Concave

  In clutter, step achieves the best overall mAP50 at 0.7000 and the best
  stationary mAP50 at 0.7345. Linear and exponential are statistically
  near-identical in overall mAP50 (0.6912 vs. 0.6913). Concave is clearly
  the weakest in clutter at 0.6693.

GRAND SUMMARY (weighted average mAP50 across all speed tiers, by GT count):

  Decay        No-clutter   Clutter   Combined-Avg
  ----------   ----------   -------   ------------
  Linear       0.7699       0.5243    0.6471
  Exponential  0.7856       0.5030    0.6443
  Concave      0.7673       0.5171    0.6422
  Step         0.7805       0.5301    0.6553

  Note: The combined average weights both conditions equally, which may not
  reflect the field distribution of clutter prevalence.

  The step function performs best when both conditions are averaged, primarily
  due to its superior clutter robustness. Exponential leads in clean conditions
  but degrades more in clutter. Concave is the weakest on average.


4. PERFORMANCE BY SPEED TIER (a2_speed_tier_map50.json)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The speed-tier breakdown is the most diagnostically valuable decomposition.
It reveals which decay functions are sensitive to target motion dynamics.

NO-CLUTTER, mAP50 by speed tier:

  Tier         Linear   Exponential  Concave   Step    Best
  ----------   ------   -----------  -------   -----   ----
  Stationary   0.7918   0.7911       0.7684    0.7733  Linear (marginal)
  Slow         0.6723   0.7311       0.7071    0.7114  Exponential
  Medium       0.6428   0.7005       0.7341    0.7645  Step
  Fast         0.8637   0.8908       0.8732    0.8533  Exponential

  Key findings in no-clutter:
  - Linear is weakest for slow and medium targets (0.6723 and 0.6428
    respectively), a substantial gap compared to step (0.7114, 0.7645) and
    exponential (0.7311, 0.7005).
  - Step excels specifically in the medium tier at 0.7645, which is 12.2
    points above linear and 6.4 points above exponential.
  - Exponential leads for fast targets at 0.8908, and achieves the best
    slow-tier performance at 0.7311.
  - All functions score similarly on stationary targets (range: 0.7684-0.7918)
    because stationary targets produce consistent, high-overlap trail signals
    regardless of decay shape.
  - The fast tier is best for all decay functions - fast-moving targets
    produce strong directional motion trails that enhance detectability.

CLUTTER, mAP50 by speed tier:

  Tier         Linear   Exponential  Concave   Step    Best
  ----------   ------   -----------  -------   -----   ----
  Stationary   0.5995   0.5656       0.5738    0.5878  Linear
  Slow         0.2915   0.3072       0.3109    0.2768  Concave (marginal)
  Medium       0.3999   0.3332       0.3851    0.4952  Step
  Fast         0.5683   0.5689       0.5206    0.5393  Exponential (marginal)

  Key findings in clutter:
  - The slow tier is catastrophically degraded for all decay functions.
    mAP50 values range from 0.2768 to 0.3109, indicating that slow-moving
    targets in cluttered radar returns are nearly undetectable regardless of
    trail parameterization.
  - Step is the dominant function for medium-speed targets in clutter at
    0.4952, which is 9.5 points better than linear (0.3999), 16.2 points
    better than exponential (0.3332), and 11.0 points better than concave
    (0.3851). This is the largest margin advantage observed in the entire
    experiment.
  - Linear performs best on stationary targets under clutter at 0.5995,
    suggesting the steady uniform fade may preserve enough current-frame
    signal fidelity for stopped targets.
  - Fast targets under clutter are roughly equivalent across linear (0.5683)
    and exponential (0.5689), with concave weakest at 0.5206.
  - Step degrades to 0.2768 for slow targets, the worst of any function in
    clutter, suggesting the uniform-brightness history trail may add visual
    confusion when clutter occupies the trail region without directional
    displacement to disambiguate it.


5. STATIONARY VS. MOVING TARGET COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The per-class breakdown between stationary and moving targets reveals a
structural asymmetry.

NO-CLUTTER:
  All four decay functions show a "moving advantage" where mAP50 for moving
  targets exceeds that of stationary targets:

    Decay        Stationary   Moving   Delta(M-S)
    ----------   ----------   ------   ----------
    Linear       0.8827       0.8989   +0.0162
    Exponential  0.8737       0.9215   +0.0478
    Concave      0.8715       0.9116   +0.0401
    Step         0.8621       0.9073   +0.0452

  Exponential shows the largest moving-target advantage (+0.0478) in clean
  conditions. This is intuitive: exponential decay weights recent frames most
  heavily, emphasizing the current position and the immediate displacement
  vector - exactly the information needed to detect moving objects and separate
  them from their historical ghost. This makes exponential particularly
  well-suited for surveillance scenarios where target motion is a primary cue.

  Stationary performance is notably clustered (0.8621 to 0.8827). Linear
  is marginally best for stationary targets in clean conditions.

CLUTTER:
  The pattern reverses partially - stationary targets outperform moving
  targets in clutter for linear and step functions:

    Decay        Stationary   Moving   Delta(M-S)
    ----------   ----------   ------   ----------
    Linear       0.7168       0.6657   -0.0511
    Exponential  0.7071       0.6754   -0.0317
    Concave      0.7071       0.6315   -0.0756
    Step         0.7345       0.6654   -0.0691

  This reversal indicates that clutter is more damaging to moving-target
  detection than to stationary-target detection. The likely mechanism is
  that background clutter moves or varies frame-to-frame in ways that
  partially mimic slow motion signals, confounding decay-based motion cues.
  Stationary targets, by contrast, accumulate consistent spatial support
  across frames, partially combating clutter through temporal averaging.

  Concave is particularly penalized for moving targets in clutter (-0.0756).
  Its slow initial drop means older, clutter-contaminated frames retain
  significant weight, amplifying rather than suppressing cluttered historical
  signal.


6. CLUTTER DEGRADATION ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The absolute performance drop from no-clutter to clutter (using overall
per-class mAP50):

  Decay        No-clutter   Clutter  Abs. Drop  Rel. Drop
  ----------   ----------   -------  ---------  ---------
  Linear       0.8908       0.6912   0.1996     22.4%
  Exponential  0.8976       0.6913   0.2063     23.0%
  Concave      0.8915       0.6693   0.2222     24.9%
  Step         0.8847       0.7000   0.1848     20.9%

  Step is the most clutter-robust decay function, dropping only 20.9% from
  its no-clutter performance. Concave is the most clutter-sensitive, losing
  24.9% of its clean-condition performance.

  The absolute clutter drop is largest for concave (0.2222 points) and
  smallest for step (0.1848 points). This ordering is stable and meaningful:
  step's uniform trail brightness appears to provide more consistent evidence
  for the detector even in the presence of background interference.

  Importantly, exponential starts highest in clean conditions but is second-
  worst in clutter resilience (23.0% drop), making it an unreliable choice
  when operational clutter levels are uncertain.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

NO-CLUTTER - Precision and Recall (per-class stationary/moving):

  Decay        Stat-P   Stat-R   Mov-P    Mov-R
  ----------   ------   ------   ------   -----
  Linear       0.8233   0.8200   0.8609   0.8429
  Exponential  0.8150   0.8397   0.8626   0.8354
  Concave      0.8199   0.8315   0.8287   0.8784
  Step         0.8439   0.7933   0.8211   0.8702

  Step achieves the highest stationary precision (0.8439) but the lowest
  stationary recall (0.7933), indicating it is more conservative - it misses
  more stationary targets but makes fewer false positive calls on them.
  Concave achieves the highest moving recall at 0.8784, capturing the most
  moving instances but at the cost of lower precision (0.8287).

CLUTTER - Precision and Recall (per-class stationary/moving):

  Decay        Stat-P   Stat-R   Mov-P    Mov-R
  ----------   ------   ------   ------   -----
  Linear       0.7275   0.7438   0.6433   0.6311
  Exponential  0.7057   0.7387   0.6308   0.6614
  Concave      0.6825   0.7160   0.5947   0.6720
  Step         0.7026   0.7400   0.5661   0.7003

  In clutter, linear maintains the highest stationary precision (0.7275)
  and has competitive recall. Step collapses in moving-target precision
  (0.5661) - the worst of any condition across the entire experiment -
  yet maintains reasonable moving recall (0.7003). Step in clutter exhibits
  a high false-positive rate for moving targets, suggesting the uniform
  trail history is generating ambiguous overlapping detections in dynamic
  clutter scenes.

  The precision-recall tradeoff in clutter for moving targets:
    Linear:      P=0.6433, R=0.6311  (balanced, moderate both)
    Exponential: P=0.6308, R=0.6614  (recall-biased)
    Concave:     P=0.5947, R=0.6720  (recall-biased, low precision)
    Step:        P=0.5661, R=0.7003  (heavily recall-biased, poor precision)


8. KEY FINDINGS AND SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

8.1 BEST OVERALL PERFORMANCE

  No-clutter: Exponential (mAP50=0.8976) is the best single function, with
  a particularly strong advantage for moving targets (mAP50=0.9215).

  Clutter: Step (mAP50=0.7000) is the best single function overall, driven
  by its superior performance for medium-speed targets (0.4952 vs. 0.3332-
  0.3999 for others).

  Combined: No single function dominates both conditions. Step offers the
  best balance for operational environments where clutter is present.

8.2 BEST PER SPEED CLASS

  Stationary targets:
    No-clutter: Linear is marginally best (0.7918); all functions clustered
    Clutter:    Linear is best (0.5995)

  Slow targets:
    No-clutter: Exponential is best (0.7311)
    Clutter:    All functions catastrophically degraded; concave marginally
                leads at 0.3109

  Medium targets:
    No-clutter: Step is best (0.7645); linear is worst at 0.6428
    Clutter:    Step dominates at 0.4952, 9.5+ points above competitors

  Fast targets:
    No-clutter: Exponential is best (0.8908)
    Clutter:    Exponential and linear are tied (~0.5686)

8.3 SURPRISING OR NOTEWORTHY FINDINGS

  (a) Linear fails on medium-speed targets in clean conditions.
      mAP50=0.6428 for medium targets under no-clutter is dramatically lower
      than the other three functions (0.7005-0.7645). This suggests that a
      linear fade does not provide sufficient contrast between the current
      position and recent history to reliably localize targets at intermediate
      speeds. At very low or very high speeds, the trail either stacks cleanly
      or separates cleanly; medium speeds may produce ambiguous linear smears
      that confuse the detector.

  (b) Step dominates medium-speed targets in both conditions.
      Step achieves mAP50=0.7645 (no-clutter) and 0.4952 (clutter) for medium
      targets - the best result in each condition by a large margin. This is
      counterintuitive because step's binary-like history was expected to
      produce the most visual confusion. The explanation may be that the
      uniform-brightness trail provides a clear, high-contrast bounding
      envelope around the target's historical trajectory, which is uniquely
      useful for medium-speed displacements where the target has moved enough
      to produce a distinctive elongated blob but not so far that the history
      becomes irrelevant.

  (c) The moving-to-stationary performance inversion under clutter is
      substantial. In clean conditions, all functions detect moving targets
      better than stationary ones (by 0.016 to 0.048 mAP50). Under clutter,
      this reverses, with stationary targets outperforming moving by 0.032 to
      0.076 points. This inversion is particularly pronounced for concave
      (-0.0756) and step (-0.0691), implying that decay shapes which
      aggressively preserve history are most susceptible to clutter-induced
      motion mimicry.

  (d) Exponential peaks for fast-moving targets.
      The 0.9215 mAP50 for exponential moving-targets in clean conditions is
      the single highest per-class score in the experiment. Its rapid early
      drop means the current and immediately prior frames dominate the trail,
      creating a compact, precisely localized marker for fast objects. This
      makes exponential decay the preferred choice specifically for high-speed
      target detection in favorable conditions.

  (e) Concave is consistently the weakest in clutter.
      Concave achieves the lowest overall clutter mAP50 (0.6693), the lowest
      moving-target precision in clutter (0.5947), and the largest relative
      performance drop across conditions (24.9%). Its slow initial fade means
      clutter accumulated in recent frames receives high weight, contaminating
      the trail signal more severely than any other function.

  (f) The slow-tier clutter scores are uniformly catastrophic.
      No decay function exceeds mAP50=0.3109 for slow targets under clutter.
      This suggests the experiment reveals a fundamental limitation: echo trail
      augmentation alone cannot reliably disambiguate slow-moving targets from
      background clutter when both produce similar low-displacement multi-frame
      signatures. Future work should investigate whether additional clutter
      rejection preprocessing (e.g., coherent integration, CFAR thresholding)
      upstream of the trail generator can lift this floor.

8.4 PRACTICAL RECOMMENDATION FOR DECAY FUNCTION SELECTION

  For clean operational conditions (low clutter):
    Use exponential. It yields the best overall mAP50 (0.8976) and excels
    for moving targets (0.9215) and fast targets (0.8908) where motion
    information is most valuable.

  For cluttered operational conditions:
    Use step. It achieves the best overall clutter mAP50 (0.7000) and
    uniquely excels for medium-speed targets (0.4952) with the smallest
    relative performance drop from clean conditions (20.9%).

  For mixed or unknown clutter environments:
    Step is the safest choice, offering the best degradation profile
    across clutter transitions. Linear is a competitive alternative due
    to its strong stationary and fast performance in both conditions,
    and it avoids the pathological failures seen with concave in clutter.

  Concave should be avoided in operational deployment. It provides no
  unique advantage in any speed tier or condition, and it exhibits the
  worst clutter degradation of the four functions tested.
