\section*{Problem 1.6}

We are given that $A$ is invertible and the block matrix equation:
\[
\begin{bmatrix} A & A \\ B & A \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} A \\ C \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ I \end{bmatrix}
\]

Expanding the block matrix multiplication:

\textbf{First row:}
\[
A \cdot A + A \cdot C = 0
\]
\[
A^2 + AC = 0
\]

\textbf{Second row:}
\[
B \cdot A + A \cdot C = I
\]
\[
BA + AC = I
\]

\textbf{Solving for $C$:}

From the first row equation:
\[
A^2 + AC = 0
\]
\[
AC = -A^2
\]

Since $A$ is invertible, we multiply both sides by $A^{-1}$ on the left:
\[
C = -A
\]

\textbf{Solving for $B$:}

Substituting $C = -A$ into the second row equation:
\[
BA + A(-A) = I
\]
\[
BA - A^2 = I
\]
\[
BA = I + A^2
\]

Multiplying both sides by $A^{-1}$ on the right:
\[
B = (I + A^2)A^{-1}
\]

Alternatively, this can be written as:
\[
B = A^{-1} + A
\]

\textbf{Verification:}

We can verify: $BA = (A^{-1} + A)A = A^{-1}A + A^2 = I + A^2$ \checkmark

\textbf{Final Answer:}
\[
\boxed{B = A^{-1} + A, \quad C = -A}
\]


\section*{Problem 1.7}

We need to show that the product of two symmetric matrices may not be symmetric. A matrix is symmetric if $M = M^T$.

\textbf{Counterexample:}

Let:
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
\]

\textbf{Verify that $A$ and $B$ are symmetric:}

\[
A^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = A \quad \checkmark
\]

\[
B^T = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = B \quad \checkmark
\]

\textbf{Compute $AB$:}

\[
AB = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
\]

\textbf{Compute $(AB)^T$:}

\[
(AB)^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
\]

\textbf{Compare:}

\[
AB = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \neq \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = (AB)^T
\]

Therefore, $AB \neq (AB)^T$, demonstrating that $AB$ is \textbf{not symmetric}.

\textbf{Conclusion:}

Although both $A$ and $B$ are symmetric matrices, their product $AB$ is not symmetric. This shows that the product of symmetric matrices is not necessarily symmetric. (Note: $AB$ would be symmetric if and only if $AB = BA$, i.e., if $A$ and $B$ commute.)
