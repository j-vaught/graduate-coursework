\section*{Problem Statement}

Assume that we decide to use a rectangular filter at the transmitter (instead of sinc or root-raised cosine function), i.e., 
\[
p_{tx}(t) = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{for } t \in \left[-\frac{T_{spacing}}{2}, \frac{T_{spacing}}{2}\right], \\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Is it possible to achieve an inter-symbol-interference (ISI)-free communication (i.e., \( \hat{d}_n = d_n \)) if the channel is \( \delta(t) \) (i.e., no multipath) and the receiver is based on the matched filter (i.e., \( p_{tx}(t) = p_{rx}(t) \)) and the receiver samples the output \( y(t) \) every \( T_{spacing} \) to obtain \( \hat{d}_n \)? Why? Why not?

\section*{Solution}

According to the Nyquist criterion for zero ISI, the composite pulse \( p_{composite}(t) \), which is the overall impulse response of the transmitter filter, channel, and receiver filter, must satisfy the following condition at the sampling instants:
\[
p_{composite}(nT_{spacing}) = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if } n = 0, \\
0 & \text{if } n \neq 0.
\end{cases}
\]

In the given system, the channel is an ideal delta function \( \delta(t) \), which means that the channel has no effect on the shape of the transmitted pulse \( p_{tx}(t) \). Thus, the overall impulse response is simply the convolution of \( p_{tx}(t) \) with itself, due to the matched filter at the receiver being identical to the transmitter filter. The convolution of a rectangular pulse with itself results in a triangular pulse with a base width of \( T_{spacing} \) and a peak at \( t = 0 \). Sampling this triangular pulse at \( T_{spacing} \) intervals would result in non-zero values at sampling points other than \( t = 0 \), which indicates the presence of ISI.

Therefore, it is not possible to achieve ISI-free communication with a rectangular pulse shape in this system, because the resulting triangular pulse does not satisfy the Nyquist criterion for zero ISI at the sampling instants.
