Evaluation uses real radar and maritime benchmarks that expose domain shift and adverse conditions, including PoLaRIS and LaRS, with auxiliary stress testing on radar-focused datasets such as RADIATE and CARRADA \cite{choi2024polaris,zust2023lars,sheeny2020radiate,ouaknine2020carrada,schumann2021radarscenes}. We use strict splits by route/location and leave-one-environment-out testing to prevent leakage.

We compare five settings: (1) fixed simulator defaults, (2) uniform random domain randomization, (3) Bayesian optimization tuning, (4) RL tuning without environment labels, and (5) RL tuning with environment labels (proposed).

Primary outcomes are real-data task performance and sim-to-real gap reduction. Secondary outcomes include cross-domain holdout performance, unseen-route robustness, run-to-run variance, and low-SNR/high-clutter stress behavior. We also report real-validation-call efficiency to quantify adaptation cost.

Each novelty claim is tied to a direct test: (a) sequential RL adaptation versus BayRn under matched real-evaluation budget \cite{muratore2020bayrn}; (b) environment-conditioned versus unconditioned RL; and (c) realism-constrained versus unconstrained action policies.

Published evidence already motivates this direction (Fig.~\ref{fig:published-evidence}). Real FMCW transfer can degrade toward chance-level behavior without adaptation, while calibrated adaptation improves substantially \cite{trinh2026digitaltwin}. In synthetic-to-measured SAR transfer, stronger structured adaptation outperforms synthetic-only and simple-noise baselines \cite{kim2024soft}. These patterns motivate feedback-driven simulator control instead of fixed randomization.
