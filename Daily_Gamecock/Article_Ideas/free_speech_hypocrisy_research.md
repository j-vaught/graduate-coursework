# Free Speech Hypocrisy on College Campuses: Both Sides

## Research Summary for Daily Gamecock Article

This document provides documented examples demonstrating that BOTH the political left and right engage in selective, hypocritical application of "free speech" principles on college campuses. The goal is to show that free speech absolutism is often a rhetorical weapon rather than a genuine principle for partisans on either side.

---

## FROM THE RIGHT: Conservative Free Speech Hypocrisy

### Example 1: Florida's DeSantis-Led Attack on Academic Freedom (2023-2024)

**The Hypocrisy:** Conservative groups demand platforms for right-wing speakers while supporting state laws that dictate what professors can teach and fire faculty for their viewpoints.

**Key Facts:**
- In May 2023, Governor Ron DeSantis signed a law banning DEI programs at Florida's public universities. He stated DEI stands for "discrimination, exclusion and indoctrination."
- The law bans general education courses that "distort significant historical events or include a curriculum that teaches identity politics" or critical race theory.
- **University of Florida fired 13 full-time DEI employees and ended 15 administrative appointments** in March 2024.
- The 2023 post-tenure review process resulted in **10 faculty members being terminated statewide** and 64 put on improvement plans.
- Professor Sharon Austin lost general-education status for courses titled "The Politics of Race" and "Black Horror and Social Justice."
- Over 80% of surveyed Florida professors said they would NOT recommend graduate students seek employment in the state.

**Sources:**

### Example 2: New College of Florida Takeover (2023-2024)

**The Hypocrisy:** While claiming to protect "viewpoint diversity," conservative activists eliminated entire academic programs and drove out faculty who held views they opposed.

**Key Facts:**
- On January 6, 2023, DeSantis appointed six new board members including Christopher Rufo, a conservative activist.
- The board fired the college president and installed Richard Corcoran (conservative former state House speaker).
- **The board denied tenure to ALL five faculty applicants** in April 2023 with no explanation.
- **36 professors (one-third of faculty) left** after the takeover; another 12 left in 2024.
- The gender studies program was abolished; the student gender and diversity center was destroyed with **books collected over 30 years thrown in dumpsters**.
- Interim President Corcoran fired the dean for diversity, equity, and inclusion.
- The school dropped **46 spots in U.S. News rankings** over two years.

**The Hypocrisy:** Texas Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick demanded free speech for conservative students while pushing legislation to fire professors for teaching certain ideas.

- **Senate Bill 17** (effective January 2024) banned DEI offices and policies at Texas public universities.
- **Senate Bill 18** (effective September 2023) prohibits tenure in higher education across Texas.
- UT Austin closed its multicultural center, canceled scholarships for undocumented students, and fired DEI-related employees.
- Texas A&M professor Wendy Moore, who taught CRT seminars for 18 years, said she is considering leaving higher education entirely.

## FROM THE LEFT: Progressive Free Speech Hypocrisy

### Example 1: Stanford Law School Shoutdown of Judge Kyle Duncan (March 2023)

**The Hypocrisy:** Students who demand protection for their protest rights shouted down a federal judge, preventing him from speaking, while a DEI dean joined in criticizing his presence.

**Key Facts:**
- On **March 9, 2023**, students persistently heckled Fifth Circuit Judge Kyle Duncan, preventing him from delivering remarks.
- Associate Dean for DEI **Tirien Steinbach** came to the podium with **prepared remarks** asking "Is the juice worth the squeeze?" - questioning whether free speech protections should allow speakers who cause "harm."
- Steinbach told Duncan: "For many people here, your work has caused harm."
- Stanford President and Law Dean sent **formal apology to Judge Duncan**.
- Steinbach was placed on leave and later resigned.
- All law students were required to attend **mandatory half-day training** on freedom of speech.
- **Impact:** Before the event, 45% of conservative students felt comfortable disagreeing with a professor; after, this dropped to just 6%.

**Sources:**
- [Stanford Daily: Judge Duncan's Stanford visit explained](https://stanforddaily.com/2023/04/05/judge-duncan-stanford-law-school-explained/)
- [FIRE: The Judge Duncan Shoutdown](https://www.thefire.org/research-learn/judge-duncan-shoutdown-what-stanford-students-think)

### Example 2: Middlebury College Charles Murray Attack (March 2017)

**The Hypocrisy:** Students violently attacked a professor who was merely moderating an event, demonstrating that "protection from harmful speech" can itself become violent speech suppression.

**Key Facts:**
- **March 2017:** Hundreds of students disrupted an event with political scientist Charles Murray (author of "The Bell Curve").
- Professor **Allison Stanger** agreed to moderate despite disagreeing with Murray, believing campus should allow debate.
- Students chanted and turned their backs; Murray was forced to livestream from another location.
- After the livestream, protesters **attacked Stanger and Murray**; Stanger was hospitalized with a **concussion** and wore a neck brace.
- Protesters surrounded their car, standing on the hood and throwing a traffic sign.
- Stanger later wrote: "They couldn't look me in the eye, because if they had, they would have seen another human being."
- A small group of **6-12 masked individuals dressed in black** appeared to lead the violence.

**Quote from Stanger:** "I feared for my life."

**Sources:**

### Example 3: Hamline University Fires Professor for Showing Islamic Art (2022-2023)

**The Hypocrisy:** A university fired a professor for showing historically significant artwork in an art history class after a single student complaint, labeling academic content "Islamophobic."

**Key Facts:**
- **October 6, 2022:** Art history professor **Erika López Prater** showed 14th-century and 16th-century paintings depicting Prophet Muhammad during an online lecture.
- She provided a **two-minute content warning** before showing the images.
- The artwork was created by **Muslim artists** (Persian scholar Rashid al-Din and Ottoman Turkish illustrator).
- One student, president of the Muslim Student Association, complained.
- The dean of students sent an email calling it "undeniably inconsiderate, disrespectful, and **Islamophobic**."
- Hamline declined to renew López Prater's contract.
- **Even the Muslim Public Affairs Council (MPAC)** called for her reinstatement.
- FIRE, PEN America, and the Academic Freedom Alliance condemned the firing.
- The university president **retired in April 2023** after faculty calls for resignation, acknowledging she "mishandled the situation."
- A settlement was reached (details under seal).

### Example 4: UC Berkeley Ann Coulter Cancellation (April 2017)

**The Hypocrisy:** The birthplace of the Free Speech Movement effectively cancelled a conservative speaker due to threatened violence from progressive protesters, with the university citing "security concerns."

**Key Facts:**
- **April 2017:** Berkeley College Republicans invited Ann Coulter to speak on April 27.
- University administrators initially cancelled citing "specific threats by anarchist groups."
- They rescheduled to May 2, but moved it to daytime, in a building far from campus center, during study week when no classes were in session.
- Young America's Foundation withdrew, blaming Berkeley for "lack of assurances for protections from foreseeable violence from unrestrained leftist agitators."
- **The ACLU criticized the protesters:** "For the future of our democracy, we must protect bigoted speech from government censorship."
- **Even progressive Senators Bernie Sanders and Elizabeth Warren** said Coulter should have been permitted to speak.
- The Berkeley College Republicans and YAF sued the university for discrimination against conservative speakers.

**Coulter's statement:** "It's a sad day for free speech... Everyone who should believe in free speech fought against it or ran away."

**Sources:**
- [NPR: Ann Coulter Speech Is Off at UC Berkeley](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2017/04/26/525745159/after-back-and-forth-ann-coulter-speech-is-off-at-uc-berkeley)

## KEY STATISTICS

### Overall Trends (2023-2024)

- **2024 saw record deplatforming attempts:** Around half resulted in cancellations, revoked invitations, or substantial disruptions.
- **90% of ideologically motivated disruptions** came from the left (213 vs. 23), according to FIRE's database.
- **Since October 7, 2023:** 54% of all deplatforming attempts related to Israeli-Palestinian conflict.
- **Self-censorship:** 42% of conservative students say they cannot speak freely on campus vs. 13% of liberal students.

### State-Level Censorship

- **10,000 books banned** from U.S. schools in 2023-24 school year under Republican-led laws (tripling from previous year).
- **8,000+ book bans** occurred in Florida and Iowa alone.
- Florida, Texas, Georgia, North Carolina: **Over 60% of professors considering leaving** due to political climate.

## CONCLUSION

Both sides claim to champion "free speech" when it benefits their viewpoint and seek to restrict speech when it harms their cause:

**THE RIGHT claims:**
- Conservative speakers are silenced on campus
- Viewpoint diversity is needed

**THE RIGHT practices:**
- State laws banning what professors can teach
- Eliminating tenure to make faculty easier to fire
- Organized harassment campaigns against liberal professors
- Book bans at unprecedented scale

**THE LEFT claims:**
- Speech that causes "harm" should be restricted
- Marginalized voices need protection

**THE LEFT practices:**
- Shouting down speakers, including moderates and even Democrats
- Violence against those who merely attend controversial events
- Firing professors over academic content that offends
- Treating disagreement as inherently harmful

**The honest assessment:** Neither side is principled about free speech. Both use it as a rhetorical weapon to protect their own expression while restricting their opponents'. True free speech advocacy would defend the speech of those you disagree with - something both sides consistently fail to do.
