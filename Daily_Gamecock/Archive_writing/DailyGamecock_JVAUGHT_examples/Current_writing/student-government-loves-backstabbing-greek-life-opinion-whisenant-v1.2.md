# Guest Column: Student Government loves backstabbing Greek life

Fraudulent.

That's the word Student Government chose for a funding request for a charity event benefiting kids with disabilities, illnesses and life challenges. Whether that label was justified or a convenient excuse depends on how much credit you're willing to give an organization that approved the funding first and asked questions later.

Alpha Phi Omega submitted the request, while the Interfraternity Council planned Greek Jam, a charity basketball tournament in Five Points benefiting Camp Cole, which gives kids with disabilities, illnesses and life challenges time to be kids rather than patients.

Alpha Phi Omega, a co-ed service fraternity, submitted the funding request on behalf of Greek Jam. The Interfraternity Council organized and ran the event. APO's role was administrative — it was the registered organization that could apply for Student Government funds. This distinction matters, because when Student Government labeled the request "fraudulent," it punished APO for filing paperwork on an event everyone involved understood IFC was running.

Student Government was asked to fund the event. It approved it, let the organizers move forward on that promise and then, by all appearances, turned around and branded the request fraudulent — suspending Alpha Phi Omega from accessing funding tied to a nonprofit that supports children living with serious illnesses and disabilities.

Columbia Mayor Daniel Rickenmann also put his support in writing, endorsing Greek Jam to the Five Points Association and explicitly stating he wanted the event to happen.

The finance committee approved Greek Jam with a written mayoral endorsement already on file. By any reasonable standard, Greek Jam had earned its legitimacy before it ever tipped off.

The Student Government Senate Finance Committee [approved Greek Jam](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1AjhDQ1290hrnh4VBMNH7c8zYHWtYpPTI/edit?usp=share_link&ouid=118374658715920394884&rtpof=true&sd=true) just 22 days before the event. The ask was simple and specific: help cover the core costs required to run a charity tournament for Camp Cole so the focus could stay on the mission instead of the bills.

For a moment, it looked like Student Government was going to take the free PR Greek life handed them. Instead, it greenlit the reimbursement, let Greek Jam happen and then retroactively pulled the funding. If it was an honest mistake, they had 22 days to catch it. They didn't. They waited until the event was over and the bill was due, likely leaving the organizers to cover costs they were told would be reimbursed.

Just six days after the event, Student Government flipped. By labeling the Greek Jam funding [request as fraudulent](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1ybAxnPd2fjGpFInpOTrOFVLqQCgjXVPH/edit?usp=share_link&ouid=118374658715920394884&rtpof=true&sd=true) and suspending all funding, it retroactively cast the event itself under suspicion. What had been unanimously approved was suddenly treated as illegitimate. If the first vote was genuine support, the reversal sure didn't look like it. Whether it was incompetence or strategy, the result is the same: organizers who trusted an official approval were left holding the bill for a charity event. That's not a paperwork issue — that's a breach of trust.

But this doesn't appear to be isolated to funding. It looks like a pattern: friendly until it's time to enforce power. Greek life represents a massive share of this campus — [over 10,000 students](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/fraternity_and_sorority_life/index.php). That's thousands of students who vote, volunteer and bankroll philanthropy for the university and students on campus.

If Student Government would undermine a charity event for kids with disabilities, illnesses and life challenges, what would it do to a campaign that actually threatened its power? Greek life found out in 2025.

In 2025, Student Government repaid that goodwill with what appeared to be a betrayal of a Greek candidate in its elections. Candidates from the Greek community ran against Student Government's seemingly handpicked [Courtney Tkacs and Jordan Richardson](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/02/meet-the-2025-student-government-executive-candidates-news-swenson). Instead of contesting the race on ideas, the campaign seemed less interested in winning than in destroying the candidate personally.

Tkacs herself is a member of [Alpha Delta Pi](https://sc.edu/study/colleges_schools/honors_college/about/our_people/honors_ambassadors/courtney_tkacs.php) — a Greek organization. She benefited from the same community she helped undermine. The moment a Greek candidate threatened her ticket, those ties apparently stopped mattering.

The Tkacs-Richardson campaign's communications director, Morgan Poirier, [posted a TikTok](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/02/election-violation-hearing) that skipped policy entirely and accused the opposing candidate of doing "the same thing that almost killed me to another girl." No evidence, no process, no chance to respond — just a claim that, intentionally or not, was built to spread before anyone could verify it. The damage would land before any defense could be mounted.

The Greek candidate tried to report fraud through official channels, but, by [their account](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/02/constitutional-council-rules-sufficient-evidence-of-fraud-dismisses-other-cases-news-flowe), the very bodies meant to police the election discouraged and blocked them.

The Tkacs-Richardson campaign wasn't just playing defense — it went on offense with [formal accusations](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1QvBi3uIyWpjjMnFzWrUbnWQSNkU8YeKE/view?usp=share_link) that the Greek candidate's team had improperly courted sororities, such as Delta Zeta and Phi Mu, before the official campaign period. The Constitutional Council reviewed the evidence and dismissed the complaints entirely. The very campaign that the Constitutional Council found sufficient evidence of fraud against tried to accuse its opponent of violations the council found no merit to.

The [Constitutional Council's ruling](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/02/constitutional-council-rules-sufficient-evidence-of-fraud-dismisses-other-cases-news-flowe) confirmed the damage. It found sufficient evidence of election fraud. It acknowledged that the TikTok had created a "climate of uncertainty and tainted the voting process," causing lasting harm throughout the voting period. By its own assessment, the process was compromised.

The remedy? A [24-hour social media ban](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RTyzr9O6_ymosVvDrEnGf1Ecuc5vxwcy/view?usp=share_link). [Tkacs became president](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/03/courtney-tkacs-will-become-next-student-body-president-news-beckman). The council found fraud, acknowledged a tainted process and handed down what amounted to a slap on the wrist. It's hard to read that as accountability.

The council's legal reasoning tried to split the difference. It found insufficient evidence that the claims were knowingly false under one section of the code but still found fraud under a broader provision. [Four justices signed the opinion](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/03/constitutional-council-releases-full-opinions-on-three-election-code-violation-cases-heard-expands-on-decisionsnews-bassett). The result was a ruling that acknowledged the process had been tainted while allowing the outcome to stand unchanged.

Student Government wants you to believe its codes are sacred. From where I'm standing, they are not. The codes appear to exist to be applied when convenient and ignored when costly, and when ignoring them is not an option, they get interpreted into whatever outcome Student Government needs.

Maybe no one in Student Government sat down and said "let's screw Greek life." They didn't have to. Greek Jam was fine when it made Student Government look generous. The Greek candidate was fine when he was just another name on the ballot. The moment either succeeded independently — or threatened to — Student Government moved to destroy them. That's not a conspiracy. It's a reflex.

Maybe none of this was personal. But when the same institution approves funding and then rescinds it, clears a candidate and then lets fraud go unpunished — and both times it's Greek organizations on the losing end — the pattern speaks louder than the intent. The problem is not one vote, one event or one ruling. It's a system where discretion beats fairness whenever the stakes rise.
