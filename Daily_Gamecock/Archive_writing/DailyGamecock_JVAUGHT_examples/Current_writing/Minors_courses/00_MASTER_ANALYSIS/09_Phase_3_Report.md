Count: 31

Examples:
  - Russian and Eurasian Studies Minor: 1 required, 100.0% loss per course
  - International Studies Minor: 1 required, 100.0% loss per course
  - Psychology Minor: 1 required, 100.0% loss per course

## Recommendations for Immediate Department Review

### Tier A: Critical - Requires Restructuring
Count: 36 minors

Actions:
1. **Restructure Required Courses**: Convert non-foundational required courses to electives
2. **Add Course Alternatives**: Create equivalent courses from different departments
3. **Cross-Listing Opportunities**: Collaborate with other departments for co-listed courses

### Tier B: High Risk - Requires Attention
Count: 22 minors

Actions:
1. **Expand Elective Options**: Increase number of elective courses
2. **Diversify Required Course Prefixes**: Add courses from other departments
3. **Create Substitution Paths**: Allow alternative courses to satisfy requirements

### Tier C: Moderate Risk - Monitor
Count: 7 minors

Actions:
1. **Ensure Course Stability**: Verify required courses are offered regularly
2. **Build Backup Resources**: Develop alternative learning materials
3. **Plan Department Communication**: Establish regular updates between departments

## Framework for Phase 4: Integrating Grade Spread & Enrollment Data

### Data Points to Collect in Phase 4

1. **Course Availability & Scheduling**
   - Historical course offering frequency (per academic year, per semester)
   - Instructor availability and course load distributions
   - Planned course cancellations or retirements

2. **Student Enrollment Patterns**
   - Grade distributions for each required course (A-F breakdown)
   - Historical course enrollment caps and actual enrollment
   - Pass rates and prerequisite satisfaction rates

3. **Grade Spread Analysis**
   - Percentage of students unable to satisfy prerequisites (Grade <C)
   - Correlation between required course difficulty and minor completion rates
   - Impact of grade requirements on forward progression

4. **Temporal Dynamics**
   - When required courses are offered (Spring vs. Fall bias)
   - Multi-year scheduling conflicts with other minors or majors
   - Semester-to-semester course availability variance

### Phase 4 Analysis Questions

1. Of the 81 TIER 1 minors, how many become **actually impossible** when combined with:
   - Grade prerequisites (e.g., Grade <C = no credit)
   - Actual course availability in recent years
   - Enrollment caps that exclude students

2. What is the **true student completion rate** for each minor?
   - How many students start vs. finish each minor
   - Where do students drop out (after which course)
   - How do completion rates correlate with our Phase 3 vulnerability scores

3. Which minors are at **greatest risk of de facto elimination**?
   - Even if technically possible, completion rates <20% indicate real problems
   - Recommend these for immediate curricular redesign

### Integration Methodology

1. **Overlay Phase 3 Vulnerability with Phase 4 Data**
   - High structural risk (Phase 3) + Low actual availability (Phase 4) = CRISIS
   - Create risk matrix: Structural Risk vs. Actual Risk

2. **Recalculate Impossible-if-One-Required with Real Data**
   - Use actual course offering frequency from 5-year historical data
   - Weight by probability of course not being available
   - Adjust scores for actual pass rates and grade requirements

3. **Identify Recovery Paths**
   - For impossible minors, identify which courses could be made available online
   - Which electives could substitute for required courses
   - Which courses could be co-listed with other departments
