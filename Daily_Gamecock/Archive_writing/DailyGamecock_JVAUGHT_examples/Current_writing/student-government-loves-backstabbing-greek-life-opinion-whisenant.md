Fraudulent.

That's how Student Government characterized a funding request for a charity event for kids with disabilities, illnesses and life challenges.

Alpha Phi Omega submitted the request, while the Interfraternity Council planned Greek Jam, a charity basketball tournament in Five Points benefiting Camp Cole, which gives kids with disabilities, illnesses and life challenges time to be kids rather than patients.

Student Government was asked to fund the event. What did it do? It happily approved it, let the organizers move forward on that promise and then turned around and branded the request fraudulent, suspending Alpha Phi Omega from accessing funding tied to a nonprofit that supports children living with serious illnesses and disabilities.

Columbia Mayor Daniel Rickenmann also put his support in writing, endorsing Greek Jam to the Five Points Association and explicitly stating he wanted the event to happen.

The finance committee approved Greek Jam with a written mayoral endorsement already on file. By any reasonable standard, Greek Jam had earned its legitimacy before it ever tipped off.

The Student Government Senate Finance Committee [approved Greek Jam](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1AjhDQ1290hrnh4VBMNH7c8zYHWtYpPTI/edit?usp=share_link&ouid=118374658715920394884&rtpof=true&sd=true) just 22 days before the event. The ask was simple and specific: help cover the core costs required to run a charity tournament for Camp Cole so the focus could stay on the mission instead of the bills.

For a moment, it looked like Student Government was going to take the free PR Greek life handed them. But it just couldn't stand the idea of a good thing happening for disabled kids, so it greenlit the reimbursement, let Greek Jam happen and then retroactively pulled the funding, which all but guarantees the organizers were left holding the bill.

Just six days after the event, Student Government flipped. By labeling the Greek Jam funding [request as fraudulent](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1ybAxnPd2fjGpFInpOTrOFVLqQCgjXVPH/edit?usp=share_link&ouid=118374658715920394884&rtpof=true&sd=true) and suspending all funding, it retroactively cast the event itself under suspicion. What had been unanimously approved was suddenly treated as illegitimate. If the first vote was genuine support, the reversal sure did not look like it. It looked like a setup.

But this is not isolated to funding. It is a pattern: friendly until it is time to enforce power. Greek life represents a massive share of this campus — [over 10,000 students](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/fraternity_and_sorority_life/index.php). That's thousands of students who vote, volunteer and bankroll philanthropy for the university and students on campus.

If Student Government would sabotage a charity event for kids with disabilities, illnesses and life challenges, what would it do to a campaign that actually threatened its power? Greek life found out in 2025.

In 2025, Student Government repaid that goodwill with a betrayal of a Greek candidate in its elections. Candidates from the Greek community ran against Student Government's seemingly handpicked [Courtney Tkacs and Jordan Richardson](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/02/meet-the-2025-student-government-executive-candidates-news-swenson). Instead of contesting the race on ideas, the campaign tried to ruin the candidate's entire life, not just the election, and Courtney Tkacs, an [Alpha Delta Phi](https://sc.edu/study/colleges_schools/honors_college/about/our_people/honors_ambassadors/courtney_tkacs.php) member, still turned on Greek candidates the moment they threatened her ticket.

The Tkacs-Richardson campaign's communications director, Morgan Poirier, [posted a TikTok](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/02/election-violation-hearing) that skipped policy entirely and accused the opposing Greek community candidate of doing "the same thing that almost killed me to another girl." Whether or not the claim was true, the format guaranteed it would spread before anyone could verify it. The damage would land before any defense could be mounted.

The Greek candidate tried to report fraud through official channels, but, by [their account](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/02/constitutional-council-rules-sufficient-evidence-of-fraud-dismisses-other-cases-news-flowe), the very bodies meant to police the election discouraged and blocked them.

The Tkacs-Richardson campaign wasn't just playing defense — it went on offense with [formal accusations](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1QvBi3uIyWpjjMnFzWrUbnWQSNkU8YeKE/view?usp=share_link) that the Greek candidate's team had improperly courted sororities, such as Delta Zeta and Phi Mu, before the official campaign period. The Constitutional Council reviewed the evidence and dismissed the complaints entirely. The very campaign that committed proven election fraud tried to accuse its opponent of violations that the council found no merit to.

The [Constitutional Council's ruling](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/02/constitutional-council-rules-sufficient-evidence-of-fraud-dismisses-other-cases-news-flowe) confirmed the damage. It found sufficient evidence of election fraud. It acknowledged that the TikTok had created a "climate of uncertainty and tainted the voting process," causing lasting harm throughout the voting period. By its own assessment, the process was compromised.

The remedy? A [24-hour social media ban](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RTyzr9O6_ymosVvDrEnGf1Ecuc5vxwcy/view?usp=share_link). [Tkacs became president](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/03/courtney-tkacs-will-become-next-student-body-president-news-beckman). Election fraud was brushed off, and the Greek candidate was left empty-handed.

The council's legal reasoning tried to split the difference. It found insufficient evidence that the claims were knowingly false under one section of the code but still found fraud under a broader provision. [Four justices signed the opinion](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2025/03/constitutional-council-releases-full-opinions-on-three-election-code-violation-cases-heard-expands-on-decisionsnews-bassett). The result was a ruling that acknowledged the process had been tainted while allowing the outcome to stand unchanged.

It wants you to believe its codes are sacred. They are not. The codes exist to be applied when convenient and ignored when costly, and when ignoring them is not an option, they get interpreted into whatever outcome Student Government needs.

Regardless of whether backstabbing Greek life was the intent or not, the optics read the same. Greek Jam was fine when it made Student Government look generous. The Greek candidate was fine when he was just another name on the ballot. The moment either succeeded independently — or threatened to — Student Government moved to destroy them.

Greek Jam proved it in finance. The election proved it in enforcement. The problem is not one vote, one event or one ruling. It is a system where discretion beats fairness whenever the stakes rise.
