# Guest Column: The importance of free speech on campus

**Author:** Brendan Connors
**Date:** December 6, 2023, 9:29pm
**Publication:** The Daily Gamecock

---

## The USC Event

Last spring, controversial Florida politician Laura Loomer visited USC to discuss free speech on campuses. Around 50 protesters gathered outside the venue, with approximately 20 entering the event space. Upon Loomer's arrival, demonstrators chanted "Fascist-free USC!" and disrupted proceedings by banging on doors and walls for over 20 minutes until police threatened arrest.

Connors notes that one USC student was assaulted during the incident, though serious injuries were avoided thanks to administrator and police intervention.

## Broader Campus Concerns

Connors highlights Penn State University's handling of a similar 2022 event. When Penn State canceled a comedy show featuring Alex Stein and Gavin McInnes following violent protests, administrators labeled the speakers using terms like "hateful and discriminatory" in official statements.

Connors argues this language may have inadvertently encouraged suppression of speakers, as students interpreted institutional messaging as justification for shutting down controversial viewpoints. He emphasizes that while certain speech categories lack First Amendment protection, "hate speech" is not legally recognized as such in the United States.

## Progress and Reflection

One year later, Penn State successfully hosted Stein and McInnes without administrative statements preceding the event. Connors suggests this reflected institutional reconsideration of their previous approach.

At USC, Connors points to a successful November 2023 porn ban debate featuring over 100 attendees, describing it as evidence that genuine dialogue is possible when the campus environment supports open discourse.
