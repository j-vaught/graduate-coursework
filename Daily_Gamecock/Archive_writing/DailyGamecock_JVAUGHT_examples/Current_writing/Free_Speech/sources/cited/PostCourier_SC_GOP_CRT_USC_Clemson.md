# USC Professors Worry About Becoming Political Targets Over Critical Race Theory Teachings

**Author:** Jessica Holdman
**Published:** December 12, 2021
**Source:** The Post and Courier - Columbia

## Overview

University of South Carolina professors have expressed concerns about potential political targeting related to their teaching of critical race theory (CRT) and related academic content. The article examines tensions between academic freedom and political scrutiny in South Carolina's higher education landscape.

## Key Information

In June, U.S. Representative Ralph Norman (R-SC), who represents the Rock Hill area, penned a letter to the presidents of USC and Clemson University calling on the institutions to "eradicate" any classes related to critical race theory, which Norman referred to as "neo-racist," "Marxist," and "antithetical."

### Congressional Signatories

Rep. Norman was joined on the letter by fellow South Carolina Republican congressional representatives:
- Nancy Mace (SC-1)
- Joe Wilson (SC-2)
- Jeff Duncan (SC-3)
- William Timmons (SC-4)
- Tom Rice (SC-7)

## Faculty Concerns

According to reporting, USC faculty were initially concerned when school leaders did not immediately respond to the letter from the congressional delegation. Faculty called for a statement from the administration in support of academic freedom.

The piece references education professor Allison Anders, who was on a research sabbatical at the time and could not be reached for comment.

## Broader Context

The article explores the broader statewide debate about how race-related historical topics should be taught in South Carolina's educational settings. It examines the tension between educational instruction and political scrutiny affecting universities across the state, with parallel concerns raised at Clemson University.
