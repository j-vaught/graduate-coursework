COLUMBIA — The University of South Carolina says it will continue to allow student organizations to hold outdoor events after the U.S. Department of Justice said it had received allegations that USC told some groups that controversial speakers or activities wouldn’t happen outside for the rest of the semester.

The exchange comes in the wake of the Sept. 10 assassination of conservative activist Charlie Kirk at a Utah university, which has prompted safety concerns at campuses elsewhere.

Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon [wrote in a Sept. 19 letter](https://x.com/AAGDhillon/status/1969107848492699713/photo/1) to university President Michael Amiridis that the department had received “troubling allegations” of USC administrators telling the campus chapters of Turning Point USA and Uncensored America that "controversial speakers and activities will no longer happen outside for the rest of the semester.”

Such a rule, if it existed, could violate the First Amendment rights of the student groups, Dhillon wrote. She specifically noted that she was not “opining on the veracity” of those allegations.

Turning Point USA is the conservative campus group Kirk founded in 2012. The Uncensored America group on campus [drew headlines last](https://www.postandcourier.com/education-lab/usc-roast-proud-boys-lawsuit-kamala-harris/article_b9656fce-75e6-11ef-9db5-db8ad48aeb4d.html) year for its vulgar “roast” of then-Vice President Kamala Harris featuring the founder of the Proud Boys.

“TPUSA and UA may voluntarily choose indoor venues for campus safety reasons in light of the potential threat from those that celebrate the horrific assassination of Charlie Kirk, but a mandate directed only at these groups because they are ‘controversial’ and may ‘disrupt’ a ‘population’ is impermissible viewpoint discrimination,” she wrote.

University spokesman Jeff Stensland said in an ensuing statement that the school honors requests from student organizations to host outdoor speakers, regardless of the organization or speaker. Stensland added that he did not have complete information about what may have been conveyed to the groups, but that it has always been USC’s practice to accommodate requests for outdoor events.

“As we have done for years, we will work directly (with Turning Point USA and Uncensored America) to ensure a safe outdoor environment for speakers & attendees,” Stensland said in a text.

Those measures will now include security assessments of “extended range threats” after the Utah shooting, university police chief Christopher Wuchenich [told lawmakers earlier](https://www.postandcourier.com/politics/south-carolina-campus-safety-hbcu-political/article_cfefabf2-c778-43a8-9998-1b5a666b8dbb.html) in the week.

S.C. Attorney General Alan Wilson also urged schools to not use security concerns as a reason to limit campus speech. Wilson said in a statement that he had had spoken to Amiridis on Sept. 19, and that the president had assured him that “no policies limiting free speech will be implemented.”

“Universities shouldn’t shut down free speech on campus because of potential danger but should be encouraging civil discourse and condemning political violence at all levels,” his statement said. “I believe that’s what Charlie would have wanted, and it’s what I want as well.”

Student leaders of Turning Point USA and Uncensored America did not immediately respond to the newspaper’s request for comment.

Kirk’s killing has already had an impact on South Carolina campuses.

After significant political pressure from the right, some faculty and staff at [Clemson University](https://www.postandcourier.com/greenville/politics/clemson-fires-professors-comments-kirk-shot/article_50b9d83a-b67d-4b1e-9b0c-6a9b690f2518.html), [Coastal Carolina University](https://www.postandcourier.com/myrtle-beach/news/coastal-carolina-fires-employee-charlie-kirk-murder-posts/article_fd7ef0a5-7ea8-4606-b366-18e06cf377af.html) and USC’s satellite campus at Union who had made online posts that were critical of Kirk or deemed insensitive to his killing were fired.
