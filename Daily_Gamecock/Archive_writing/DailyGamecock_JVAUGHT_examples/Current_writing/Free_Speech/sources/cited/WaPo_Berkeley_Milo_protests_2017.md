# Trump Threatens UC-Berkeley's Funding After Violent Protests Shut Down Milo Yiannopoulos Speech

**Source:** The Washington Post
**Date:** February 2, 2017

## What Happened

University of California police canceled an event by Milo Yiannopoulos and put the Berkeley campus on lockdown on Wednesday, February 1, 2017, after about 150 people wearing masks swarmed into a group of about 1,500 students who had been peacefully protesting the speech, and began setting fires, smashing windows, and throwing rocks and firecrackers at police.

## Trump's Response

President Trump picked a fight with California's flagship public university Thursday, threatening to pull funding from a school that has long symbolized the fight for free speech, on the day after violent protests shut down a controversial speaker.

Trump tweeted: "If U.C. Berkeley does not allow free speech and practices violence on innocent people with a different point of view — NO FEDERAL FUNDS?"

## Background and Context

In advance of the event, more than 100 faculty members signed letters urging Berkeley Chancellor Nicholas Dirks to cancel the speech, arguing that Yiannopoulos's views could change from protected speech to "incitement, harassment and defamation once they publicly target individuals."

## Analysis

The Washington Post published analysis describing the cancellation as "a predictable disaster that still managed to surprise" that "packed in half a dozen lessons about politics in the Trump era." The Berkeley protests, like the inaugural protests the month before, "went off the rails" due to activists using "black bloc" tactics to start violence and damage property.

## Broader Impact

Yiannopoulos, whose speech was canceled amid violent protest, later planned to return in September for a week of free-speech rallies. Free speech became "an exceptionally volatile issue," with debate over code words, safe spaces, implicit bias and microaggressions on campuses across the country.
