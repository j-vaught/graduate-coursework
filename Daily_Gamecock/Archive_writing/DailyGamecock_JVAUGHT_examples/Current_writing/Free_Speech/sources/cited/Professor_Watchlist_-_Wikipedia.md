**Professor Watchlist** is a website run by [conservative](/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States "Conservatism in the United States") advocacy organization [Turning Point USA](/wiki/Turning_Point_USA "Turning Point USA"). The website lists academic staff which Turning Point believes "discriminate against conservative students, promote anti-American values and advance leftist propaganda in the classroom."[1] It was launched in 2016 and had listed about 200 professors by December of that year.[2][3]

Responses to the site include the [American Association of University Professors](/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Professors "American Association of University Professors") and _[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ raising fears that it threatens [academic freedoms](/wiki/Academic_freedom "Academic freedom") by harassing and intimidating staff, conservative magazine _[National Review](/wiki/National_Review "National Review")_ describing it as an "irritable gesture" of victimhood by conservatives, and concerns about the safety and welfare of staff following a trend of threatening behavior and communication, including rape and death threats, being sent to listed faculty.[4][5][6][7][8]

In December 2016, 1,500 professors and faculty from across the globe petitioned to have their name added to the list in solidarity with academics who had been targeted and intimidated following their listing, with the message that the listed are "the sort of company we wish to keep."[9]

[Turning Point UK](/wiki/Turning_Point_UK "Turning Point UK") also maintains a similar site, Education Watch, which has been described as "[McCarthyism](/wiki/McCarthyism "McCarthyism") in the UK", gaining support from Conservative MP [Jacob Rees-Mogg](/wiki/Jacob_Rees-Mogg "Jacob Rees-Mogg") and Home Secretary [Priti Patel](/wiki/Priti_Patel "Priti Patel"). The [Council for the Defence of the British Universities](/wiki/Council_for_the_Defence_of_the_British_Universities "Council for the Defence of the British Universities") has also called Education Watch "populist rightwing propaganda".[8]

## Sourcing

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Professor_Watchlist&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Sourcing")]

Concerns have been raised about the process in which academic staff are listed. Professors have reported being listed for any discussion of race or politics, including in academic publications. [Julio Cesar Pino](/wiki/Julio_Cesar_Pino "Julio Cesar Pino"), a professor of Latin American history, was added to the list on the basis of rumors that the [FBI](/wiki/FBI "FBI") may have investigated him for having connections to [ISIS](/wiki/ISIS "ISIS"). Lamb, a director of constitutional enforcement and transparency at Turning Point, has described the site as "simply aggregating" professors who have been the subject of news reports.[6]

[Campus Reform](/wiki/Campus_Reform "Campus Reform"), a part of the [Leadership Institute](/wiki/Leadership_Institute "Leadership Institute"), and [Discover the Networks](/wiki/Discover_the_Networks "Discover the Networks"), a website run by the [David Horowitz Freedom Center](/wiki/David_Horowitz_Freedom_Center "David Horowitz Freedom Center"), were the sources for most of the professors initially listed on the Professor Watchlist.[10]

## Concerns

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Professor_Watchlist&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Concerns")]

_[Slate](/wiki/Slate_\(magazine\) "Slate \(magazine\)")_ columnist Rebecca Schuman described the website as "abjectly terrifying" and said that she feared for the safety of the listed professors.[11] Some have criticized the website as a threat to academic freedom; Hans-Joerg Tiede, the associate secretary for the American Association of University Professors' department of academic freedom, tenure and governance, told _The_ _New York Times_ : "There is a continuing cycle of these sorts of things. They serve the same purpose: to intimidate individuals from speaking plainly in their classrooms or in their publications."[12] One professor included in the site, [George Yancy](/wiki/George_Yancy "George Yancy"), wrote that it is "essentially a new species of McCarthyism, especially in terms of its overtones of 'disloyalty' to the American republic".[13][14]

According to _[Inside Higher Ed](/wiki/Inside_Higher_Ed "Inside Higher Ed")_ , some critics consider the website "more annoying than dangerous".[14]

Critics including [Peter Dreier](/wiki/Peter_Dreier "Peter Dreier") of [Occidental College](/wiki/Occidental_College "Occidental College")—who is listed on the site for having criticized the [National Rifle Association](/wiki/National_Rifle_Association "National Rifle Association") and using [Howard Zinn](/wiki/Howard_Zinn "Howard Zinn")'s _[A People's History of the United States](/wiki/A_People%27s_History_of_the_United_States "A People's History of the United States")_ as a required text—have pointed out errors of fact that may make Professor Watchlist less than reliable as a source of information. Dreier's entry formerly listed him as a former employee of the [Industrial Areas Foundation](/wiki/Industrial_Areas_Foundation "Industrial Areas Foundation") and as the man who inspired college student [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") to become a community organizer. Dreier identifies these claims about him as "complete fantasy". He also noted elements of his biography that the website completely omitted, such as his work with [labor unions](/wiki/Trade_union "Trade union"), his activism in favor of a [minimum wage](/wiki/Minimum_wage "Minimum wage"), and the books he wrote.[15]

[Kent State](/wiki/Kent_State_University "Kent State University") professor [Julio Pino](/wiki/Julio_Cesar_Pino "Julio Cesar Pino") said to _The New York Times_ the site is "a kind of normalizing of prosecuting professors, shaming professors, defaming professors."[16]

The website's organizers say that it simply provides conservative students with a guide to their professors, akin to [RateMyProfessors.com](/wiki/RateMyProfessors.com "RateMyProfessors.com"),[17] enabling them to avoid left-wing classes.[18]

Over one hundred [University of Notre Dame](/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame "University of Notre Dame") faculty members signed an open letter asking to be included in the site, saying in part:[19]

> We surmise that the purpose of your list is to shame and silence faculty who espouse ideas you reject. But your list has had a different effect upon us. We are coming forward to stand with the professors you have called "dangerous," reaffirming our values and recommitting ourselves to the work of teaching students to think clearly, independently and fearlessly.

In response to the Notre Dame letter, [University of Chicago](/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") psychology professor [Leslie Kay](/wiki/Leslie_M._Kay "Leslie M. Kay") started the website "Free Academics". This website lists the names of professors across the United States who have signed it to ask for their names to be added to the list. As of December 2016, it had over 1,500 signatories.[20]

The list has been accused of disproportionately targeting professors of color and other minority professors.[21]

The website has been criticized for using [surveillance](/wiki/Surveillance "Surveillance")-type propaganda to manipulate ideas of truth, equality, and freedom.[22][23][24] Critics have compared Professor Watchlist to the actions of U.S. senator [Joseph McCarthy](/wiki/Joseph_McCarthy "Joseph McCarthy"), who tried to publicly identify American citizens as [Communists](/wiki/Communists "Communists") and Communist sympathizers in the 1950s.[25][26] _[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ wrote that it was "a threat to academic freedom."[22]

Hans-Joerg Tiede, a staff member of the [American Association of University Professors](/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Professors "American Association of University Professors"), said of a professor who was named for writing a book chapter on teaching mathematics to minority ethnic children: "She was inundated with death threats. She was Jewish and received anti-Semitic threats and threats of sexual assault. Instances like that are happening with some regularity".[27]

The website erroneously claimed that [Hunter Biden](/wiki/Hunter_Biden "Hunter Biden") would "assist in lecturing a course" at [Tulane University](/wiki/Tulane_University "Tulane University") in the fall semester of 2021.[28]
