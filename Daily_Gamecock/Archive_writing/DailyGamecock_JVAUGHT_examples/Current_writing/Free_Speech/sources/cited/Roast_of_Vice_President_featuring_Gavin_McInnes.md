COLUMBIA, S.C. (WIS) - In a 27 to 10 vote, the University of South Carolina’s (USC) student government denied a $3,576.99 allotment for funding for Uncensored America’s roast of Vice President Kamala Harris on Wednesday.  
  
The student organization’s request included $1,500 guest speakers – including [Proud Boys Founder Gavin McInnes](https://www.wistv.com/2024/08/31/proud-boys-founder-gavin-mcinnes-speaks-ahead-usc-comedy-roast-wis-exclusive-interview/ "https://www.wistv.com/2024/08/31/proud-boys-founder-gavin-mcinnes-speaks-ahead-usc-comedy-roast-wis-exclusive-interview/"), $803 for decorations, $647 for camera rentals, $126.99 for advertising flyers and $500 for security provided by USC Police.

Some student senators argued that a 2010 U.S. Supreme Court case of [Citizens United vs the Federal Elections Commission](https://www.fec.gov/legal-resources/court-cases/citizens-united-v-fec/ "https://www.fec.gov/legal-resources/court-cases/citizens-united-v-fec/") ruled that money going toward political campaigns is considered a form of protected speech.

Other senators made the point that the roast is not a part of former president Donald Trump’s campaign and as it relates to university rules for student funding requests.

“In order to both protect – I think – the university and student government, we have kind of had a policy of, ‘We will not take your political viewpoint into consideration when doing this.’ We don’t have to pick sides. If you’re going to not receive money, it’s because you did something fundamentally wrong. You broke our rules, or our policies, or our codes. They did follow our rules, and I think ultimately there weren’t many legitimate reasons to deny them the money,” said Student Senator Camden Kaye.

Meanwhile, student body president Patton Byars is encouraging students to attend, Blatt Bonanza, another event on campus happening that same day.

“This “the” event on our campus on September 18. It’s Blatt Bonanza, and we’re going to have a lot of free and fun activities there. Whether that’s inflatables, bouncy houses, spike ball, pickle ball among other things. That’s the event that we should be talking about because that’s the free and fun event for all of our students,” said Byars.

Senior Zamiyah Johnson, who attended Wednesday night’s senate meeting, shared her response to the vote.

“Actually I honestly truthfully did not go in there thinking that they were going to...that it was going to go the way that it went. But I am absolutely ecstatic,” said Johnson.

Student Senator Isabella Miller also argued funding flyers for advertising the event from the student government could also violate [South Carolina laws ]( https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/title-16/chapter-15/section-16-15-305/ " https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/title-16/chapter-15/section-16-15-305/")about sexual misconduct.

“The South Carolina law specifically dictates secretions as part of the sexual misconduct and the promotional material that they’re putting out there does not only have the name of certain excretions but the image. And with Instagram that is available to 13 and older, they may have been disseminating that to people who are under the age of 18 which is a violation of state law,” said Miller.

In a separate motion, the student senate ultimately approved a motion funding for increased security across campus on Wednesday, Sept. 18, before the event.
