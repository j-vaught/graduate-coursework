### Uncensored America "Roast of Kamala Harris" Event - September 2024

**Event Overview**
- **Title:** The Roast of Cumala Harris with Milo & Gavin
- **Date:** September 18, 2024
- **Location:** Russell House Ballroom, University of South Carolina
- **Speakers:** Gavin McInnes (Proud Boys founder), Milo Yiannopoulos (political commentator)
- **Organizer:** Uncensored America student organization
- **Description:** Comedy roast event that became a focal point for free speech and viewpoint neutrality debates on campus. Student senate initially denied funding but later reconsidered and granted the request.

**Free Speech and Legal Implications**
- Uncensored America sued USC for violating First Amendment rights by failing to remain viewpoint neutral in initial funding denial
- Student petition against the event garnered over 25,000 signatures
- Event highlighted tensions between inclusive campus environments and free expression

**Counter-Event: Blatt Bonanza**
- **Date:** September 18, 2024 (simultaneous with roast)
- **Location:** Blatt Field, USC
- **Attendance:** Over 1,000 students
- **Organizers:** Student Government Association, Carolina Productions, Kappa Delta Chi, Residence Hall Association
- **Activities:** Free food, games (Spikeball), bounce houses, video game trucks, free t-shirts
- **Context:** Organized as counter-programming to provide alternative space during controversial Uncensored America roast
- **Recognition:** Selected as Program of the Year at SAACURH fall conference; won Student-Driven Program of the Year from Association of College Unions International

**Event Overview**
- **Title:** The American Comeback Tour with Charlie Kirk
- **Date:** March 2025 (USC stop)
- **Location:** Greene Street/Tommy Trojan, University of South Carolina

- **Format:** Interactive debate format where students were invited to challenge Kirk at a microphone tent marked "Prove Me Wrong"

- White privilege and racial equity

- Event drew both supporters and protesters

## National Campus Free Speech Controversies

### Milo Yiannopoulos UC Berkeley 2017 Protests

**February 1, 2017 Event**
- **Location:** UC Berkeley, California
- **Speaker:** Milo Yiannopoulos (editor-at-large, Breitbart News)
- **Scheduled Topic:** Cultural appropriation
- **Planned Time:** 8 p.m.
- **Status:** Cancelled due to violent protests

**Protest Details**
- Over 1,500 protesters gathered on Sproul Plaza outside student union building
- Approximately 150 "black bloc" protesters infiltrated the larger protest crowd
- Property damage: $100,000+ in campus damages
- Vandalism included: windows smashed, fires set, ATMs damaged, Bank of America and Starbucks targeted
- Violent incidents documented: pepper spray deployment, physical altercations captured on video
- UC Police evacuated Yiannopoulos from campus approximately 2 hours before scheduled event

**Video Documentation**
- Video of woman in "Make Bitcoin Great Again" hat being pepper sprayed while being interviewed
- Video of physical altercations during the protest
- Milo Yiannopoulos later released YouTube videos claiming they were made at Sproul Plaza

**Aftermath: September 2017 "Free Speech Week"**
- Yiannopoulos announced planned "Free Speech Week" for September 24-27
- Promised appearances by Yiannopoulos, Ann Coulter, and Steve Bannon
- Event fell apart after co-organizers (The Berkeley Patriot and Yiannopoulos) failed to confirm guest list and book indoor venues
- Ultimately cancelled

## Congressional Hearings on Campus Free Speech and Antisemitism

### December 5, 2023 Congressional Hearing on Campus Antisemitism

**Event Details**
- **Committee:** House Committee on Education and the Workforce
- **Date:** December 5, 2023
- **Format:** Congressional hearing with live stream
- **Topic:** Antisemitism on college campuses

**University Presidents Testifying**
- Claudine Gay (Harvard University)
- Liz Magill (University of Pennsylvania)
- Sally Kornbluth (Massachusetts Institute of Technology)
- Pamela Nadell (Professor of History, American University)

**Key Controversy: Genocide Rhetoric**
- Representative Elise Stefanik asked whether calls for genocide of Jews would violate university codes of conduct
- Presidents repeatedly deflected the question
- Claudine Gay stated response would depend on "context"
- Line of questioning highlighted free speech vs. protected speech boundaries debate

**Consequences**
- Liz Magill resigned as University of Pennsylvania president four days after hearing
- Hearing sparked intense criticism and widespread commentary
- Prompted additional congressional hearings on campus speech and antisemitism

**Congressional Response**
- White House condemned university presidents following the hearing
- Congress held additional hearings on campus antisemitism and related issues

**Legislative Actions**
- **H.R.2508:** Campus Free Speech Restoration Act (118th Congress, 2023-2024)
- House Committee passed bill mandating free speech policies for federal funding eligibility
- Bill requires institutions to:
  - Ensure colleges/universities not forced to adhere to DEI principles
  - Make free speech a condition for receiving federal funds under Title IV of Higher Education Act
  - Educate students on First Amendment rights
  - Prohibit forcing students, faculty, or applicants to take political litmus tests
  - Require schools to annually disclose free speech policies

## Educational Censorship and Book Banning

### PEN America Reports and Resources

**Organization Overview**
- PEN America: National organization fighting government censorship in education
- Maintains Index of Educational Gag Orders tracking state-level censorship legislation since January 2021

**Major Reports**
1. **America's Censored Classrooms (2024)**
   - Documents censorship trends in K-12 and higher education
   - Tracks educational gag orders and legislative restrictions on freedom to learn and teach

2. **America's Censored Campuses 2025: Expanding the Web of Control**
   - 2025 data: 93 bills censoring higher education in 32 states
   - 21 bills enacted into law in 15 states
   - Documents stealth censorship through institutional neutrality and viewpoint diversity policies

3. **The Normalization of Book Banning**
   - 2023-2024 school year: Over 10,000 instances of banned books
   - Impacts more than 4,000 unique titles
   - Targeted removals focus on books with characters of color, LGBTQ+ identities, sexual content

4. **America's Censored Classrooms 2023**
   - Previous year data on educational censorship trends

5. **Banned in the USA: Narrating the Crisis**
   - Narrative analysis of censorship patterns and impacts

6. **Educational Censorship Continues: The 2023 Legislative Sessions**
   - Mid-year analysis of 2023 legislative censorship efforts

**Current Focus Areas**
- 2024-2025 shift: Moving beyond direct topic prohibition toward "stealth censorship"
- Censorship bills increasingly attach to worthy-sounding goals: institutional neutrality, viewpoint diversity
- Expansion of censorship in higher education sector

## Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression (FIRE)

### Organization Overview and Presentations

**About FIRE**
- **Name:** Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression (formerly Foundation for Individual Rights in Education)
- **Founded:** 1999
- **Status:** 501(c)(3) non-profit civil liberties organization
- **Mission:** Protect freedom of speech on college campuses and off-campus free speech advocacy
- **Rebranding:** 2022 name change and expansion into broader free speech advocacy beyond education

**Academic Freedom Work**
- Assists students and faculty in vindicating free speech and academic freedom rights
- Targets violations of due process rights beyond constitutional law and stated university policies
- Strategy: Threat of exposure or legal action often sufficient to persuade institutional remedies
- Success rate: Successfully intervened in hundreds of disputes, mostly through negotiation, some through litigation

**Notable Cases**
- Hamline University (2022): Objected to punishment of adjunct professor Erika López Prater for showing Islamic prophet Muhammad painting in art history course; filed accreditor complaint

**FIRE Faculty Network**
- Coalition of faculty supporters promoting free expression and academic freedom
- **April 13, 2023 Presentation:** Amy Wax spoke to Faculty Network about accusations against her
  - 90-minute webinar with 1-hour Q&A
  - Attendance: Over 150 faculty members from FIRE's network
  - Format: Faculty had opportunity to challenge her arguments and ask difficult questions

**Video Presentations and Content**
- FIRE produces video content on free speech topics
- Notable: John McWhorter conversation on social media mobs, safetyism, and free speech
- Linguist and college professor perspective on campus speech issues

## Key Themes and Patterns

### Free Speech vs. Institutional Values
- Tension between protecting free expression and maintaining inclusive campus environments
- Debate over institutional neutrality vs. values-driven institutional policies
- Questions about funding allocation based on viewpoint

### Censorship Methods Evolution
- 2021-2023: Direct educational gag orders prohibiting specific topics
- 2024-2025: "Stealth censorship" through institutional neutrality language and viewpoint diversity policies

### Congressional Engagement
- Increased federal scrutiny of campus free speech issues
- Bipartisan concern about educational censorship
- Legislation conditioning federal funding on free speech protections

### Student and Community Response
- Counter-programming events like Blatt Bonanza show alternative approaches
- Large-scale protests and activism regarding controversial speakers
- Documented incidents of property damage and violence in some contexts (UC Berkeley 2017)

### Legal Challenges
- Viewpoint discrimination claims by student organizations
- First Amendment litigation against universities
- Constitutional questions about institutional liability
