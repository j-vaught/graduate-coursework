# Proud Boys founder Gavin McInnes speaks ahead of University of South Carolina comedy roast in WIS exclusive interview

COLUMBIA, S.C. (WIS) - Proud Boys founder Gavin McInnes spoke in an exclusive interview with WIS Investigative reporter Deric Rush Friday on the controversy surrounding a scheduled roast of Vice President and Democratic Presidential nominee Kamala Harris. 

The conversation has been ongoing for a week now. Uncensored America, a student organization at the University of South Carolina, will have McInnes and Milo Yiannopoulos as speakers at a comedy roast of Vice President Harris on Sept. 18. 

The University administration said it would not interfere with the event with respect to Uncensored America and their guests’ First Amendment rights

Alumni and some legislators said they felt USC’s reaction to the event has been poor. 

“Allowing these types of speakers onto campus and allowing them to use the university facilities? This statement just wasn’t good for minority students or any other marginalized community on campus,” said former Alumna Courtney McClain, who started a petition protesting the event that has received over 15,000 signatures after its initial goal of just 1,000. 

Gov. Henry McMaster’s stance on the event, however, was a bit different.

“The University does not have the right to cancel speech,” he said, only making an exception for speech that would directly cause harm to others.

Despite McInnes sparking controversy, legal experts explain that legally, the university stepping in to stop the event could open up the floodgates of lawsuits against the school.

“The organizers would like nothing better than to have the even canceled because then they will be able to claim truthfully that their speech has been censored,” said South Carolina Press Association attorney Jay Bender.

Bender explained, in the event the university stopped the roast, USC would be liable under federal civil rights laws that protect hate speech under the first amendment.

McInnes insists he will not be the one bringing the hate to the event.

“If you’re looking for violence you’re looking on the wrong side of the political spectrum. The left are the violent ones they burnt down this country for two years straight. We had one riot on January 6,” he said.

When asked what he hoped would come out of the event, McInnes said, “It won’t exactly be like the classic roast you see in Comedy Central. We’re just going to make fun of what could be the worst president in American history.”

The roast is scheduled for Sept. 18 at 7 p.m. in the Russell House Ballroom on the university’s campus.
