# Bodies Upon the Gears

**URL:** https://www.thefire.org/bodies-upon-the-gears

## Content

## Overview

This webpage presents a documentary titled "Bodies Upon the Gears," produced by the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression (FIRE). The film explores the historic Free Speech Movement at UC Berkeley in 1964.

## Key Content

**Documentary Focus:**
The film chronicles how UC Berkeley's ban on political campus speech sparked a pivotal student movement. Students from diverse political backgrounds—including "Students for Goldwater, conservatives, liberals, and communists"—united to defend First Amendment rights.

**Central Figure:**
Mario Savio, a Queens, New York native who had volunteered during the 1964 Mississippi Freedom Summer registering Black voters, became a leading voice. Returning to Berkeley that fall, he helped organize the movement in response to the university's prohibition on student activism tables.

**Savio's Famous Quote:**
"There's a time when the operation of the machine becomes so odious—makes you so sick at heart—that you can't take part" before calling people to action.

**Production Details:**
- Directed by Academy Award-winning filmmaker Paul Wagner
- Narrated by Nadine Strossen, FIRE Senior Fellow and former ACLU national president
- Presented as an American Focus production

**Historical Impact:**
What began as a campus protest at Berkeley eventually spread nationwide, reshaping American history's approach to free expression on college campuses.

The page includes archival photographs from December 1964 protests and offers access to the full documentary.
