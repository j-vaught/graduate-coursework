# Big win for campus free speech: Harvard won't issue statements on hot-button social and political issues

**URL:** https://www.thefire.org/news/big-win-campus-free-speech-harvard-wont-issue-statements-hot-button-social-and-political

## Content

Harvard University has adopted a policy of institutional neutrality, announcing it will not issue official statements on contentious social and political matters outside its core educational mission. The Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression (FIRE) celebrates this decision as a significant victory for campus free speech.

### The Policy Change

Harvard's leadership, including its governing board, accepted recommendations from a faculty working group stating the university should refrain from "issue official statements about public matters that do not directly affect the university's core function." The institution defines its core function as maintaining "an environment in which its members can research, teach, and learn."

### Rationale

The report provides three primary reasons for this approach:

1. **Institutional credibility**: Official positions on matters outside the university's expertise undermine institutional integrity, as faculty members' personal expertise differs from institutional authority.

2. **Avoiding pressure**: Taking positions invites competing demands from multiple constituencies on virtually every issue.

3. **Community inclusivity**: Official stances risk alienating community members by appearing to favor particular viewpoints.

### Broader Impact

FIRE notes this aligns with principles outlined in the University of Chicago's "Kalven Report" and suggests Harvard's leadership may inspire similar reforms across American higher education.

### Historical Context

Harvard previously scored lowest in FIRE's 2024 College Free Speech Rankings, prompting sustained advocacy for reform.
