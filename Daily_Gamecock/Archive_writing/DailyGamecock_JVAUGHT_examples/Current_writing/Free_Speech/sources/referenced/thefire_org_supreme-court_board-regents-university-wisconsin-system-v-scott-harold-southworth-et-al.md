# BOARD OF REGENTS OF THE UNIVERSITY OF WISCONSIN SYSTEM v. SCOTT HAROLD SOUTHWORTH, et al.

**URL:** https://www.thefire.org/supreme-court/board-regents-university-wisconsin-system-v-scott-harold-southworth-et-al

## Content

## Case Information
- **Citation:** 529 U.S. 217 (2000)
- **Argued:** November 9, 1999
- **Decided:** March 22, 2000
- **Court:** Rehnquist Court
- **Vote:** 9-0 (Unanimous)

## Legal Question
The case examined "whether a mandatory student activity fee used to facilitate extracurricular student speech at a public university violates the First Amendment."

## Background
Three law students at the University of Wisconsin-Madison challenged a mandatory activity fee that funded various campus services and student organizations with diverse political viewpoints. The Christian students argued this system forced them to financially support groups whose ideologies they opposed, violating their free speech and religious liberty rights. Lower courts initially sided with the students.

## Ruling
The Supreme Court reversed the lower courts' decisions, upholding the fee system as constitutional, provided it maintained viewpoint neutrality in fund distribution.

## Significance
This landmark decision confirmed that universities can require activity fees to support diverse campus speech, establishing important protections for student expression rights in higher education while requiring content-neutral administration.
