# Florida Sociology Curriculum and Professor Pushback

## The Core Conflict

Florida International University faculty are opposing new state-mandated curriculum and textbooks for Introduction to Sociology courses. Nineteen FIU sociology professors signed a letter asserting the changes violate academic freedom and misrepresent the discipline.

## Key Complaint

The primary concern focuses on content omissions. As one associate professor stated:

> "There are no discussions of systemic or structural racism — a core concept in sociology."

## The Textbook Creation Process

Rather than developing original material, Florida's statewide working group adapted an existing open-source textbook by **deleting entire chapters** covering disfavored topics. The group included:
- Four sociologists
- Four state education officials

## Legislative Timeline

- **2023**: Legislature banned teaching "unproven, speculative" concepts including systemic inequality
- **2024**: Sociology eliminated as a core requirement
- **2025**: State determined all existing textbooks violated new restrictions; created replacement textbook

## Academic Freedom Concerns

Professors argued that the state has shifted:

> "from telling us what we cannot teach to telling us exactly what we can teach."

One professor opted out of teaching the course rather than risk violating state mandates.

## Administration Response

Governor DeSantis dismissed shared governance concerns, stating universities should be:

> "run by the president and administration" consistent with state interests—not faculty preferences.

## Implications

This case demonstrates:
1. Direct state intervention in curriculum design
2. Removal of established academic content
3. Undermining of faculty expertise and shared governance
4. Pressure on faculty to self-censor rather than teach established disciplinary content
5. State prioritization of political ideology over academic standards

## Significance for Academic Freedom

The Florida sociology case represents one of the most direct examples of state-level censorship of academic content, moving beyond limiting new courses to actively removing established academic material and replacing it with state-approved alternatives.
