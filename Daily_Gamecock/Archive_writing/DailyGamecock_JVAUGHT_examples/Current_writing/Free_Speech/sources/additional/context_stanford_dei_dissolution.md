# Stanford Dissolves DEI Office

Stanford University has shut down its Office for Inclusion, Belonging and Intergroup Communication (IBIC), citing financial and organizational reasons.

## Impact on Staff

The dissolution has created uncertainty for employees. The closure has left staff and program participants questioning their futures at the institution, with expressions of concern about what comes next for the various initiatives that IBIC oversaw.

## Context of Federal Pressure

President Donald Trump signed an Executive Order to end DEI programs across federally funded agencies in January 2025, and he has specifically targeted Ivy League universities, reducing research funding and threatening to remove the visa status of international students. The U.S. Department of Education warned that it may withdraw federal funding from private universities, including Stanford, unless they dismantle "race-based practices."

## What Happens Next

Some of IBIC's programs will be transferred to the Office of Student Engagement (OSE), though the scope of program integration remains unclear. The lay off of the director and one part-time employee represents a significant reduction in resources dedicated to inclusion and belonging work on campus.

## Broader Pattern

Stanford's action reflects a broader institutional response to federal pressure following Trump's executive orders. Universities have increasingly used fiscal pressures and federal compliance concerns as justifications for eliminating diversity-focused programs and offices, framing these actions as necessary institutional responses to federal directives rather than voluntary ideological choices.

## Website Changes

In February 2025, Stanford had already begun removing DEI-related content from its websites, including removing statements about the university's "deep respect for diversity in all its forms."
