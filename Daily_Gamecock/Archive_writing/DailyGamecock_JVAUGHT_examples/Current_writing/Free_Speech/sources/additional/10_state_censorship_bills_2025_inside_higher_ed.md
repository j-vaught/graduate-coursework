# 2025 Higher Education Censorship Bills: Record-Breaking Legislative Expansion

## Key Findings

According to a PEN America report, state lawmakers enacted a record 21 censorship bills across 15 states in 2025. All legislatures that passed these measures were Republican-controlled.

## Main Statistics

- 32 states introduced 93 total bills restricting higher education
- 21 bills were enacted across Arkansas, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, North Dakota, Ohio, Texas, Utah, West Virginia, and Wyoming
- More than half of U.S. college students now study in states with at least one restrictive law

## Types of Censorship

### Direct Censorship (Gag Orders)

Fourteen enacted bills contained gag orders; seven specifically targeted higher education. These bills restrict what topics faculty can teach or discuss:
- Examples include Texas Tech's rules banning transgender topic instruction
- Texas A&M's restrictions on teaching about race or gender
- Direct restrictions on what professors can say in classrooms

### Indirect Censorship

State legislators introduced 78 indirect censorship measures, including:
- Curriculum restrictions
- Tenure limitations
- Institutional neutrality mandates
- Diversity and inclusion bans (DEI eliminations)
- Governance restrictions

Twenty of these indirect censorship bills passed into law.

## Notable State Laws

**Ohio SB 1:**
- Requires institutional neutrality on political issues
- Bans diversity, equity, and inclusion initiatives
- Restricts faculty input on institutional governance

**Indiana HB 1001:**
- Limits faculty senate powers
- Reduces faculty involvement in academic decisions
- Shifts more control to administrative leadership

**Texas Restrictions:**
- Texas Tech: Transgender topic instruction bans
- Texas A&M: Restrictions on teaching about race and gender

**Other States:**
- Multiple states enacted variations on curriculum control and institutional neutrality requirements

## Federal Pressure Context

The Trump administration's actions coincided with state-level censorship:
- 90+ Title VI investigations into higher education institutions
- $3.7 billion in research funding targeted for review/cuts
- 19 education-related executive orders issued
- International student visa revocations affecting enrollment

## Broader Implications

This represents the highest number of new higher education censorship laws passed in a single year, reflecting a coordinated effort across multiple states to control academic content and institutional autonomy. The laws target not just specific topics but the structural conditions that enable academic freedom.
