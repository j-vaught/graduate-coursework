# Harvard, Penn, MIT Presidents Testify to Congress About Campus Antisemitism

## Summary

The presidents of Harvard, University of Pennsylvania, and MIT testified before the House Committee on Education and the Workforce regarding rising antisemitism on their campuses. The hearing, titled "Holding Campus Leaders Accountable and Confronting Antisemitism," addressed tensions following Hamas's October 7 attack on Israel and subsequent Gaza conflict.

## Key Points

### Context

A report by the Anti-Defamation League found that Jewish students feeling physically safe on campus dropped from a majority pre-October 7 to just 46% afterward, with emotional safety declining to 33%.

### University Presidents' Positions

**Claudine Gay (Harvard)** acknowledged the difficulty of balancing "confront hate while preserving free expression," noting the school increased security and expanded support services.

**Elizabeth Magill (Penn)** emphasized that "where antisemitism goes unchecked, other forms of hate spread" and outlined a three-pronged action plan addressing safety, engagement, and education.

**Sally Kornbluth (MIT)** argued that "problematic speech needs to be countered with other speech and education" rather than through restrictive policies.

### Legislative Concerns

House Republicans questioned policies and demanded accountability, while Democrats advocated for increased funding to the Education Department's Office for Civil Rights, which investigates campus discrimination claims.

## Notes

This hearing marked a significant moment in the debate over free speech on college campuses and institutional responsibility in addressing hate speech and discrimination. The presidents' responses to questions about whether certain speech violated conduct codes would later spark significant controversy and calls for their resignations.
