# University of Florida Post-Tenure Review Results

## Key Findings

The University of Florida's first round of post-tenure reviews produced striking results that drew scrutiny from faculty leadership. Approximately **21-27% of reviewed professors** faced negative outcomes, significantly higher than peer institutions.

## Specific Numbers

According to Provost J. Scott Angle's June Faculty Senate presentation:

**Initial Review Round:**
- 258 tenured faculty members were reviewed
- Those receiving the lowest ratings or departing comprised "about 21 percent"

**Final Report (July 1):**
Among 262 professors:
- 31 retired, entered retirement agreements, or resigned
- 34 "did not meet expectations"
- 5 were rated "unsatisfactory"
- **Combined total: 27%**

## Comparative Context: Florida Public Universities

UF's outcomes diverged sharply from other Florida public universities:

| Institution | Unsatisfactory/Below Expectations |
|------------|----------------------------------|
| **University of Florida** | 27% |
| Florida State University | 0% |
| University of Central Florida | ~11% |
| Florida International University | 5% |
| University of West Florida | 7% |
| University of North Florida | 6% |

## Faculty Concerns

Union president Meera Sitharam characterized the implementation as:

> "Tenure's gone. It's been replaced by a five-year contract."

Faculty leadership worried that the system enables administrators to remove disfavored faculty members, effectively undermining tenure protections.

## Legislative Context

This review process emerged from 2023 Florida legislation targeting diversity, equity, and inclusion programs. The law established five-year reviews for tenured professors and gave college presidents the sole authority to fire defiant faculty, fundamentally altering traditional tenure protections.

## Significance

The UF post-tenure review demonstrates how state legislation can effectively circumvent tenure protections, creating new mechanisms for removing faculty and potentially chilling academic freedom, particularly around sensitive topics related to DEI, race, and social justice.
