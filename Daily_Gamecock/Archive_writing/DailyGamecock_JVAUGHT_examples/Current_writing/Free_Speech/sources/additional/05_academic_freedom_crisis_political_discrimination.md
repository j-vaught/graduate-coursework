# Academic Freedom in Crisis: Key Findings

Research on political discrimination and academic freedom in higher education reveals systemic issues affecting scholarly work.

## Political Discrimination & Hiring

Conservative and gender-critical scholars face substantial workplace discrimination. Over 40% of US and Canadian academics would exclude Trump supporters from hiring, while roughly one-third of British academics would reject Brexit supporters. Gender-critical feminist scholars experience even more severe discrimination, with only 28% comfortable socializing with those opposing transwomen in women's shelters.

## Self-Censorship Prevalence

The research reveals alarming levels of self-suppression:
- In social sciences and humanities, over 90% of Trump-supporting academics avoid expressing views to colleagues
- More than half of North American and British conservative academics self-censor their research and teaching
- 70% of conservative US academics report hostile departmental climates

## Hard Authoritarianism

Disciplinary threats disproportionately target right-leaning scholars. Approximately 38% of conservative North American PhD students and 43% of conservative academics report experiencing threats or actual disciplinary action. The most common source: colleagues wielding informal peer pressure and formal departmental authority.

## Cancel Culture Support (Limited but Concerning)

While only 7-18% of faculty actively support dismissal campaigns against controversial researchers, roughly 50% remain undecided—neither defending academic freedom nor opposing cancellations. This ambivalence is particularly troubling among younger academics and PhD students, with North American Millennial PhD students showing 56% support for dismissal.

## Generational Divide

Younger scholars demonstrate significantly greater intolerance. A 30-year-old far-left British academic has roughly 50% likelihood of endorsing dismissal, compared to 10% for 70-year-old non-leftists. This trajectory suggests worsening campus authoritarianism ahead.

## Implications

These findings document structural problems in academic freedom that extend beyond individual incidents, revealing systemic patterns of political discrimination and professional retaliation.
