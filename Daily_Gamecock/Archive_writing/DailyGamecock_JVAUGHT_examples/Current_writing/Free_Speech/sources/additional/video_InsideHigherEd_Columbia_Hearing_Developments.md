# Key Developments in Higher Ed Since Columbia University House Hearing

**Publication:** Inside Higher Ed
**Author:** Katherine Knott
**Date:** May 22, 2024

## Overview

Following Columbia University President Minouche Shafik's April congressional testimony on campus antisemitism, higher education experienced significant disruptions and policy developments. A third House hearing on campus antisemitism was scheduled for May 23, 2024, featuring leaders from Northwestern University, Rutgers University, and UCLA.

## Congressional Hearings & Political Pressure

### The Third Hearing

Congressional representatives planned a third hearing to examine antisemitism on college campuses, with university leaders facing intense scrutiny. Representative Virginia Foxx characterized university presidents as "spineless" for negotiating with student protesters rather than using force to disperse encampments, escalating political pressure on institutions.

### Political Interference Concerns

The hearings drew criticism from the higher education community, who viewed them as unconstitutional interference in campus affairs. Faculty organizations and university associations argued that such political interventions undermined principles of academic freedom and institutional autonomy.

## Campus Protests & Encampments

### Widespread Student Activism

Following Shafik's testimony, widespread student encampments emerged across campuses demanding university divestment from companies linked to Israel's military operations. These protests represented significant activism regarding geopolitical conflicts and university investment policies.

### Columbia's Escalation

Columbia's situation intensified when President Shafik reversed her initial negotiation stance and allowed the NYPD to clear a building occupation. This action resulted in over 100 arrests and sparked further controversy about administrative handling of student protest.

## Varied Administrative Responses

### Enforcement-Heavy Approach

Some universities, including Columbia and USC, deployed police to clear encampments and canceled graduation ceremonies. This approach prioritized removing protesters and limiting their visibility during major institutional events.

### Negotiation-Based Approach

Other institutions adopted different strategies. Northwestern and Rutgers negotiated agreements with student protesters, offering:

- Scholarships for Palestinian students
- Dedicated gathering spaces for Middle Eastern communities
- Dialogue-based approaches to address student concerns

This negotiation strategy represented an alternative to police enforcement.

## Higher Education Community Response

### Faculty & Professional Organizations

Faculty organizations and university associations criticized the hearings as inappropriate political intervention. The American Association of University Professors (AAUP) issued statements that:

- Accused politicians of attempting to dictate campus policy
- Characterized such hearings as unconstitutional interference
- Argued that political pressure was incompatible with democratic principles and academic freedom

### Institutional Autonomy Concerns

The higher education community rallied around the principle that universities should maintain autonomy in addressing campus issues without direct political pressure or direction.

## Consequences for University Leaders

### Administrative Resignations

Previous hearing participants faced significant professional consequences. University leaders who testified in earlier hearings experienced career-ending fallout:

- **Harvard's President** resigned following testimony
- **University of Pennsylvania's President** resigned following testimony

### Ongoing Challenges at Columbia

Columbia's president faced a faculty no-confidence vote despite her attempts to appease lawmakers through various policy positions, demonstrating that administrative attempts to satisfy political pressure did not necessarily resolve institutional crises.

## Key Takeaways

The period following the initial Columbia University hearing revealed several important dynamics in higher education:

1. **Political Polarization:** Congressional intervention in campus affairs created tensions between institutional autonomy and political pressure
2. **Administrative Vulnerability:** University leaders who testified faced severe professional consequences regardless of their positions
3. **Diverse Institutional Responses:** Universities adopted varied approaches to student activism, from police enforcement to negotiated agreements
4. **Institutional Defense:** The higher education community united in opposing what it viewed as unconstitutional political interference
5. **Unresolved Tensions:** Administrative attempts to satisfy political pressure did not resolve underlying campus conflicts
