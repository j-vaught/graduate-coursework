# College Leaders Face Congressional Hearing Over Antisemitism and Islamophobia

## Summary

University presidents from Harvard, MIT, and the University of Pennsylvania testified before the House Committee on Education and the Workforce regarding concerns about hate speech and discrimination on their campuses following the October 7 Hamas attack on Israel.

## Key Points

### Opening Statements

Committee Chairwoman Virginia Foxx asserted that "Institutional antisemitism and hate are among the poison fruits" of the institutions' cultures. Harvard President Claudine Gay acknowledged community members "experiencing grief, fear and trauma" while noting that Muslim and Arab students also reported hurt.

### Presidents' Responses

MIT President Sally Kornbluth stated she "abhors antisemitism" and her administration is combating it. All three leaders condemned bigotry against any group and described efforts to foster dialogue, though lawmakers pressed for specific disciplinary actions.

### Contentious Questions

Republican congresswoman Elise Stefanik asked directly about consequences for students using phrases like "from the river to the sea," which Gay said constitutes speech she finds "personally abhorrent." Gay emphasized that when speech becomes conduct violating harassment or bullying policies, disciplinary action occurs.

### Congressional Positions

Democrats cautioned against using the hearing to attack higher education and diversity initiatives. The Department of Education launched investigations into seven schools regarding antisemitism and Islamophobia complaints, with potential funding consequences.

## Context

This hearing occurred in the immediate aftermath of the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack on Israel and the subsequent Gaza conflict, which heightened tensions on college campuses and sparked debates about free speech versus institutional responsibility in addressing hate speech.
