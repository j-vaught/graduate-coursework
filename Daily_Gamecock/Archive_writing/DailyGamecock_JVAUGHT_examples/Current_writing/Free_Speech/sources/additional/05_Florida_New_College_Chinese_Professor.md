# New College of Florida Fires Chinese Professor Under SB 846

## The Incident

New College of Florida terminated Kevin Wang's contract on March 12, 2025, citing Florida's controversial "countries of concern" law (SB 846). Wang, a Chinese national seeking asylum, had taught Chinese language and culture classes for nearly two years at the Sarasota institution.

## The Law: SB 846

SB 846, effective July 1, 2023, restricts public universities from employing individuals who are not U.S. citizens or permanent residents and are domiciled in designated countries including:
- China
- Cuba
- Iran
- Russia
- Venezuela

## Wang's Background and Status

- Chinese national who fled political persecution in China
- Fled after criticizing the Chinese government
- Possessed valid work authorization in the U.S.
- Was pursuing asylum status
- Had successfully taught at New College for nearly two years

## Termination Timeline

- **March 10**: Wang inquired about unpaid paychecks
- **March 11**: Administrator discovered missing employment documentation
- **March 12**: New College terminated his contract immediately without allowing him to inform students

## Dismissal Details

Wang's termination letter explicitly stated the termination was **not for misconduct** but rather due to his immigration status and country of origin.

## Legal Challenges

The ACLU of Florida and Chinese American Legal Defense Alliance filed suit against SB 846, arguing it violates:
- Constitutional equal protection provisions
- Federal employment law

A federal magistrate recommended temporarily suspending enforcement, though the district judge had not yet ruled at the time of reporting.

## Campus Impact

Students expressed "high level of anger" about the abrupt dismissal mid-semester, raising concerns about:
- Academic freedom at the institution
- Institutional commitment to faculty rights
- Impact on faculty morale and recruitment

## Broader Implications

This case represents state-level policy directly impacting academic employment and freedom, demonstrating how legislation ostensibly aimed at national security can intersect with and threaten academic freedom.
