# America's Censored Classrooms 2024: Refining the Art of Censorship

## Overview

PEN America released this report, titled "America's Censored Classrooms 2024: Refining the Art of Censorship," unveiling a growing and sophisticated campaign to restrict academic freedom in the United States.

## Key Findings

### Legislative Activity

A total of 56 educational gag orders were filed in state legislatures in 2024, a decrease from previous years, but a higher percentage became law. Seven new gag orders became law this year, and an eighth was implemented through executive action.

### New Tactics

Legislative efforts to curtail educational speech have evolved from overt gag orders into new stealth tactics that threaten higher education across the nation. Rather than blatant bans on controversial topics, these new tactics obscure their intent to impose devastating new restrictions on higher ed by labeling them with popular causes such as promoting "institutional neutrality" or "viewpoint diversity."

### Enforcement Methods

"Jawboning," bullying and threatening university presidents into restricting their own programs without even passing a law were identified as tactics used by lawmakers.

### Higher Education Focus

For the first time, higher education tied with K-12 schools as a primary target, with eight direct classroom speech restrictions enacted and five additional bills affecting academic freedom through indirect means.

### Impact on Educators

Faculty are self-censoring to avoid legal consequences. Teachers report avoiding certain literature and topics, resulting in diminished educational breadth. As one educator noted: "The message has been loud and clear to queer families, students, and educators, we're not so welcome in our schools."

### K-12 Legislation

Thirty-five K-12 gag orders were introduced in 2024—the lowest number since tracking began—yet five became law, including restrictions on LGBTQ+ discussions and race/sex-related instruction in multiple states.

### Institutional Response

Education leaders recommend establishing "institutional safeguards" through curriculum policies and collective bargaining agreements before crises emerge, protecting academic freedom proactively.

## Future Outlook

PEN America's America's Censored Classrooms 2024 concludes with a warning: educational censorship will continue escalating into 2025.
