# University Presidents, Republican Lawmakers Spar Over Alleged Antisemitism on Campuses

## Summary

College presidents from DePaul University, Haverford College, and California Polytechnic State University faced a contentious House Education and Workforce Committee hearing regarding antisemitism on campus. This marks the ninth such hearing since October 7, 2023.

## Key Developments

### Republican Criticism

House Republican Conference Chairwoman Lisa McClain told DePaul President Robert Manuel, "Your apologies are a little hollow." Rep. Elise Stefanik criticized Haverford's lack of transparency, stating the school "still doesn't get it" and lacks specificity compared to other presidents.

### University Responses

**Cal Poly President Jeffrey Armstrong** defended his institution, asserting, "We do not tolerate threatening activity."

**Haverford President Wendy Raymond** apologized, acknowledging "we can do better."

## Democratic Perspective

Democrats challenged the committee's approach, noting the Department of Education's civil rights division faces severe staffing cuts—half its workforce eliminated—while handling 12,000 open cases. Rep. Bobby Scott questioned how the agency could fulfill responsibilities "with half the staff."

## Enforcement Threats

McClain warned that federal funding could be jeopardized, noting, "The only thing that these universities respect is the dollar."

## Broader Context

This hearing reflects the ongoing nature of Congressional scrutiny of universities on antisemitism and campus speech issues more than a year after the December 2023 hearings that led to the resignations of Harvard and Penn presidents. It demonstrates that the underlying tensions between free speech protections and institutional responsibility for addressing hate speech remain unresolved.

## Note

This article is from May 2025, making it significantly later than the initial December 2023 congressional hearings, showing how the issue has persisted as a focal point of Congressional attention.
