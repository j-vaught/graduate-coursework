# 2025: A Breakthrough Year for Free Speech on Campus

**Author:** Edward L. Yingling, Cofounder of Princetonians for Free Speech
**Published:** January 24, 2025
**Read Time:** 15 minutes

## Overview

Yingling outlines anticipated developments in campus free speech advocacy across 2025, organized by stakeholder groups and institutional trends.

## Student and Applicant Behavior

Applications are reportedly declining at prestigious universities that faced reputational damage from 2024 campus protests, while schools emphasizing free speech values are seeing increased interest. Employers have begun selective recruitment based on institutional values.

## Faculty Movements

Multiple universities—Harvard, Princeton, Duke, Columbia, MIT, and Yale—have recently established faculty councils dedicated to academic freedom. Yingling notes that "one out of three faculty members admitted to hiding their political views to avoid censorship reprisals," citing FIRE research.

## Alumni Organizing

Over thirty alumni organizations now support campus free speech, coordinated through the Alumni Free Speech Alliance. Columbia University experienced significant giving reductions, with donations dropping approximately 29% during its 2024 Giving Day.

## Legislative and Regulatory Changes

Congress may enact higher taxation on large university endowments and implement regulations addressing DEI programs. The Trump Administration's executive orders targeting diversity initiatives are expected to create substantial institutional pressure through federal funding mechanisms.

## University Institutional Changes

The piece identifies emerging trends:
- Adoption of institutional neutrality policies
- Elimination of DEI programs
- Removal of diversity statement requirements in faculty hiring
- Creation of civics-focused academic programs
