# Texas A&M Professor Melissa McCoul Firing: Academic Freedom Violation

## The Incident

Melissa McCoul, an instructor in the English department at Texas A&M, was dismissed in September 2024 after a video went viral showing a student confronting her in class and claiming the professor's gender identity lesson was illegal.

## Timeline of Events

- **September 8**: A viral video showed the classroom confrontation
- **September 9**: State Rep. Brian Harrison labeled the lesson "transgender and DEI indoctrination"
- **September 10**: Governor Greg Abbott demanded her termination
- **Hours later**: The dean and English department head were demoted, and McCoul was fired

## Faculty Panel Findings

Two faculty panels found McCoul's termination violated her academic freedom and was not justified. The university's faculty council concluded:

> "The content of the course was the reason for the dismissal and not the stated reason."

## Official Justification vs. Reality

- **University's stated reason**: Course content did not match the catalog description
- **Faculty council assessment**: This was false; the actual reason for dismissal was the course content

## Academic Freedom Violations

The faculty council determined that:
1. The dismissal violated academic freedom protections
2. The university failed to follow required dismissal procedures
3. The stated justification was pretextual

## Broader Context

This case is part of a larger pattern in Texas higher education where new laws have expanded gubernatorial and regent authority over curriculum, hiring, and discipline. Universities have launched course audits and adopted new restrictions on how race, gender, and sexuality are taught.

## Significance

The McCoul case represents a major incident in the broader academic freedom crisis in Texas, demonstrating how political pressure can override established faculty governance and tenure protections.
