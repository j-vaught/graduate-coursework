# House Education Committee Passes Free Speech Funding Bill

**Author:** Diamy Wang
**Published:** March 21, 2024

## Overview

The House Committee on Education and the Workforce advanced legislation linking federal higher education funding to campus free speech protections. Rep. Brandon Williams (R-NY) and Chair Virginia Foxx (R-NC) introduced the "Respecting the First Amendment on Campus Act" on March 15, which passed committee 24-14 along party lines.

## Key Provisions

The bill would require colleges and universities receiving Title IV federal funding to disclose policies on speech, association, and religion. It also seeks to protect student organizations, including single-sex groups and religious organizations.

## Support and Opposition

### Republican Position

Sponsors argued that "Americans' confidence in postsecondary education is at an all-time low" and cited institutions' "inconsistency in protecting the First Amendment rights." Rep. Kevin Kiley emphasized that disagreements should occur in "institutions of higher learning."

### Democratic Concerns

Rep. Kathy Manning countered that institutional neutrality would prevent universities from condemning hate speech, warning the measure "would actually protect the kind of extreme hate speech and rhetoric we condemned."

## Context

The legislation emerges amid ongoing investigations into University of Pennsylvania's handling of antisemitism, including congressional inquiries and Department of Education probes following December 2023 hearings with former President Liz Magill.
