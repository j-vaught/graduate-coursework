# FIRE to Congress, University Presidents: Don't Expand Censorship. End It.

**Author:** Nico Perrino
**Published:** December 6, 2023

## Summary

FIRE (Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression) responds to a House Committee hearing where college presidents from Harvard, Penn, and MIT faced criticism over their handling of antisemitic speech on campus. The organization argues these administrators inconsistently invoke free speech protections while maintaining vague harassment codes used to censor other viewpoints.

## Key Points

### The Hypocrisy Problem

FIRE contends that college leaders selectively apply free speech principles. When speech they oppose occurs, administrators deploy harassment codes; when sympathetic to the speech, they cite free expression commitments. "Speech codes depend for their very existence on the exercise of double standards," the article notes.

### The Solution Proposed

Rather than expanding censorship tools, FIRE advocates eliminating vague harassment policies entirely and protecting expression uniformly across all cases—"No hypocrisy. No double standards."

### Legal Standards Matter

The organization emphasizes the Supreme Court's *Davis* standard for peer-on-peer harassment: conduct must be targeted, unwelcome, and "so severe, pervasive, and objectively offensive that it effectively bars the victim's access to an educational opportunity."

### Context and Protection

FIRE acknowledges that genuinely unprotected conduct—violence, true threats, disruptive activities, and harassment meeting the *Davis* standard—warrants institutional response. However, political expression on contentious topics deserves protection.
