# Carol Harrison's Testimony on Academic Freedom Policy

Carol Harrison, chair of the ad-hoc committee on academic freedom at USC, addressed faculty concerns about protecting free speech while maintaining institutional values.

## On University Neutrality

Harrison argued that complete institutional neutrality can inadvertently support the status quo. She stated:

> "The idea that the university leadership will remain neutral in all circumstances and never speak at all has the effect of the leadership supporting those who are in power."

## On Balancing Speech Protection with Values

Harrison proposed using the Carolinian Creed as a framework for when the university might appropriately respond to problematic speech:

> "I think it is entirely possible for the university to say, 'We disapprove of the content of the speech ... We think that it violates the principles of the Carolinian Creed.'"

## On the Amendment's Purpose

Harrison emphasized that the proposed changes aim to shield USC from potential state-level restrictions on curriculum, while simultaneously establishing stronger protections for academic freedom across all campus contexts, including areas the Chicago Principles don't adequately address.

## Policy Context

The ad-hoc committee on academic freedom was formed amid state legislative efforts to restrict the teaching of critical race theory. The committee sought to expand protections in USC's faculty manual to defend all people on campus' right to free speech and expression in an educational setting.

## Significance

Harrison's testimony illustrates the debate over institutional free speech policies and the balance between protecting expression and asserting institutional values that protect dignity and equity.
