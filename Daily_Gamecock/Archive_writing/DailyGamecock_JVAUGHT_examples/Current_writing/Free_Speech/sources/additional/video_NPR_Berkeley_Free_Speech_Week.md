# Berkeley Free Speech Week Cancellation and Controversy

## Event Details
The four-day "Free Speech Week" at UC Berkeley, organized by far-right activist Milo Yiannopoulos and student publication *The Berkeley Patriot*, was canceled before its scheduled Sunday start date in September 2017.

## Why It Failed

### Logistical Collapse
Organizers could not confirm guest speakers or secure indoor campus venues. Reported speakers Steve Bannon and Ann Coulter never confirmed attendance, adding to the confusion surrounding the event.

## The Controversy

### Stated Purpose
Student organizers framed the event as a response to what they viewed as "Berkeley's efforts to shut down conservative speakers."

### Opposition Arguments
About 130 faculty members and students called for a boycott, arguing that speakers' "anti-immigrant, anti-female, anti-gay rhetoric" would make many feel unsafe and risked "uncontrollable confrontation."

### Historical Context
The controversy intensified after Berkeley canceled Yiannopoulos' February 2017 appearance due to security concerns and Ann Coulter's April 2017 speech was abruptly canceled.

## Free Speech Legal Analysis

A Santa Clara University law professor clarified that "hate speech is not considered...unprotected under the First Amendment." Prosecution requires an underlying crime—not merely the viewpoint expressed.

## Strategic Intent

According to KQED analysis, the far-right deliberately targets Berkeley to provoke reactions, acknowledging the speakers have no academic value.
