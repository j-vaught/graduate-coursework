# Senate Resolution 443: Book Banning and Freedom of Expression

## Overview

**S. Res. 443** is a resolution from the 119th Congress (2025-2026) that expresses concern regarding the growing problem of book banning and the proliferation of threats to freedom of expression in the United States. The resolution was introduced and submitted by Senator Schatz along with 18 Democratic co-sponsors, including Senators Blumenthal, Merkley, Hirono, and Padilla.

## Key Findings and Statistics

### Book Banning Data
- **PEN America Report**: Between July 2024 and June 2025, PEN America identified **6,870 instances of individual books banned**, affecting:
  - 3,751 unique titles
  - 2,589 authors, illustrators, and translators

### Geographic Distribution
States with the highest numbers of banned or restricted books:
1. **Florida**: At least 2,304 banned or restricted books
2. **Texas**: 1,781 books
3. **Tennessee**: 1,622 books

## Targeted and Banned Books

### Classic Literature Under Challenge
The resolution documents challenges and bans against widely taught literary classics, including:
- "Brave New World" by Aldous Huxley
- "The Handmaid's Tale" by Margaret Atwood
- "To Kill a Mockingbird" by Harper Lee

### Books Targeted in Federal Institutions
The resolution criticizes Executive Orders directing the removal of approximately:
- **596 books** from Department of Defense Education Activity schools
- **Nearly 400 books** from military academies (including Nimitz Library at the United States Naval Academy)

Specific examples cited include:
- "I Know Why The Caged Bird Sings" by Maya Angelou
- Works featuring LGBTQ+ characters and narratives
- Books addressing race, racism, and systemic inequities

## Disproportionate Impact

The resolution identifies patterns of targeting:
- **LGBTQ+ literature**: Disproportionate removal of works featuring LGBTQ+ characters and storylines
- **Anti-racism materials**: Books addressing racial history and contemporary racism
- **Diverse perspectives**: Works exploring gender identity and social justice

## Federal Actions Criticized

Beyond school and library bans, the resolution documents federal government actions:
- Grants administered by the **National Endowment for the Arts (NEA)** have been terminated or revoked for using language related to race, gender, and LGBTQ+ identity
- **National Endowment for the Humanities (NEH)** grants have similarly been terminated
- Executive orders directing removal of books from federal educational institutions

## Congressional Actions Called For

The resolution requests that Congress and federal agencies:
1. **Reaffirm commitment** to First Amendment protections and freedom of expression
2. **Encourage school districts** to follow professional guidelines for book selection and retention
3. **Restore books** removed from federal schools, military academies, and DoDEA institutions
4. **Repeal restrictive executive orders** that mandate book removal based on content related to race, gender, and identity

## Significance

This resolution represents Congressional concern about what supporters characterize as an unprecedented wave of book banning in the United States. It emphasizes:
- The importance of intellectual freedom and access to diverse perspectives
- The role of libraries and schools in providing unrestricted information to communities
- Constitutional protections for freedom of expression and the dangers of censorship

The timing of this resolution (119th Congress, 2025-2026) reflects ongoing national debate about book access in schools and libraries, the role of parents and administrators in determining book availability, and broader questions about free speech and intellectual freedom in American society.
