# White House Condemns University Presidents Over Antisemitism Hearing

## Summary

The White House issued a statement condemning the presidents of Harvard University, the University of Pennsylvania, and MIT after they appeared to avoid directly answering questions during a congressional hearing about whether calls for Jewish genocide violate campus conduct codes.

## Key Statements

White House spokesperson Andrew Bates declared: "Calls for genocide are monstrous and antithetical to everything we represent" and emphasized that "any statements that advocate for the systematic murder of Jews are dangerous and revolting."

## The Congressional Hearing

The House Education and the Workforce Committee questioned university leaders about rising antisemitism on campuses following Hamas's October 7 attack on Israel.

## Presidents' Responses That Sparked Backlash

**Claudine Gay (Harvard)** stated it "depends on the context" whether genocide calls violate conduct codes.

**Elizabeth Magill (Penn)** said such speech becomes actionable "if the speech becomes conduct."

**Sally Kornbluth (MIT)** said she hadn't heard such calls on campus but indicated they'd be investigated as harassment "if pervasive and severe."

## Follow-up Clarifications

Both Gay and Magill released clarifications the next day explicitly condemning genocide rhetoric as "vile" and unacceptable at their institutions. However, the initial equivocation had already generated significant backlash.

## Campus Climate Data

A recent Anti-Defamation League poll found 73% of Jewish college students experienced or witnessed antisemitic incidents during the 2023-24 school year.

## Significance

The White House condemnation marked a critical moment in which the presidents' attempts to balance free speech protections with institutional responsibility faced intense scrutiny from federal leadership, contributing to the cascade of events that would lead to calls for their resignations.
