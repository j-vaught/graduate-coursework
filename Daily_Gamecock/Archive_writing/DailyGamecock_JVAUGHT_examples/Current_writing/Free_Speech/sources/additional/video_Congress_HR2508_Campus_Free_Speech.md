# H.R. 2508 - Campus Free Speech Restoration Act

## Bill Overview

H.R. 2508, the Campus Free Speech Restoration Act, was introduced in the 118th Congress (2023-2024) to protect and restore constitutionally protected free speech rights on college and university campuses.

## Key Provisions

### Prohibition of Free Speech Zones
The bill seeks to prohibit campus free speech zones that restrict student and faculty expressive activity to limited areas of campus, ensuring that First Amendment protections apply to the entire campus environment.

### Speech Codes Limitations
The legislation limits the ability of institutions to enforce speech codes that restrict or penalize speech based on content or viewpoint, particularly those that have been used to suppress protected expression.

### Institutional Application
The bill requires both public and private institutions of higher education to comply with expressive activity protections, establishing uniform standards across different types of educational institutions.

## Legal Remedies

Persons claiming that their expressive activity rights were violated may bring an action in Federal court to:
- Enjoin a violation
- Recover compensatory damages
- Recover reasonable court costs
- Recover reasonable attorney fees

## Scope and Exceptions

The bill includes an important exception: the section does not apply to institutions of higher education whose primary purpose is the training of individuals for the military services of the United States, or the merchant marine. These institutions retain authority to impose restrictions necessary for military readiness and training.

## Legislative Context

This bill emerged from broader Congressional concerns about free speech restrictions on campuses, particularly following high-profile cases of speaker disinvitations and student protests against invited speakers. It represents an attempt to use federal legislation to ensure First Amendment compliance at institutions receiving federal funding.

## Related Legislation

Similar efforts in the 118th Congress included S. 1511, a companion Senate measure with the same title and comparable provisions addressing free speech on campuses.

## Significance

H.R. 2508 reflects ongoing debates about the balance between institutional autonomy and First Amendment protections, with proponents arguing that federal action is necessary to protect student and faculty speech rights, while critics raise concerns about federal overreach in higher education governance.
