# Texas A&M Professor Firing and Academic Freedom Concerns

## The Melissa McCoul Case

Texas A&M University fired lecturer Melissa McCoul in September 2025 after a video of her teaching about gender identity in a children's literature class went viral following circulation by a Republican state lawmaker.

## Faculty Committee Findings

The university's Committee on Academic Freedom, Responsibility and Tenure voted 8-0 on November 18, 2025 that Texas A&M "had no justification for dismissing" McCoul and "failed to follow required procedures at multiple stages." The committee unanimously rejected all three reasons Texas A&M gave for firing McCoul—that she failed to perform her duties, violated policies and acted unprofessionally.

## University's Final Decision

Despite the faculty panel's findings, Texas A&M University will not reinstate the lecturer. In a December 19 memo, the Texas A&M system's vice chancellor for academic affairs, James Hallmark, wrote that he had "determined that Dr. McCoul's dismissal was based upon good cause."

## Broader Implications

Faculty and advocacy groups have raised serious concerns that this firing threatens academic freedom at the institution. The case appears to center on tensions between institutional decisions and principles of academic freedom—the ability of scholars to conduct research and teach without fear of retaliation or censorship.

The piece reflects larger national conversations about balancing institutional authority with protections for faculty scholarship and expression, particularly in contexts where political pressure influences employment decisions.

## Pattern of Concern

This incident follows years of conservative pressure on universities to police classroom instruction, particularly regarding topics related to gender identity and sexual orientation. The firing despite a faculty committee's support raises questions about the limits of peer review protections in the current political climate.
