# After House Hearing on Campus Antisemitism, College Presidents Are Under Fire

## Summary

The article covers the fallout from a House hearing where university presidents from Penn, Harvard, and MIT faced criticism over their responses to questions about campus antisemitism policies.

## The Core Issue

When asked if "calling for the genocide of Jews" would violate campus conduct codes, the presidents gave evasive answers citing context and connection to hostile action, triggering widespread backlash.

## Sources of Criticism

Multiple voices from across the political spectrum condemned the presidents' responses:

- **Congressional Republicans** (Rep. Elise Stefanik)
- **Tech entrepreneur Elon Musk**
- **Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren**
- **Anti-Defamation League** head Jonathan Greenblatt, who stated: "It's time for new leadership. It's not just a failure of their fiduciary responsibility. It's an utter collapse" of moral duty.

## Institutional Responses

**Penn's president Liz Magill** acknowledged that genocide calls are "threatening, deeply so" and announced policy reconsideration. **Harvard's president** clarified her position but stopped short of direct connections to threats.

## Broader Context and Concerns

Harvard professor Steven Pinker noted universities face accusations of selective enforcement, allowing certain speech from the left while cracking down on other offensive speech. Congressional investigation into disciplinary policies is now underway, with complaints about absent scrutiny of Islamophobia and anti-Palestinian rhetoric.

## Implications

This hearing and its aftermath became a turning point in campus free speech debates, demonstrating how institutional attempts to balance free speech with addressing hate speech could backfire when responses are perceived as insufficiently clear or direct.
