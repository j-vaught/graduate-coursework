# Perspectives on Student Protesters and Speaker Disruptions

## Progressive Students' View

Progressive activists frame disruptions as advocacy for marginalized communities rather than censorship. At the University of Albany protest of conservative speaker Ian Haworth, organizers characterized their actions as "a demonstration of positivity, joy and support of LGBTQ+ students." They argue that harmful speech can incite violence and therefore warrant intervention.

## Free Speech Advocates' Perspective

Organizations like FIRE contend that disrupting invited speakers violates free speech principles. According to a FIRE representative, "when a speaker goes through a process to be given a forum, and then someone comes in and disrupts that, it's not free speech."

## Underlying Philosophical Divide

Research by sociology professor Jeffrey Kidder reveals a fundamental disagreement: Conservative students emphasize First Amendment protections, while progressive students view speech through a harm-reduction lens. Progressive activists believe "speech can be harmful" and therefore justifiably limited to protect vulnerable populations.

## University Administration's Challenge

Institutions struggle balancing free speech obligations with inclusion commitments. After a Stanford incident, the university apologized for not enforcing its speech policy, while UAlbany's administration attempted to reassure students it protects speech without endorsing speakers' views—a distinction some protesters found insufficient.

## Frequency and Context

Four substantial disruptions occurred in spring 2023 alone, reflecting growing tension over how campuses should handle controversial speakers. However, the actual incidence of speaker disinvitations remains relatively low compared to the volume of campus events, with research showing that most universities experience no such controversies.

## Key Distinction

This debate often becomes framed as a double standard, with critics pointing to disruptions of conservative speakers as evidence of progressive intolerance. However, researchers note that the narrative often conflates protest (protected speech) with censorship (preventing speech), and that differential treatment varies significantly by institution and political context.
