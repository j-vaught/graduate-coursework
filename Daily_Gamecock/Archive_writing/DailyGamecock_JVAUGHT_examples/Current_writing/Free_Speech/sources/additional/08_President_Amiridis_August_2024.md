# President Amiridis's August 2024 Statement on Campus Speakers and Free Speech

**Date:** August 27, 2024

## Overview

President Michael Amiridis and Board Chair Thad Westbrook issued a statement addressing concerns about a speakers' event organized by a registered student group (not university-endorsed).

## Free Speech Position

The university adheres to the **Chicago Principles**, committing to protect constitutionally protected free expression. Amiridis emphasized that:

> "The solution to fighting offensive speech is 'more speech, not enforced silence,'"

This principle references Justice Louis Brandeis's foundational free speech philosophy.

## University Values

While denouncing the speakers' "vile and juvenile rhetoric," the leadership affirmed that **USC's Carolinian Creed**—which calls students to "respect the dignity of all persons" and "discourage bigotry"—remains central to campus culture.

## Core Argument

The statement suggests that censorship amplifies extremist messages rather than diminishing them. Instead, leadership encouraged the campus community to engage through:

> "Peaceful civil discourse that generates constructive debates"

as a democratic response to offensive viewpoints.

## Official Position

The university maintained:
1. It does not endorse the event
2. It defends students' **constitutional right** to organize it
3. Democracy requires active engagement with views we oppose

## Event Context

The statement addressed concerns about an event entitled "The Roast of Cumala Harris," hosted by alt-right commentators and activists Milo Yiannopoulos and Gavin McInnes.

## Significance

Amiridis's statement represents USC's institutional commitment to free speech principles while balancing concerns about offensive speech, demonstrating how universities attempt to navigate tensions between protecting expression and upholding campus values of respect and dignity.

## Relationship to Later DOJ Letter

This August 2024 statement provides important context for the September 2025 DOJ letter concerning USC's outdoor event policies, showing the university's formal commitment to free speech principles and the Chicago Principles.
