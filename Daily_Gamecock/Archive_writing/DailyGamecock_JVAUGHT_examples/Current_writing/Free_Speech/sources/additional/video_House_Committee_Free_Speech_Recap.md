# House Committee on Education & the Workforce: Free Speech on Campus Hearing Recap

## Overview

On March 30, 2023, the Higher Education and Workforce Development Subcommittee held a hearing examining the degradation of constitutionally protected free speech on college campuses, with Republican members emphasizing their duty to uphold First Amendment rights.

## Key Witness Testimony

### Ilya Shapiro
Manhattan Institute Director of Constitutional Studies described being prevented from speaking at UC Hastings College of Law, calling it "the first time I'd ever been protested in more than a thousand" events and characterizing it as a "damning indictment of the state of academia."

### Josiah Joner
Stanford Review Executive Editor framed free speech as nonpartisan: "This is the issue at the core of what defines our society...through healthy dialogue and the free exchange of ideas."

## Republican Concerns

Members highlighted two primary issues:

1. **Student-level censorship**: Documented cases of speakers being shouted down and disinvited for expressing differing viewpoints

2. **Institutional DEI programs**: Rep. Jim Banks questioned how federal funding—approximately $190 billion annually—could be leveraged to prevent universities from implementing programs that "directly attack people of faith"

## Republican Position

Subcommittee Chairman Burgess Owens stated the committee should "explore possible legislative avenues" to remind universities of their accountability as recipients of taxpayer funding, emphasizing that restoring First Amendment principles on campuses remains a priority.

## Context

This hearing occurred amid broader Congressional focus on free speech issues on university campuses, particularly following incidents of speaker disinvitations and protests against invited speakers across multiple institutions. The committee's emphasis on federal funding leverage reflected an emerging legislative strategy to address free speech concerns at federally-funded institutions.
