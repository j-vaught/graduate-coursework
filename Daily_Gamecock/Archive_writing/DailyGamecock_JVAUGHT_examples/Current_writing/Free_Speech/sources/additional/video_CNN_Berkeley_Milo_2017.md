# Berkeley Cancels Milo Yiannopoulos Talk After Violent Protests

## Overview
Violent protests erupted on the UC Berkeley campus on February 1, 2017, forcing university officials to cancel a scheduled speech by right-wing commentator Milo Yiannopoulos. The incident sparked national debate about free speech, campus security, and protest tactics.

## The Incident

### Violence and Property Damage
Protests that erupted at UC Berkeley ahead of the planned appearance by Yiannopoulos caused $100,000 worth of damage to the campus, according to the university. Black-clad protesters wearing masks threw commercial-grade fireworks and rocks at police, with some even hurling Molotov cocktails that ignited fires. They also smashed windows of the student union center on the Berkeley campus where the Yiannopoulos event was to be held.

### Scale of Unrest
The university blamed "150 masked agitators" for the unrest, saying they had come to campus to disturb an otherwise peaceful protest. At least six people were injured in the violence.

### Event Cancellation
Administrators decided to cancel the Wednesday event about two hours before the Breitbart editor's speech. UC Berkeley said it removed him from campus "amid the violence and destruction of property and out of concern for public safety."

## Political Response
President Donald Trump weighed in on Twitter, suggesting the possibility of cutting federal funds to the public university, tweeting: "If U.C. Berkeley does not allow free speech and practices violence on innocent people with a different point of view – NO FEDERAL FUNDS?"

## Context
This incident at UC Berkeley—the birthplace of the Free Speech Movement in the 1960s—became highly symbolic in national debates about political expression and campus activism in 2017.
