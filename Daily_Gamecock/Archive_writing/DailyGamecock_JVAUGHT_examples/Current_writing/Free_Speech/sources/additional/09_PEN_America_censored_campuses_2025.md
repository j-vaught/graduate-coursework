# Higher Education Censorship Crisis: 2025 Legislative Surge

## Unprecedented Legislative Assault

In 2025, state legislators introduced 93 bills across 32 states designed to censor higher education. This represents a significant escalation in efforts to restrict academic freedom and control educational content.

## Key Statistics

- **21 bills enacted** across 15 states: Arkansas, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, North Dakota, Ohio, Texas, Utah, West Virginia, and Wyoming
- **All legislatures** that passed censorship bills were Republican-controlled
- **More than half** of U.S. college students now study in states with at least one law or policy restricting what can be taught or how campuses can operate
- **78 indirect censorship bills** were introduced nationally, with 20 enacted into law

## Types of Censorship

**Direct Censorship (Educational Gag Orders):**
- 14 of the 21 enacted bills contained gag orders
- 7 specifically targeted higher education
- Include restrictions on teaching about race, gender, American history, and LGBTQ+ identities
- Examples: Texas Tech rules banning transgender topic instruction; Texas A&M restrictions on race or gender instruction

**Indirect Censorship:**
- State laws and policies that attack institutions enabling academic freedom
- Include curriculum restrictions, tenure limitations, institutional neutrality mandates, DEI bans, and governance restrictions
- Indirectly chill the climate for free speech by undermining faculty autonomy

## Federal Threats (2025)

- 19 executive orders issued targeting or affecting higher education
- 90+ Title VI investigations launched by Trump administration
- $3.7 billion in research funding targeted
- More than 8,000 international student visas revoked
- 17% drop in new international enrollment
- 57% of institutions reporting enrollment declines

## Notable State Laws

**Ohio SB 1:**
- Institutional neutrality requirements
- Bans on diversity initiatives
- Restrictions on faculty governance

**Indiana HB 1001:**
- Faculty senate limitations
- Reduces faculty involvement in institutional decision-making

**Texas Tech Rules:**
- Specifically bans instruction on transgender topics

**Texas A&M:**
- Restricts teaching about race and gender

## Impact Assessment

The report emphasizes that this represents an unprecedented wave of legislative censorship targeting higher education. More than half of American college students now study under at least one restrictive law or policy, fundamentally altering the landscape of academic freedom in the United States.

The combination of direct and indirect censorship creates a comprehensive system of control that extends beyond specific topics to the structural conditions necessary for academic freedom to flourish.
