# Key Findings on Faculty Free Speech Concerns

FIRE's 2024 survey of 6,269 faculty reveals widespread self-censorship in academia:

## Self-Censorship Rates

- 35% have recently toned down their writing due to controversy concerns (compared to 9% during McCarthyism)
- 27% feel unable to speak freely due to student, administrator, or colleague responses
- 42% likely self-censor in classroom discussions

## Political Disparities

Faculty with conservative views experience disproportionate pressure. While 17% of liberal faculty hide political views to protect jobs, "a staggering 55% of conservative faculty who say the same."

## Difficult Topics

Approximately 70% struggle discussing the Israeli-Palestinian conflict openly; 51% avoid racial inequality discussions; 49% hesitate on transgender rights.

## Discipline and Fear

- 14% faced discipline or threats for teaching, research, or speech
- 23% worry about job loss from misunderstood statements
- Only 27% believe academic freedom is secure on their campuses

## Institutional Concerns

Faculty expressed skepticism about administrative support. One-third lack clarity on whether administrations protect free speech, and 28% doubt administrators would defend controversial speakers.

## Key Implication

The data documents that a chilling effect has produced concerning outcomes, with faculty aware of more incidents targeting colleagues being more worried about facing repercussions for expressing their own political views, and one-fifth of faculty saying that they thought their colleagues were less willing than they were six or seven years ago to express unpopular political views on campus.
