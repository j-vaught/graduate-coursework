# DOJ Letter to USC on Free Speech and Outdoor Events

## Key Details

**The Allegation:**
The U.S. Department of Justice's Civil Rights Division sent a letter to the University of South Carolina alleging that administrators had decided to ban "controversial" outdoor events for the remainder of the semester following Charlie Kirk's death.

**What the DOJ Claimed:**
According to the letter, USC President Michael Amiridis told student leaders from campus chapters of Turning Point USA and Uncensored America that controversial speakers and activities would "no longer happen outside for the rest of the semester."

**First Amendment Concern:**
Assistant U.S. Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon stated that such a policy would violate the First Amendment, as administrators labeled Kirk as a "known disrupter to a population" without providing clear definitions of what constituted "controversial."

**State Response:**
South Carolina Attorney General Alan Wilson responded by calling on universities to ensure free speech protections on campuses, emphasizing that "Limiting free speech is unconstitutional." Wilson reported speaking directly with USC's president, who assured him that no policies limiting free speech would be implemented.

**Dhillon's Statement:**
The Assistant AG urged the university to eliminate any unconstitutional policies and reaffirm support for outdoor expressive activities to promote "the peaceful exchange of ideas Charlie Kirk championed."

## Significance for Free Speech Debate

This incident demonstrates federal-level scrutiny of university free speech policies and the tension between security concerns and First Amendment protections, particularly around controversial speakers on college campuses.
