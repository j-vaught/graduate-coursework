# Harvard's Free Speech Crisis: FIRE's Assessment

## The Rankings Collapse

Harvard has experienced a dramatic decline in FIRE's College Free Speech Rankings over four years, culminating in a historic low. The university ranked 46th in 2020, then 130th in 2021, 170th in 2022, and finally dead last in 2023 with a score of 0.00—the worst ever recorded. Harvard's actual score was -10.69, more than six standard deviations below the average and more than two standard deviations below the second-to-last school.

## Key Problems Identified

**Deplatforming pattern**: Harvard sanctioned seven of nine individuals targeted for protected speech over five years, including terminating three scholars and revoking a conservative student's admission over social media comments.

**Student self-censorship**: The percentage of Harvard students reporting frequent self-censorship increased from 16% two years prior to 24% by 2023. Only about 27% feel comfortable publicly disagreeing with professors on controversial topics.

**Policy concerns**: Harvard maintains vague speech policies rated with a "yellow light," including a requirement that students be "civil" when using computers and networks.

## FIRE's Core Message

According to FIRE, Harvard cannot solve this through policy changes alone. The organization emphasizes that the university must "consistently walk the walk by enforcing pro-speech policies" and shift campus culture from fear-based conformity to one encouraging open discourse and intellectual challenge.

## Recent Improvement

In 2025, Harvard moved up in score from 0.00 to 49.74 out of 100, earning a new rank of 245 out of 257. The climb out of last place was the result of fewer penalties and a "high honors" bonus after President Alan Garber publicly rebuffed federal demands to derecognize several student groups.

## Broader Context

This metric highlights how free speech concerns—particularly regarding deplatforming of conservative speakers and viewpoints—have become a significant measurement tool for assessing campus climates, even as critics question the validity and methodology of such rankings.
