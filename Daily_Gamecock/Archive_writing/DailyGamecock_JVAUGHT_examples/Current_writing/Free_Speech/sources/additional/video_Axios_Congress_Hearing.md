# University Leaders Hammered After Congressional Hearing on Antisemitism

## Summary

Harvard, MIT, and University of Pennsylvania presidents faced bipartisan criticism following their congressional testimony on campus antisemitism. Their responses to questions about whether calls for Jewish genocide violated institutional conduct codes sparked significant backlash and calls for their resignations.

## The Hearing and Key Questions

On December 5, 2023, Rep. Elise Stefanik posed a direct yes/no question: Does "calling for the genocide of Jews" violate your schools' codes of conduct?

The presidents' answers—citing context specificity and the need for speech to become conduct—were widely criticized as evasive and insufficient.

## Immediate Fallout

The hearing generated immediate and widespread backlash from:
- Republican members of Congress
- Technology entrepreneurs
- Democratic senators
- Civil rights organizations

## Consequences

The hearing precipitated major institutional changes:
- **Harvard President Claudine Gay** resigned on December 28, 2023, less than a month after the hearing
- **University of Pennsylvania President Liz Magill** also resigned in the aftermath
- **MIT President Sally Kornbluth** retained her position with support from MIT's academic leadership

## Broader Implications

These resignations marked a significant moment in debates over:
- Free speech protections in higher education institutions
- Institutional responsibility for addressing hate speech
- The limits of academic freedom arguments when applied to calls for genocide
- The power of Congressional pressure and public opinion on university governance

## Timeline Context

This hearing became a watershed moment in post-October 7, 2023 campus discourse, demonstrating the intense political pressure universities faced in addressing antisemitism while navigating free speech considerations.
