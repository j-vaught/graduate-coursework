# America's Censored Classrooms 2023

## Overview

"America's Censored Classrooms 2023" catalogues more than 110 bills introduced during the 2023 state legislative sessions that restrict teaching about topics such as race, gender, American history and LGBTQ identities in educational settings.

## Key Findings

### Bills and Laws

During the 2023 state legislative sessions, 110 bills that PEN America defines as educational gag orders were introduced, and 10 became law. Four more gag orders were imposed via executive order or state or system regulation: two in Florida, and one each in Arkansas and California. These developments bring the number of educational gag orders that have become law or policy to a total of 40 across 21 states as of November 1, 2023.

### Shift in Tactics

Attempts to censor faculty directly faced stiff opposition from the courts and academic groups that uphold academic freedom in public colleges and universities. Instead, legislators began targeting aspects of this support system, including faculty unions, governing boards, higher education accreditors and other structures in order to "erode" academic freedom.

### Changing Focus

The PEN America report pointed to a shift away from proposing restrictions on speech about race and racism and more towards restrictions on speech about sexual orientation and gender identity.

### Florida as Blueprint

Florida has served as a blueprint here, with SB 266, which the report called "2023's most censorious gag order." The bill prohibits core courses from teaching ideas related to identity politics and systemic racism.

### Educator Concerns

The report documents educator concerns about the chilling effect these laws create, with educators leaving their schools and seeking employment in other states.

## Broader Context

The 2023 report demonstrated the intensifying legislative campaign to restrict curriculum and suppress discussions of race, gender, sexual orientation, and identity in American schools.
