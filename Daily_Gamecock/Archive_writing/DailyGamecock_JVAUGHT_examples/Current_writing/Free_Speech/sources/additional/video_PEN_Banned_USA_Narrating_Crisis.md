# Banned in the USA: Narrating the Crisis

## Report Overview

PEN America's report, "Banned in the USA: Narrating the Crisis," documents a dramatic surge in school book censorship. During the first half of the 2023-2024 school year (July-December 2023), researchers identified 4,349 individual book bans across 23 states and 52 public school districts—exceeding the entire previous year's total of 3,362 bans.

## Key Findings

### Scale of Censorship

From July 2023 to December 2023, PEN America recorded 4,349 instances of book bans across 23 states and 52 public school districts. PEN America recorded more school book bans during the first six months of the 2023-24 school year than in all of 2022-23.

PEN America has documented over 10,000 bans over the last two and a half years (July 2021 to December 2023).

### Geographic Leaders in Censorship

Florida experienced the highest number of ban cases, with 3,135 bans across 11 school districts. Over 1,600 of those book bans took place in Escambia County Public Schools, the district with the most bans nationwide.

Other states with significant banning activity include:
- Wisconsin (481 bans)
- Iowa (142 bans)
- Texas (141 bans)

### Acceleration Trends

Book banning activity is accelerating year-over-year, indicating a worsening crisis in American schools.

## Common Targeting Patterns

Sabrina Baêta, the report's lead author, notes that "book bans are targeting narratives about race and sexual identities and sexual content writ large." Challengers frequently employ claims about "obscenity" and inflammatory language regarding "porn in schools" to justify removing materials addressing sexual violence and LGBTQ+ themes, while simultaneously attacking books exploring racial topics through rhetoric about "critical race theory" and "woke ideology."

### Content Under Attack

The report examines a wave of intense scrutiny over books that:
- Discuss women and sexual violence
- Address sexual assault and rape
- Feature trans narratives and trans authors
- Explore racial justice and identity
- Present diverse perspectives from marginalized communities

Book bans targeting trans narratives and trans authors, alongside restrictive legislation and aggressive political rhetoric, contribute to a school environment that is antagonistic toward trans students.

## Grassroots Resistance

Despite the crisis, PEN America identified growing grassroots resistance. A broad coalition of educators, librarians, parents, authors, and advocates are organizing in ways large and small to protect the freedom to read.

Across the country:
- Students are staging protests and read-ins
- Young people are founding after-school banned book clubs
- Teachers are distributing books under the radar
- Communities are creating funds to purchase new books for districts impacted by bans
- Activists are installing free community bookshelves throughout their towns

## Conclusion

The report signals both the severity of the censorship crisis and the emergence of a countermovement defending reading freedom and intellectual access in American schools.
