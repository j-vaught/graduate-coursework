# South Carolina REACH Act: Higher Education Requirements

The Reinforcing College Education on America's Constitutional Heritage (REACH Act) enacted in 2021 establishes specific curriculum mandates for South Carolina's public universities and colleges.

## Core Requirements

Public institutions of higher learning must require undergraduate students to complete "no fewer than three semester credit hours or their equivalent in American history, American government, or another equivalent course" that covers major events and turning points in U.S. history and government.

## Required Course Content

The designated course must include study of:
- The complete U.S. Constitution
- The full Declaration of Independence
- The entire Emancipation Proclamation
- A minimum of five complete essays from the Federalist Papers
- Documents foundational to the African American Freedom struggle

## Key Provisions

**Degree Requirement**: Universities cannot award baccalaureate degrees to students who fail to complete this instruction.

**Exemptions**: Students who earned three credit hours in Advanced Placement, International Baccalaureate, or dual-credit American history/government courses with passing grades may be exempt, provided those courses meet the specified content requirements.

**Implementation**: Institutions must incorporate these requirements into existing degree programs "without adding to the total number of credit hours" and without conflicting with accreditation standards.

**Oversight**: The Commission on Higher Education must annually verify compliance and report findings to legislative committees.

**Effective Date**: The requirements became effective for students entering in the 2021-2022 academic year.

## Context for Free Speech Analysis

While the REACH Act itself is a curriculum mandate rather than a free speech restriction, it represents a form of legislative direction of academic content that some argue demonstrates government intervention in educational content decisions—relevant to debates about academic freedom and institutional autonomy.
