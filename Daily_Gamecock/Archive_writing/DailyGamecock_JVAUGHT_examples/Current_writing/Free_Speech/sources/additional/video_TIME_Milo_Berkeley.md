# Milo Yiannopoulos at Berkeley: Free Speech Week Summary

## Event Overview
Milo Yiannopoulos finally appeared at UC Berkeley on September 24, 2017, though the controversial "Free Speech Week" was largely unsuccessful, with nearly all scheduled speeches canceled.

## Background
The controversy began in February when anti-fascist protesters disrupted Yiannopoulos's initial planned speech. The former Breitbart employee vowed to return, making the appearance central to the university's free speech initiative.

## The Actual Event

### Security and Restrictions
Security was intense, with "scores of police officers and barricades surrounding" Sproul Plaza. Attendees faced restrictions—no cameras or bags allowed.

### Limited Impact
When Yiannopoulos appeared at noon, only dozens of people remained in the cordoned area. The university prohibited amplified sound, severely limiting his ability to address the crowd. He primarily "signed autographs and posed for selfies" before police escorted him away roughly 20 minutes later.

## Protest Response
Hundreds gathered outside to protest, with competing messages on signs: "Milo Has a Right 2 Speak" versus "Shut Down Milo." Supporters wore MAGA hats; opponents chanted "Nazi scum off our streets!"

## Student Perspectives
Students expressed frustration over the event's ineffectiveness and resource allocation. One questioned what purpose it served if Yiannopoulos couldn't actually communicate his views to the audience.
