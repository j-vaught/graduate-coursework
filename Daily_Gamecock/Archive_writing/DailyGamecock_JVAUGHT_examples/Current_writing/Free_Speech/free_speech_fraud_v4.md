# Column: Free speech has become "My Speech" at USC

On September 18, 2024, Milo Yiannopoulos and Proud Boys founder Gavin McInnes spoke to a sparse audience behind metal barricades at the University of South Carolina's Russell House. Uncensored America — a group that describes itself as ["a nonpartisan, nonprofit organization dedicated to fighting for freedom of speech"](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/09/column-opinion-the-uncensored-america-should-not-be-taken-seriously-opinion-dunn) — had its $3,576.99 funding request [rejected 27-10](https://www.wistv.com/2024/09/13/usc-student-government-denies-funding-scheduled-roast-vice-president-kamala-harris/) by the Student Senate. Conservative media called it censorship. [FIRE agreed](https://www.thefire.org/news/kamala-harris-comedy-roast-denied-funding-university-south-carolina-student-senate). And technically, they were right — the senate's vote was viewpoint discrimination.

But here is what the conservative free speech movement will never say about itself: according to FIRE's own [Scholars Under Fire database](https://www.thefire.org/research-learn/scholars-under-fire), over 75 percent of incidents targeting scholars for their speech now come from the political right. Not the left. Not the so-called campus cancel culture warriors. The right. The same political coalition that rallied behind Uncensored America has enacted [more than 70 bills and policies across 26 states](https://pen.org/report/americas-censored-campuses-25-web-of-control/) since 2021 restricting what professors can teach, which books students can read, and which perspectives universities can value in hiring. These people do not believe in free speech. They believe in their speech.

## The Numbers Don't Lie

Since 2021, [PEN America](https://pen.org/press-release/pen-america-warns-of-expanding-web-of-control-as-politicians-escalate-campaign-to-censor-u-s-colleges-and-universities/) has tracked an unprecedented legislative assault on higher education. In 2025 alone, [21 censorship bills became law in 15 states](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/faculty-issues/curriculum/2026/01/15/report-state-lawmakers-enacted-21-censorship-bills-2025) — the highest single-year total ever recorded. Every single one of those legislatures was Republican-controlled. Over half of American college students now attend institutions in states with at least one such restriction on the books — including every student at USC.

The assault extends to libraries. PEN America documented [6,870 book bans](https://pen.org/book-bans/pen-america-index-of-school-book-bans-2024-2025/) in the 2024-2025 school year. The [American Library Association reports](https://www.ala.org/bbooks/book-ban-data) that 72 percent of censorship demands originate from organized conservative movements, elected officials, and school board members. Forty-seven percent of challenged books feature LGBTQ+ or BIPOC voices. These are the same political movements that scream "free speech" when a student senate votes against funding their campus event.

And FIRE's [faculty survey](https://www.thefire.org/facultyreport) paints the picture on the ground: 55 percent of conservative faculty say they hide their political views to protect their jobs, compared to 17 percent of liberal faculty. Thirty-five percent of all faculty have toned down their writing to avoid controversy — compared to just 9 percent during the McCarthy era. The chill is real. But it is not coming from the direction they claim.

## The TPUSA Contradiction

When Turning Point USA stopped at USC on its "Campus Clash" tour, the Russell House ballroom was packed and people were standing against the back wall. Audience questions were met with patronizing, hostile responses from the stage. This is the organization that claims it should be able to continue its rhetoric under the guise of free speech — while maintaining a [Professor Watchlist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Watchlist), a publicly searchable database targeting over 300 academics. PEN America [described the Watchlist](https://www.thefire.org/news/dual-purpose-tpusa-watchlist-warrants-two-toned-response) as "a noxious purveyor of precisely what it claims to deride: the intimidation and ostracization of those who express controversial views on campus."

The consequences are not hypothetical. At the University of Montana, history professor Tobin Miller Shearer received death threats after being placed on the list and was forced to [move his class to a secret location](https://www.chronicle.com/article/charlie-kirks-watch-list-made-some-professors-lives-a-living-hell) with campus police protection. At Diablo Valley College, political science professor Albert Ponce had a lecture on white supremacy edited into a two-minute clip and circulated through Fox News and Breitbart. He received threats against himself and his nine-year-old daughter. Family photos were posted online. He told his daughter not to open the mail. Boston University's Saida Grundy, listed for a tweet, described the experience as "the greatest stress of my life, more than stage-three cancer." Drexel law professor David S. Cohen, listed for pro-choice scholarship, put it plainly: "He was being so kind and thoughtful to highlight our work for his rabid, highly-armed rightwing extremist audience."

At Arizona State, TPUSA employees [physically assaulted](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2017/12/19/arizona-state-student-events-official-assaulted-political-group) a professor. At Ohio State, professor Treva B. Lindsey received a [surge of messages](https://matternews.org/community/targeted-ohio-state-professors-on-the-damaging-impact-of-turning-points-professor-watchlist/) with "racist language and threats of sexual violence." Professor Pranav Jani, after 22 years of tenure, described sustained harassment with violent messages threatening him and his family.

An organization cannot coherently demand that universities platform speakers while simultaneously running a database designed to intimidate professors into silence. That is not a free speech position. That is a "my speech" position.

## The Speakers USC Hosted

These are the men Uncensored America chose to bring to our campus. Yiannopoulos built his career on being aggravating — his controversies include [appearing to defend pedophilia](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2017/feb/21/milo-yiannopoulos-resigns-breitbart-pedophilia-comments) and provoking riots at [UC Berkeley](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-38837142) that caused [$100,000 in damages](https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/grade-point/wp/2017/02/01/protests-at-uc-berkeley-prompted-by-milo-yiannopoulos/). At Penn State, an Uncensored America event featuring McInnes was [canceled after protests turned violent](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/10/guest-column-free-speech-events-deserve-funding-opinion-connors). USC avoided that outcome, but the event still required metal barricades, USC Police, and SLED. Student Emily Whitaker [noted](https://www.wltx.com/article/news/local/controversial-event-usc/101-2c3eebfb-b310-4f96-bf66-6d098dc6b1f3) she had not seen that level of security for other events with opposing views, like a drag show during the first week of classes.

McInnes publicly preaches that "sunlight is the best disinfectant" — yet when the Southern Poverty Law Center labeled the Proud Boys a hate group, he [filed a defamation lawsuit](https://www.techdirt.com/2019/07/11/splc-asks-court-to-toss-proud-boy-founders-defamation-lawsuit-asking-wheres-lie/) to silence them. The flagship speakers of the free speech movement sue their critics when criticized. That tells you everything.

## South Carolina's Turn

Republican politicians have constantly tried to interfere with academic operations at South Carolina's universities. In 2021, Gov. Henry McMaster signed the REACH Act, mandating what aspects of American history universities must teach. The South Carolina Commission on Higher Education then requested section-specific syllabuses from USC, making it easy for anyone to identify exactly what individual professors were teaching.

USC political science department chair Kirk Randazzo described the fear: "not only that we're on a potentially slippery slope that may violate academic freedom for faculty members, but my greater concern is that by simply monitoring things, the state is potentially creating a chilling effect that might influence or impact how faculty teach material." USC history professor Carol Harrison was more direct: "The chill is already here."

An [open letter](https://www.postandcourier.com/politics/sc-gop-delegation-tells-usc-clemson-to-eradicate-critical-race-theory/article_85e96e5e-d53f-11eb-9e0b-7f0e925f3c53.html) from Republican members of South Carolina's Congressional delegation called on university presidents to "eradicate" any teaching of critical race theory — and identified a USC professor by name. The 2025-2026 session brought a flood of new bills: [HB 3184](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/3184.htm) banning DEI in admissions and employment, [SB 368](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/368.htm) prohibiting DEI offices statewide, and the [FORUM Act](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/4749.htm) designating campus outdoor areas as public forums.

The contradiction is plain: South Carolina legislators want to protect the speech of campus provocateurs while empowering the state to dictate what professors can teach. Free speech for the demonstrator. Government control over the classroom.

## The University Is Not Innocent

Here is where I turn on USC itself. The university claims viewpoint neutrality while making content-based decisions about whose speech gets amplified. After the Student Senate [denied Uncensored America's funding](https://www.thefire.org/news/kamala-harris-comedy-roast-denied-funding-university-south-carolina-student-senate) — a clear First Amendment violation — the administration reversed it under legal pressure. Fine. But that same night, [over 1,000 students gathered at Blatt Field](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/09/over-1000-students-attend-blatt-bonanza-during-controversial-campus-event-news-grady) for "Blatt Bonanza" — free food, bouncy houses, a rage room, and selfies with the university president — while Yiannopoulos spoke to empty chairs at Russell House. SG President Patton Byars [insisted](https://wach.com/news/local/usc-students-seek-safe-space-at-usc-event-during-controversial-political-roast): "This is the event on our campus. It's not an alternative or counter event." Nobody believed that.

President Amiridis condemned the ["vile and juvenile rhetoric"](https://www.statehousereport.com/2024/09/06/more-news-usc-gets-high-marks-for-free-speech-as-controversy-roils-campus/) used to promote the event — while defending free speech. Then he attended Blatt Bonanza, taking selfies with students at the counter-event. The president's feet revealed his position more clearly than his words.

One year later, the DOJ [sent a letter](https://www.postandcourier.com/education-lab/usc-doj-charlie-kirk-outdoor-event-controversial/article_c1d6ca0b-1173-403d-bd16-6f8fec66f748.html) citing "troubling allegations" that USC administrators told conservative student groups that "controversial speakers and activities will no longer happen outside." Administrators had labeled the late Charlie Kirk a ["known disrupter to a population"](https://abcnews4.com/news/state/assistant-ag-demands-usc-continue-outdoor-debates-september-2025-columbia) — without defining either term. USC denied any such policy existed. If that is true, why did the DOJ need to send a letter?

The deeper question is why the university funds these organizations at all. Student activity fees — money every student at USC pays — subsidize events featuring men with ties to hate groups and histories of defending pedophilia. The university hides behind viewpoint neutrality as a legal shield, but viewpoint neutrality is not a moral argument. There is no constitutional obligation for a university to hand student money to anyone who asks. USC adopted the [Chicago Principles](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/student_affairs/our_news_and_publications/news/2024/usc_commitment_to_free_speech.php) in June 2023, which state that "it is not the proper role of the University to attempt to shield individuals from ideas and opinions they find unwelcome, disagreeable, or even deeply offensive." FIRE rewarded the move, rocketing USC from [246th to 22nd](https://www.thefire.org/news/tale-two-universities-why-south-carolina-soared-and-harvard-didnt-fires-free-speech-rankings) in its national rankings. But a high ranking and a principled institution are not the same thing.

If the cost of defunding organizations like Uncensored America is also losing funding for progressive groups, that might be a trade worth making. I would rather lose student-fee-funded liberal advocacy than continue handing platforms to people whose primary contribution to public discourse is making a professor's nine-year-old daughter afraid to open the mail. Bouncy houses and counter-events are not a free speech policy. They are an admission that the university knows exactly what it is platforming and would rather distract from it than confront it.

## What About the Left?

Yes, progressive students are hypocrites too. At USC, [27,000 people signed a petition](https://www.postandcourier.com/education-lab/usc-doj-charlie-kirk-outdoor-event-controversial/article_c1d6ca0b-1173-403d-bd16-6f8fec66f748.html) to cancel the McInnes-Yiannopoulos event. Months later, many of the same voices decried the arrest of [pro-Palestinian demonstrators](https://www.fitsnews.com/2024/04/24/pro-palestine-protesters-at-the-university-of-south-carolina/) as suppression of protected expression. That is a contradiction.

But it is not an equivalence. The progressive contradiction involves petitions and protests — campus theater. The conservative contradiction involves legislation, state power, professors fired, tenure gutted, and entire fields of study banned by law. FIRE's own data confirms this: while students and peers more frequently target scholars from the left, politicians and activists are overwhelmingly responsible for targeting from the right. One side writes petitions. The other side writes laws.

## The One Question

The next time Turning Point USA or Uncensored America invokes the First Amendment at USC, ask one question: Would you apply this principle to the professors being fired in [Texas](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/09/15/texas-am-professor-firing-melissa-mccoul-academic-freedom/) and [Florida](https://www.wusf.org/education/2025-03-28/new-college-fires-chinese-professor-florida-countries-of-concern-law) for teaching concepts you oppose?

If they would, they should say so — loudly, publicly, and to the state legislators who fund their movement. They should condemn the Professor Watchlist. They should oppose the REACH Act's syllabi surveillance. They should fight HB 3184 and SB 368 with the same energy they brought to a $3,577 funding request for a roast.

If they wouldn't, they should stop pretending. Intellectual pursuits are not furthered by hiding undesirable voices, and crude antagonism is only defeated when countered with genuine depth of knowledge. But the answer to repugnant speech is greater speech — not 21 new laws in 15 states telling professors what they cannot say. The loudest voices for free speech on this campus belong to a movement that is, right now, in state legislatures across the country, doing more to destroy it than any student petition or bouncy house ever could.
