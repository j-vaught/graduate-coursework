On September 18, 2024, right-wing activists, Gavin McInnes and Milo Yiannopoulos, spoke to a room with empty chairs in the Russell House. Metal barricades caged the stage and SLED officers stood guard - some even say there were roof snipers scoping the event. 

The invoice for this amazing and wonderful event went to students.  

Through mandatory activity fees, USC charged the student body $3,576.99 — not for textbooks or tutoring, but for barricades, security details and alleged snipers protecting speakers thousands of students had petitioned against.

Uncensored America called it a free speech victory. After all, why wouldn't they? They had sued, generated headlines and forced the university to bankroll their spectacle. When the Student Senate voted to deny funding, conservative media called it censorship. FIRE called the denial "a clear violation of the First Amendment."

But after the dust settled, nobody connected the dots. The funding denial was not a setback. It was the strategy. The lawsuit generated headlines. The headlines generated pressure. The pressure generated a spectacle — barricades, snipers and empty chairs — that would play on loop as evidence of persecution. 

The free speech movement had learned what the student senate never did. Sometimes losing the vote is how you win the war.

But the movement's commitment to open discourse ends where its own critics begin. When criticized, the same men demanding platforms rush to sue their detractors into silence.

In 2019, long before coming to USC, McInnes filed a defamation suit against the Southern Poverty Law Center for labeling the Proud Boys a hate group. He invokes the First Amendment to preach violence, yet when others use their speech to represent him accurately, he sues.

McInnes uses courts to silence his critics. Turning Point USA uses a database. Its Professor Watchlist catalogs over 300 academics — what Charlie Kirk called a resource to help parents "decide" on colleges. The professors call it a target list. Since their names appeared, they have received death threats, some going even going as far as having to warn their children not to open the mail.

Clearly, the free speech movement does not want speech. It wants _their_ speech, subsidized and protected, while opponents face state power.

Progressive students circulated petitions to cancel the USC event and these same people later protested arrests of pro-Palestinian demonstrators. That is hypocrisy - the instinct to restrict speech you hate and demand protection for speech you like, and it deserves to be called out. But it is not the same kind of power. Petitions do not rewrite policy, and students do not control a legislature. When the debate moves from campus outrage to statewide enforcement, the right is the side with the machinery.

Since 2021, Republicans have enacted 70 bills restricting classroom speech across 26 states. They banned books featuring LGBTQ+ characters. They mandated syllabus surveillance through South Carolina's REACH Act. They named specific USC professors in letters demanding their firing.

One side writes petitions. The other writes laws, hires police protection for provocateurs, and forces students to fund both.

President Michael Amiridis condemned the "vile and juvenile rhetoric" while defending the event. Then he attended Blatt Bonanza, the bouncy-house festival designed to distract from the barricades. He took selfies while Yiannopoulos spoke to empty seats.

The administration reversed the Student Senate's funding denial under legal pressure. They adopted the Chicago Principles and climbed FIRE's rankings. They celebrate "open discourse" while hosting carnivals to avoid engaging with what that discourse actually says.

Here is the uncomfortable truth. If USC must choose between funding conservative provocateurs and progressive activists, it should choose neither. Defund Uncensored America. Defund them all. Return the student activity fees.

But if the university insists on funding political theater, it cannot pretend surprise when the performers use those fees to build the machinery crushing professors statewide. You cannot fund the arsonist and claim to support the fire department.

The free speech movement at USC is not a principle. It is a business model. And students are paying the invoice.

Boston University professor Saida Grundy, listed on the watchlist, said the harassment caused greater stress than her stage-three breast cancer. Drexel professor David Cohen said Kirk broadcast his information to "extremist readers with the clear intention that they should take action."

Next time Turning Point USA invokes the First Amendment at USC, ask one question. Will they condemn the Professor Watchlist and fight South Carolina's HB 3184 with the same energy they brought to a $3,577 funding request?

The silence will answer for them.
