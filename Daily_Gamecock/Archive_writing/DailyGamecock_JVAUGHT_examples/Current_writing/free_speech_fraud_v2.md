# Column: The "My Speech" Movement

On September 18, 2024, Milo Yiannopoulos and Proud Boys founder Gavin McInnes spoke to a sparse audience behind metal barricades at the University of South Carolina's Russell House.

The Student Senate had [rejected the event's funding 27-10](https://www.wistv.com/2024/09/13/usc-student-government-denies-funding-scheduled-roast-vice-president-kamala-harris/) one week earlier, reversing course only after a [lawsuit threat](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/10/uncensored-america-sues-usc-days-after-student-senate-grants-funding-for-roast-elam-news). Conservative media called it censorship. The [Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression](https://www.thefire.org/news/kamala-harris-comedy-roast-denied-funding-university-south-carolina-student-senate), the nation's leading campus free speech watchdog, agreed.

But here is what the conservative free speech movement will never say about itself: the same political coalition that rallied behind Uncensored America has enacted [more than 70 bills and policies across 26 states](https://pen.org/report/americas-censored-campuses-25-web-of-control/) since 2021 restricting what professors can teach, which books students can read, and which perspectives universities can value in hiring. They do not believe in free speech. They believe in their speech.

## The National Record

Since 2021, [PEN America](https://pen.org/report/americas-censored-campuses-25-web-of-control/) has tracked more than 70 bills and policies across 26 states attempting to censor higher education. In 2025 alone, [21 became law in 15 states](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/faculty-issues/curriculum/2026/01/15/report-state-lawmakers-enacted-21-censorship-bills-2025) — the highest single-year total ever recorded. Over 50 percent of American university students now attend institutions in states with at least one such restriction on the books. The hasty effort to enact this legislation could have an effect on everything from the literature students read to classroom discussions and activities.

The assault extends to libraries. PEN America documented [6,870 book bans](https://pen.org/book-bans/pen-america-index-of-school-book-bans-2024-2025/) in the 2024-2025 school year alone. The [American Library Association reports](https://www.ala.org/bbooks/book-ban-data) that 72 percent of these censorship demands originate from organized conservative movements, elected officials, and school board members. Forty-seven percent of challenged books feature LGBTQ+ or BIPOC voices. These are the same political movements that demand "free speech" protections for campus speakers.

## The TPUSA Contradiction

No organization embodies this hypocrisy more completely than Turning Point USA. The organization claims that it should be able to continue its rhetoric under the guise of free speech while maintaining a [Professor Watchlist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Watchlist) — a publicly searchable database targeting over 300 academics. PEN America [described the Watchlist](https://www.thefire.org/news/dual-purpose-tpusa-watchlist-warrants-two-toned-response) as "a noxious purveyor of precisely what it claims to deride: the intimidation and ostracization of those who express controversial views on campus."

The consequences are not hypothetical. At Arizona State University, TPUSA employees [physically assaulted](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2017/12/19/arizona-state-student-events-official-assaulted-political-group) a professor. At the University of Montana, a history professor received death threats after being placed on the list and was forced to [move his class](https://www.chronicle.com/article/charlie-kirks-watch-list-made-some-professors-lives-a-living-hell) to a secret location with campus police protection. When Turning Point USA stopped at USC on its "Campus Clash" tour, the Russell House ballroom had no empty seats. There was an almost tangible feeling of hostility in the room as soon as the speakers walked onto the stage, and many questions were regarded with tormenting, patronizing and angry responses.

An organization cannot coherently demand that universities platform speakers while simultaneously running a database designed to intimidate professors into silence. That is not a free speech position. That is a "my speech" position.

## The Speakers Themselves

Consider the men Uncensored America brought to USC. Yiannopoulos has based his career on being aggravating online and on college campuses. A British college dropout, his controversies include [appearing to defend pedophilia](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2017/feb/21/milo-yiannopoulos-resigns-breitbart-pedophilia-comments), riots before his [speech at UC Berkeley](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-38837142) that caused [$100,000 in damages](https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/grade-point/wp/2017/02/01/protests-at-uc-berkeley-prompted-by-milo-yiannopoulos/), and accusations of sexism, racism and hate speech. His conservative defenders championed absolute free speech until he crossed certain moral lines, at which point CPAC board member Ned Ryan [tweeted](https://ricochet.media/international/milos-very-bad-week-exposes-the-rights-hypocrisy-on-free-speech/): "While I'm all for free speech, there is such a thing as vile, hateful speech that does not deserve a platform." The principle was negotiable all along.

McInnes is a similarly controversial figure, his claim to fame being founding the Proud Boys — a group often associated with political violence, including the [U.S. Capitol attack](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/four-leaders-proud-boys-convicted-seditious-conspiracy) on Jan. 6, 2021. McInnes publicly preaches that "sunlight is the best disinfectant" — yet when the Southern Poverty Law Center labeled the Proud Boys a hate group, he [filed a defamation lawsuit](https://www.techdirt.com/2019/07/11/splc-asks-court-to-toss-proud-boy-founders-defamation-lawsuit-asking-wheres-lie/) to silence them. When the flagship speakers of a free speech movement sue critics and are abandoned by their own allies the moment their speech becomes inconvenient, the movement has revealed what it actually values: the right to offend without consequence, not the right to speak freely.

## South Carolina's Turn

Republican politicians and public officials have constantly tried to interfere with academic operations at South Carolina's universities. In 2021, Gov. Henry McMaster signed the REACH Act — a bill mandating that state universities require students to take a class teaching certain aspects of American history. The South Carolina Commission on Higher Education then requested section-specific syllabuses from USC to ensure compliance, making it easy for anyone to determine exactly what specific professors are teaching.

The fear, as stated by USC political science department chair Kirk Randazzo, is "not only that we're on a potentially slippery slope that may violate academic freedom for faculty members, but my greater concern is that by simply monitoring things, the state is potentially creating a chilling effect that might influence or impact how faculty teach material." USC history professor Carol Harrison confirmed the fear is already reality: "The chill is already here."

An [open letter](https://www.postandcourier.com/politics/sc-gop-delegation-tells-usc-clemson-to-eradicate-critical-race-theory/article_85e96e5e-d53f-11eb-9e0b-7f0e925f3c53.html) from Republican members of South Carolina's Congressional delegation called on university presidents to "eradicate" any teaching of critical race theory — and identified a USC professor by name. The 2025-2026 session brought a flood of new bills: [HB 3184](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/3184.htm) banning DEI in admissions and employment, [SB 368](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/368.htm) prohibiting DEI offices statewide, and the [FORUM Act of 2026](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/4749.htm) designating campus outdoor areas as public forums.

The contradiction is stark: South Carolina legislators want to protect the speech of campus activists and controversial speakers while simultaneously empowering the state to dictate what professors can teach. Free speech for the demonstrator; government control over the classroom.

## What About the Left?

Yes, progressive students are hypocrites too. At USC, [27,000 people signed a petition](https://www.postandcourier.com/education-lab/usc-doj-charlie-kirk-outdoor-event-controversial/article_c1d6ca0b-1173-403d-bd16-6f8fec66f748.html) to cancel the McInnes-Yiannopoulos event. Months later, many of the same voices decried the arrest of pro-Palestinian demonstrators as suppression of protected expression. While decrying censorship has long been a tool used by extremist groups to gain access to legitimate public forums, the progressive contradiction involves petitions and protests — campus theater. The conservative contradiction involves legislation, state power, professors fired, tenure eliminated, entire fields of study banned by law. These are not equivalent.

## The Administrative Enabler

USC claims viewpoint neutrality while making content-based decisions that determine whose speech gets heard. USC's campus has been ranked as one of the worst schools in the country for its handling of free speech, according to FIRE — its lack of response to de-platforming attempts played a large part in its poor ranking. In June 2023, USC adopted the Chicago Principles — which state that "it is not the proper role of the University to attempt to shield individuals from ideas and opinions they find unwelcome, disagreeable, or even deeply offensive." By 2026, the ranking jumped to [22nd out of 257 schools](https://www.thefire.org/news/tale-two-universities-why-south-carolina-soared-and-harvard-didnt-fires-free-speech-rankings).

But the September 2024 events suggest the turnaround is more reputation management than conviction. The Student Senate rejected Uncensored America's modest funding request — [FIRE called it](https://www.thefire.org/news/kamala-harris-comedy-roast-denied-funding-university-south-carolina-student-senate) "a clear violation of the First Amendment." That same night, [over 1,000 students gathered at Blatt Field](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/09/over-1000-students-attend-blatt-bonanza-during-controversial-campus-event-news-grady) for "Blatt Bonanza" — free food, bouncy houses, a rage room, and presidential selfies — while Yiannopoulos spoke to empty chairs at Russell House. SG President Patton Byars [insisted](https://wach.com/news/local/usc-students-seek-safe-space-at-usc-event-during-controversial-political-roast): "This is the event on our campus. It's not an alternative or counter event." Students described it as exactly that.

On Aug. 27, the University of South Carolina community received an email from President Amiridis and the chair of the board of trustees, Thad Westbrook, explaining the university's commitment to freedom of speech on campus. Amiridis condemned the ["vile and juvenile rhetoric"](https://www.statehousereport.com/2024/09/06/more-news-usc-gets-high-marks-for-free-speech-as-controversy-roils-campus/) used to promote the event — while defending free speech. The use of partisan language by administrators in an official statement is dangerous, regardless of intent. Students who engage in pro-censorship demonstrations believe it is their duty to shut down speakers that the university has labeled in negative terms. Compare this to Amiridis's presence at Blatt Bonanza, taking selfies with students at the counter-event while Yiannopoulos spoke to empty chairs. The president's feet revealed his position more clearly than his words.

One year later, the DOJ [sent a letter](https://www.postandcourier.com/education-lab/usc-doj-charlie-kirk-outdoor-event-controversial/article_c1d6ca0b-1173-403d-bd16-6f8fec66f748.html) citing "troubling allegations" that USC administrators told conservative student groups that "controversial speakers and activities will no longer happen outside." Administrators had labeled the late Charlie Kirk a ["known disrupter to a population"](https://abcnews4.com/news/state/assistant-ag-demands-usc-continue-outdoor-debates-september-2025-columbia) — without defining either term. USC denied any such policy existed. If that is true, why would a DOJ letter be necessary?

## The One Question

The next time Turning Point USA or Uncensored America invokes the First Amendment at USC, ask one question: Would you apply this principle to the professors being fired in Florida and Texas for teaching concepts you oppose?

If they would, they should say so — loudly, publicly, and to the state legislators who fund their movement. Intellectual pursuits are not furthered by hiding undesirable voices. Rather, crude antagonism is only defeated when countered with genuine depth of knowledge stemming from a deep pursuit of truth.

If they wouldn't, they should stop pretending. We have the right to explore topics and issues in our curriculum in ways that provide multiple perspectives for engaging in the world through ideas. If opinions cannot be openly expressed, no change is going to be made in society. The answer to misinformation and repugnant speech is greater speech, not enforced silence. The loudest voices for free speech on this campus belong to a movement that is, right now, in state legislatures across the country, doing more to destroy it than any student petition or bouncy house ever could.
