# Column: The "My Speech" Movement

On September 18, 2024, Milo Yiannopoulos and Proud Boys founder Gavin McInnes spoke to a sparse audience behind metal barricades at the University of South Carolina's Russell House. Uncensored America, the student organization that hosted them — a group that describes itself as ["a nonpartisan, nonprofit organization dedicated to fighting for freedom of speech"](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/09/column-opinion-the-uncensored-america-should-not-be-taken-seriously-opinion-dunn) — had its $3,576.99 funding request [rejected 27-10](https://www.wistv.com/2024/09/13/usc-student-government-denies-funding-scheduled-roast-vice-president-kamala-harris/) by the Student Senate, reversed only after a [lawsuit threat](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/10/uncensored-america-sues-usc-days-after-student-senate-grants-funding-for-roast-elam-news). Conservative media called it censorship. [FIRE agreed](https://www.thefire.org/news/kamala-harris-comedy-roast-denied-funding-university-south-carolina-student-senate). They were right.

But here is what the conservative free speech movement will never say about itself: the same political coalition that rallied behind Uncensored America has enacted [more than 70 bills and policies across 26 states](https://pen.org/report/americas-censored-campuses-25-web-of-control/) since 2021 restricting what professors can teach, which books students can read, and which perspectives universities can value in hiring. Free speech is being plucked away by institutions because they believe that their voices are the only valid ones. They do not believe in free speech. They believe in their speech. And the record proves it.

## The Legislative Record

2025 was a record-breaking year for state censorship of higher education. According to [PEN America's analysis](https://pen.org/report/americas-censored-campuses-25-web-of-control/), state legislators introduced 93 bills across 32 states targeting colleges and universities. Of these, [21 became law in 15 states](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/faculty-issues/curriculum/2026/01/15/report-state-lawmakers-enacted-21-censorship-bills-2025) — the highest single-year total ever recorded, in the highest number of states, with eight states enacting such restrictions for the first time. Arkansas, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, North Dakota, Ohio, Texas, Utah, West Virginia, and Wyoming all joined the censorship regime.

The cumulative toll since 2021: more than 70 bills and policies across 26 states have attempted to censor higher education. Twenty-three states have now enacted laws or policies restricting academic speech, meaning over 50 percent of American university students attend institutions in states with at least one such restriction on the books.

The hasty effort to enact this legislation could have an effect on everything from the literature students read to classroom discussions and activities. According to a [2022 study from PEN America](https://pen.org/report/banned-in-the-usa-state-laws-supercharge-book-suppression-in-schools/), at least 40 percent of book bans are due to proposed or enacted legislation opposed to critical race theory and ideological teachings in schools. This is not protecting free speech. This is the government telling professors what they can and cannot say.

## The Book Ban Epidemic

The assault extends beyond the classroom to the library. [PEN America documented](https://pen.org/book-bans/pen-america-index-of-school-book-bans-2024-2025/) 6,870 instances of book bans in the 2024-2025 school year alone, affecting nearly 4,000 unique titles across 23 states and 87 public school districts. Since 2021, nearly 23,000 book bans have been recorded in American public schools — a number, PEN America notes, "never before seen in the life of any living American."

The geography is revealing. Florida led the nation with 2,304 bans, followed by Texas with 1,781 and Tennessee with 1,622. The [American Library Association reports](https://www.ala.org/bbooks/book-ban-data) that 72 percent of these censorship demands originate from "pressure groups and government entities" — organized conservative movements, elected officials, and school board members. The targets are equally telling: 47 percent of challenged books feature LGBTQ+ or BIPOC voices and themes.

These are the same states, the same political movements, and often the same organizations that demand "free speech" protections for campus speakers. The principle applies only to speech they approve.

## The TPUSA Contradiction

No organization embodies this hypocrisy more completely than Turning Point USA. The organization claims that it should be able to continue its rhetoric under the guise of free speech while maintaining a [Professor Watchlist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Watchlist) — a publicly searchable database targeting over 300 academics sorted into categories including "diversity, equity, and inclusion," "climate alarmist," and "feminism." Groups like Turning Point USA push a mentality of "us vs. them" that breeds division, ridicule and misunderstanding that leads to hate and violence.

PEN America [described the Watchlist](https://www.thefire.org/news/dual-purpose-tpusa-watchlist-warrants-two-toned-response) as "a noxious purveyor of precisely what it claims to deride: the intimidation and ostracization of those who express controversial views on campus." Heterodox Academy warned it creates a climate of fear that "deprives everyone of the vigorous debate and disagreement that is essential for learning and scholarship." The AAUP noted that such lists "serve the same purpose: to intimidate individuals from speaking plainly in their classrooms or in their publications."

As one professor who was placed on the list told [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/charlie-kirk-professor-watchlist-free-speech-college-campus-rcna231448): "There's been so much hypocrisy... He's being touted as this paragon of free speech who just wanted to engage with people who disagree with him. In fact, he was behind a website that targeted those who he disagreed with and broadcast information about us to his extremist readers with the clear intention that they should take some kind of action against us."

The consequences are not hypothetical. At Arizona State University, TPUSA employees [physically assaulted](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2017/12/19/arizona-state-student-events-official-assaulted-political-group) Professor David Boyles. Tobin Miller Shearer, a history professor at the University of Montana, received death threats after being placed on the list and was forced to [move his class](https://www.chronicle.com/article/charlie-kirks-watch-list-made-some-professors-lives-a-living-hell) on the history of white supremacy to a secret location with campus police protection. The default targets for TPUSA and their political allies tend to be faculty of color, women, and people in what they perceive to be vulnerable positions. The efficacy of the Professor Watchlist in making a faculty member's life a living hell is deeply connected to that vulnerability.

When Turning Point USA stopped at USC on its "Campus Clash" tour, the Russell House ballroom had no empty seats and people were standing in the back because of how packed it was. There was an almost tangible feeling of hostility in the room as soon as the speakers walked onto the stage. During the Q&A, speakers made it clear that they respect those on the other side — but many inquiries were regarded with tormenting, patronizing and angry responses. Minorities in America still face slurs, hate crimes and harassment that can happen simply because of their identities. The fact that Turning Point USA makes the argument that conservatives face discrimination ignores years and years of discrimination that the far right has caused.

An organization cannot coherently demand that universities platform speakers while simultaneously running a McCarthyite database designed to intimidate professors into silence. That is not a free speech position. That is a "my speech" position.

When the flagship speakers of a free speech movement sue critics, file defamation claims against watchdog organizations, and are abandoned by their own allies the moment their speech becomes inconvenient, the movement has revealed what it actually values: the right to offend without consequence, not the right to speak freely.

## The Brain Drain

The legislative assault on academic freedom has material consequences that undermine the very universities conservatives claim to defend. The bedrock of academic freedom is the idea that instructors should have the freedom to teach mostly as they see fit, and even relatively inoffensive violations of that idea threaten the academic independence of our universities.

A [University of Southern California study](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/faculty-issues/academic-freedom/2025/06/03/post-tenure-review-resulted-florida-faculty-brain) found that after Florida implemented its post-tenure review policy in 2022, the percentage of tenure-track faculty leaving the state increased from 4 percent to 5 percent annually. The exodus was concentrated among highly productive researchers — the faculty most valuable to any institution — and disproportionately affected non-STEM fields, precisely the disciplines targeted by DeSantis's reforms.

The self-reported data is even more alarming. A [survey of over 4,250 faculty](https://www.floridatrend.com/article/37385/are-florida-laws-chasing-university-faculty-away-some-see-a-brain-drain) across four states found that nearly half of Florida respondents planned to seek employment in another state within the next year. Eighty-five percent said they would not encourage a graduate student or colleague to move to Florida. Over 95 percent described the political climate around higher education as "poor" or "very poor." The top destinations for departing faculty: California, New York, Massachusetts, and North Carolina.

In Texas, [faculty surveys show](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/09/05/texas-faculty-university-political-climate-survey/) similar sentiments after the state banned DEI programs, restructured tenure policies, and limited faculty input on curriculum and hiring. The same politicians who claim to protect free expression are driving the most qualified academics out of their states.

In Florida, Governor DeSantis's "Stop WOKE" Act and SB 266 mandated the exclusion of "identity politics" from humanities courses while emphasizing "Western canon" materials. The result: [over half the humanities courses](https://hulr.org/spring-2025/academic-freedom-under-scrutiny-legal-and-educational-implications-of-floridas-higher-education-reforms) at the University of Florida were removed. At New College, 36 professors — one-third of the faculty — fled after a conservative board takeover restructured the institution's academic mission. Texas's SB 18 eliminates tenure protections entirely, while SB 16 empowers students to initiate termination proceedings against professors who teach concepts related to critical race theory.

## South Carolina's Turn

South Carolina is not immune to this wave. Republican politicians and public officials have constantly tried to interfere with academic operations at South Carolina's universities. This trend is a dangerous threat to academic freedom in the state.

In 2021, Gov. Henry McMaster signed the REACH Act into law — a bill that mandates state universities require their students to take a class teaching certain aspects of American history, including the country's "founding documents." The South Carolina Commission on Higher Education then moved to request section-specific syllabuses from USC and other universities to ensure compliance — making it easy for anyone to determine exactly what specific professors are teaching.

The fear, as stated by USC political science department chair Kirk Randazzo, is "not only that we're on a potentially slippery slope that may violate academic freedom for faculty members, but my greater concern is that by simply monitoring things, the state is potentially creating a chilling effect that might influence or impact how faculty teach material." USC history professor Carol Harrison confirmed the fear is already reality: "The chill is already here. The chilling effect, we are feeling it."

An [open letter](https://www.postandcourier.com/politics/sc-gop-delegation-tells-usc-clemson-to-eradicate-critical-race-theory/article_85e96e5e-d53f-11eb-9e0b-7f0e925f3c53.html) from Republican members of South Carolina's Congressional delegation to university leadership called on presidents to "eradicate" any teaching of critical race theory. Disturbingly, the letter identified and attacked a USC professor by name who teaches a CRT class. Professors understandably do not want to become targets of smear campaigns. Teachers might also refrain from teaching controversial but important topics if they believe it might trigger backlash from the legislature or other conservative actors — a concern particularly important for faculty without tenure, women and people of color.

The 2025-2026 legislative session has seen a flood of new proposed restrictions. [House Bill 3184](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/3184.htm), introduced in January 2025, would prohibit public institutions from using "political ideology or statements on diversity, equity, and inclusion" in admissions or employment decisions, ban DEI training requirements, and prohibit any "adverse action" against employees who refuse to participate in such training. [House Bill 3572](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/3572.htm), introduced the same day, adds provisions against "viewpoint discrimination" — language that sounds neutral until you realize it defines viewpoint discrimination as anything that might make a conservative student uncomfortable.

[Senate Bill 368](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/368.htm) and [House Bill 3927](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/3927.htm) go further, extending restrictions beyond higher education to every state agency and political subdivision. The bills would prohibit any promotion of "differential treatment or special benefits to individuals on the basis of race, sex, ethnicity, gender, or sexual orientation," ban the establishment of offices that promote DEI, and prohibit hiring anyone who has worked in such roles.

Notably, the same legislature introduced the [FORUM Act of 2026](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/4749.htm) in January, which would designate outdoor campus areas as public forums and prohibit universities from creating "free speech zones." The bill demands protection for "peaceful assembly, protests, speeches and guest speakers, distribution of literature, carrying signs, and circulating petitions."

The contradiction is stark: South Carolina legislators want to protect the speech of campus activists and controversial speakers while simultaneously empowering the state to dictate what professors can teach and which perspectives universities can value in hiring. Free speech for the demonstrator; government control over the classroom.

## The Polling Paradox

Survey data reveals the intellectual incoherence at the heart of this position. According to a [University of Chicago free expression survey](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/free-expression-survey-finds-bipartisan-opposition-state-restrictions-what-professors-teach), about two-thirds of Americans — 68 percent — oppose state government restrictions on what professors can teach at state universities. This opposition holds across party lines.

Yet the same political movement that demands government stay out of faculty speech supports state legislatures dictating which topics professors can address. A [Knight Foundation study](https://knightfoundation.org/reports/college-student-views-on-free-expression-and-campus-speech-2024/) found that Republican students overwhelmingly favor government denial of federal funds to colleges that do not adequately protect free speech — the same students whose political allies are enacting state laws that restrict faculty speech by force of law.

Freedom of expression is becoming a right-wing issue when it should be an issue for everyone. Free speech is needed to facilitate conversations between people from both sides of the political spectrum. Without a free market of ideas and a voice for truth, the fundamental way to oppose evil, oppression and false information is lost. Yet the answer to misinformation and repugnant speech is more speech, not enforced silence — a principle the conservative movement applies selectively.

The contradiction is captured perfectly by a [Middle Tennessee State University survey](https://firstamendment.mtsu.edu/post/americans-love-free-speech-survey-finds-until-they-realize-their-opponents-have-it-too/): "Large majorities of Americans passionately uphold free speech as a cornerstone of democracy. But fewer of them are supportive of free speech when faced with specific controversial speakers or topics." The principle is universal in the abstract and partisan in application.

For years, conservative politicians warned that college campuses were hostile to free speech. When pro-Palestinian protests erupted, these same politicians demanded the demonstrations be banned and halted by force. When professors teach about systemic racism, they must be fired. When drag shows occur on campus, they must be prohibited. When books feature LGBTQ+ characters, they must be removed.

Political extremists will always make themselves out to be the victims and, as such, they will always claim to be censored. But the progressive contradiction involves petitions and protests — campus theater. The conservative contradiction involves legislation. State power. Professors fired, tenure eliminated, entire fields of study banned by law. One side shouts down speakers. The other dismantles academic freedom itself. These are not equivalent, and pretending they are is its own form of intellectual dishonesty.

## The Administrative Enabler

The administration's role in this story is not neutral either. USC claims viewpoint neutrality while making content-based decisions that determine whose speech gets heard — and that pattern has now drawn federal attention.

### From "Ten Worst" to Chicago Principles: A Convenient Conversion

The backstory matters. In February 2016, USC administrators [interrogated student Ross Abbott](https://www.thefire.org/students-interrogated-for-organizing-free-speech-event-file-first-amendment-lawsuit-against-university-of-south-carolina/) and threatened him with expulsion — for organizing a free speech event. In 2015, a group of students had hosted a free speech event at USC with the university's approval. Protesters voiced concerns about the symbols displayed, including a swastika, and notified the university of racist and sexist comments allegedly made at the event. USC investigated, found there was no cause for additional investigation, and dropped the matter. The students who held the event then filed suit for violations of their First Amendment rights and the "chilling effect" of free speech on campus as a result of USC's investigation.

The groups filed a First Amendment lawsuit with FIRE's backing. The Court of Appeals decided the students' freedom of speech had not been "chilled" and dismissed their case. But USC fought the lawsuit, lost in the court of public opinion, and landed on [FIRE's "ten worst colleges for free speech"](https://www.fitsnews.com/2017/02/22/usc-ranks-among-worst-campuses-for-free-speech/) list. USC's campus has been ranked as one of the worst schools in the country for its handling of free speech, according to FIRE. USC's lack of response to its de-platforming attempts, which prevented multiple speakers from coming to speak on campus, played a large part in its poor ranking. By 2024, USC ranked 246th out of 251 institutions — bottom five in the nation.

Then came the pivot. The school began working on fixing its reputation by taking steps to improve its free speech policies. In June 2023, USC adopted the Chicago Principles — which state that "it is not the proper role of the University to attempt to shield individuals from ideas and opinions they find unwelcome, disagreeable, or even deeply offensive" — and revised four speech-restrictive policies. By 2025, the ranking jumped to 34th; by 2026, to [22nd out of 257 schools](https://www.thefire.org/news/tale-two-universities-why-south-carolina-soared-and-harvard-didnt-fires-free-speech-rankings). FIRE praised the "180-degree" turnaround. President Amiridis earned laudatory press for championing free expression.

The question is whether this represents genuine institutional commitment or strategic reputation management. The September 2024 events suggest the latter.

### The Funding Decision

On September 11, 2024, USC's Student Senate faced a [$3,576.99 funding request](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/09/student-senate-denies-funding-for-uncensored-america-passes-bill-to-increase-security-at-event-news-woodson) from Uncensored America for their "Roast of Kamala Harris" event featuring Milo Yiannopoulos and Proud Boys founder Gavin McInnes. As the title implied, this was not an opportunity for students to participate in healthy political discourse, but an attempt at comedy demeaning the Vice President and 2024 Democratic party nominee. The finance committee had already approved the request, confirming the group followed all relevant rules.

The full senate rejected it [27-10](https://www.wistv.com/2024/09/13/usc-student-government-denies-funding-scheduled-roast-vice-president-kamala-harris/).

The breakdown of the denied request reveals its modesty: $1,500 for speakers, $803 for decorations, $647 for camera rentals, $127 for advertising, and $500 for USC Police security. This was not an extravagant ask. It followed every procedural requirement. The finance committee certified compliance. When it comes to speech on college campuses, the First Amendment is the law of the land, and while there are plenty of legally recognized categories of unprotected speech, the United States does not generally recognize "hate speech" as one of those exceptions.

The senate denied it anyway. [FIRE's analysis](https://www.thefire.org/news/kamala-harris-comedy-roast-denied-funding-university-south-carolina-student-senate) was blunt: "By denying Uncensored America's funding request in an attempt to remain politically neutral, the student senate in fact chose to allocate funding on the basis of the group's viewpoint — in clear violation of the First Amendment."

One week later, facing a lawsuit and national attention, the senate reversed course and [granted the original request](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/10/uncensored-america-sues-usc-days-after-student-senate-grants-funding-for-roast-elam-news). Uncensored America [sued anyway](https://www.wistv.com/2024/10/02/uncensored-america-files-lawsuit-against-university-of-south-carolina-following-controversial-roast/), alleging First Amendment violations. The reversal did not erase the constitutional harm of the initial denial.

### The Counter-Programming

While Student Government debated $3,576.99 for speakers the majority found objectionable, the administration took a different approach to speakers it preferred.

On September 18, 2024 — the same night as the Uncensored America event — [over 1,000 students gathered at Blatt Field](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/09/over-1000-students-attend-blatt-bonanza-during-controversial-campus-event-news-grady) for "Blatt Bonanza." Event organizers reported over 1,500 attendees. The event featured free food, inflatables, bouncy houses, spike ball, pickle ball, a mobile rage room, and free t-shirts for all attendees. Multiple campus organizations collaborated: Student Government Association, Carolina Productions, Kappa Delta Chi, and the Residence Hall Association.

President Amiridis himself attended, posing for selfies with students while Yiannopoulos and McInnes spoke to a sparse audience behind metal barricades at Russell House.

SG President Patton Byars [framed the event](https://wach.com/news/local/usc-students-seek-safe-space-at-usc-event-during-controversial-political-roast) carefully: "This is the event on our campus. It's not an alternative or counter event; this is just what we do here."

But the timing was not coincidental. Students [described the event](https://www.dailygamecock.com/article/2024/09/over-1000-students-attend-blatt-bonanza-during-controversial-campus-event-news-grady) as a way to protest the Uncensored America roast. President Amiridis acknowledged Student Government "initially proposed the event" before collaborating with other student groups. The collaboration between SG, Carolina Productions, and institutional resources produced a counter-event that dwarfed the speech being countered.

The disparity is the story. Uncensored America requested $3,576.99 and faced a 27-10 rejection from elected student representatives. Blatt Bonanza — with its bouncy houses, rage room, free food for over 1,500 students, and presidential attendance — received full institutional backing. The administration claims these decisions reflect viewpoint neutrality. The math suggests otherwise.

### The DOJ Letter

One year later, federal officials noticed the pattern.

On September 19, 2025, Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon sent a letter to President Amiridis citing ["troubling allegations"](https://www.postandcourier.com/education-lab/usc-doj-charlie-kirk-outdoor-event-controversial/article_c1d6ca0b-1173-403d-bd16-6f8fec66f748.html) that USC administrators told Turning Point USA and Uncensored America student leaders that "controversial speakers and activities will no longer happen outside for the rest of the semester."

According to the DOJ, administrators labeled the late Charlie Kirk as a ["known disrupter to a population"](https://abcnews4.com/news/state/assistant-ag-demands-usc-continue-outdoor-debates-september-2025-columbia) — without defining either "disrupter" or which "population" he allegedly disrupted. No clarification was provided when students asked.

Dhillon's letter was direct: "TPUSA and UA may voluntarily choose indoor venues for campus safety reasons in light of the potential threat from those that celebrate the horrific assassination of Charlie Kirk, but a mandate directed only at these groups because they are 'controversial' and may 'disrupt' a 'population' is impermissible viewpoint discrimination."

The letter concluded with a memorable flourish: "U of SC — act unwise, get a surprise!"

USC's response came quickly. Spokesman Jeff Stensland [stated](https://www.postandcourier.com/education-lab/usc-doj-charlie-kirk-outdoor-event-controversial/article_c1d6ca0b-1173-403d-bd16-6f8fec66f748.html) that the university "honors requests from student organizations to host guest speakers outdoors and will continue to do so, regardless of the student organization or the speaker visiting campus." He admitted he did not have "complete information about what may have been conveyed to the groups."

State Attorney General Alan Wilson spoke to Amiridis, who assured him ["no policies limiting free speech will be implemented."](https://wach.com/news/local/assistant-ag-demands-usc-continue-outdoor-debates-september-2025-columbia)

The denial itself is telling. If no such policy existed, why would administrators allegedly tell students it did? If USC's commitment to viewpoint neutrality was genuine, why would a DOJ letter be necessary?

### The Security Cost Question

The Supreme Court addressed content-based security fees directly in [*Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement*](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/123/) (1992): "Speech cannot be financially burdened, any more than it can be punished or banned, simply because it might offend a hostile mob." Charging speakers more because their content provokes opposition is unconstitutional.

In September 2025, South Carolina Attorney General Alan Wilson joined [16 other state attorneys general](https://scdailygazette.com/2025/09/19/ags-in-sc-and-16-other-states-warn-colleges-against-high-security-fees-for-conservative-voices/) in warning colleges against using "exorbitant" security fees to suppress conservative speech. The letter cited *Forsyth County* and warned that differential security treatment could violate state Consumer Fraud Acts.

To their credit, USC representatives [told state legislators](https://www.postandcourier.com/columbia/news/sc-lawmakers-want-new-law-to-protect-college-campus-protests-despite-lack-of-activism/article_3f4fe20a-e2e9-11ed-b0e2-cb477d4866bc.html) they had eliminated security fees from their policies. But Student Government's rejection of Uncensored America's funding included $500 for USC Police security — meaning the cost burden fell on the student organization regardless. Since 2024, SLED has assisted in [18 events at USC](https://www.wyff4.com/article/sc-college-university-campus-security-concerns-laws/66130441) alone, from sporting events to guest speakers, raising questions about which events require state law enforcement and which do not.

Student Emily Whitaker, attending the Uncensored America event, [questioned why elevated security was needed](https://www.wltx.com/article/news/local/controversial-event-usc/101-2c3eebfb-b310-4f96-bf66-6d098dc6b1f3) when she had not seen such measures for other campus events with opposing views — pointing to a drag show during the first week of classes as an example.

### The Amiridis Contradiction

On Aug. 27, the University of South Carolina community received an email from President Amiridis and the chair of the board of trustees, Thad Westbrook, explaining the university's commitment to freedom of speech on campus. While not explicitly stated, the email referred to recent pushback to the upcoming Uncensored America event. Amiridis [stated publicly](https://sc.edu/about/our_leadership/president/presidential-communications/2024-08-27_statement_about_campus_speakers.php) that USC follows the Chicago Principles and remains "steadfast in safeguarding the First Amendment rights of our students, even when we may be offended by their choices and statements."

In the same statement, Amiridis and Westbrook condemned the ["vile and juvenile rhetoric"](https://www.statehousereport.com/2024/09/06/more-news-usc-gets-high-marks-for-free-speech-as-controversy-roils-campus/) used to promote the event — while defending the university's commitment to free speech. Simply stating that we do not share the views of these guests, while still giving them a forum, will never be the complete story. The use of partisan language by administrators in an official statement is dangerous, regardless of intent. Students who engage in pro-censorship demonstrations believe it is their duty to shut down speakers that the university has labeled in negative terms.

Compare this to Amiridis's presence at Blatt Bonanza three weeks later, taking selfies with students at the university-backed counter-event while Yiannopoulos spoke to empty chairs. The president's feet revealed his position more clearly than his words.

This is viewpoint neutrality? The administration condemns one event's rhetoric while its president literally appears at the counter-programming? Either USC takes no position on speaker content or it does. It cannot be both.

### The Policy Fix That Proved the Point

USC's trajectory tells the real story. In a survey conducted among USC students, respondents were asked how safe they felt to express their opinions on campus. The average rating was a 3.2 on a scale of 5. For a state school that preaches the importance of expression and equity, that is an extremely poor and embarrassing rating. According to FIRE, 49 percent of students are scared to share their beliefs on abortion due to potential backlash.

In 2024, [FIRE ranked USC](https://thefire.org/sites/default/files/2024-09/2025%20CFSR%20Spotlight%20-%20U%20of%20South%20Carolina.pdf) 246th out of 248 institutions — bottom five in the nation, rated "Very Poor." One year later: 34th. By 2026: [22nd out of 257 schools](https://www.thefire.org/news/tale-two-universities-why-south-carolina-soared-and-harvard-didnt-fires-free-speech-rankings).

What changed? Not hearts and minds. In June 2023, the Board of Trustees endorsed the Chicago Statement. Over the following year, administrators [revised four specific policies](https://www.thefire.org/news/university-south-carolina-earns-top-rating-free-speech): the harassment provision in the student code of conduct, the technology misuse provision, the posting policy, and the Carolinian Creed's enforcement language. By June 2024, USC earned a "green light" rating — one of only 66 schools nationwide.

According to FIRE, two-thirds of USC's ranking improvement came from students reporting a better speech climate after these policy changes. But the university still ranks in the bottom 50 for "Comfort Expressing Ideas" — students remain hesitant to speak freely even as official policies improved. No matter how many policies are put into place, it is up to the students to lead the fight to end censorship. Institutional structure changed behavior. Conviction had nothing to do with it.

## The One Question

The next time Turning Point USA or Uncensored America invokes the First Amendment at USC, ask one question: Would you apply this principle to the professors being fired in Florida and Texas for teaching concepts you oppose?

If they would, they should say so — loudly, publicly, and to the state legislators who fund their movement. They should demand the Professor Watchlist be taken down. They should oppose every bill that tells a professor what they can and cannot teach with the same energy they bring to defending provocateurs at Russell House. Intellectual pursuits are not furthered by hiding undesirable voices. Rather, crude antagonism is only defeated when countered with genuine depth of knowledge stemming from a deep pursuit of truth.

If they wouldn't, they should stop pretending. As [PEN America](https://pen.org/report/educational-gag-orders/) documented: "There is no act of censorship that is more explicit than literally banning words." You cannot coherently demand a platform for speakers who call women "biologically inferior" while supporting legislation that fires professors for teaching about systemic racism. That is not a free speech position. That is a power position — constitutional language wielded as a weapon by people who would abandon it the moment it stopped serving their interests.

We have the right to explore topics and issues in our curriculum in ways that provide multiple perspectives for engaging in the world through ideas. That should be a fundamental priority. If opinions cannot be openly expressed, no change is going to be made in society. The answer to misinformation and repugnant speech is greater speech, not enforced silence. The loudest voices for free speech on this campus belong to a movement that is, right now, in state legislatures across the country, doing more to destroy it than any student petition or bouncy house ever could.
