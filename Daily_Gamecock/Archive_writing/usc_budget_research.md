# USC Budget Research - Comprehensive Financial Data

**Research Date:** January 19, 2026
**Fiscal Years Covered:** FY 2024 (ended June 30, 2024), FY 2025 (ending June 30, 2025), FY 2026 (ending June 30, 2026)

## Executive Summary

The University of South Carolina operates on a budget exceeding $1.6 billion annually, with significant increases in both revenue and expenditures over recent years. The FY 2025 budget projected $1.633 billion in revenue (10.6% increase) and $1.632 billion in expenditures (10.8% increase).

## Molinaroli College of Engineering and Computing

### $30 Million Donation Details

**Donors:** Alex Molinaroli (1983 USC alumnus, former CEO of Johnson Controls) and his wife Kristin Ihle Molinaroli
**Announcement:** June 2024
**Purpose:** Support creation of new programs, student and faculty recruitment, facility and equipment enhancements, and research start-ups

### Allocation Plans for $30M Gift

The donation will be used to establish the college as a leading educational and research institution throughout the Mid-Atlantic and Southeast. Specific initiatives include:

1. **Center for Industry Solutions**
   - Formalize partnerships with South Carolina engineering and computing industries
   - Students work on real-world problems
   - AI-powered technology for manufacturing inspections

2. **New Academic Programs**
   - Industrial Engineering degree program (confirmed)
   - Other high-demand programs (unspecified)

3. **Student and Faculty Recruitment**
   - Attract best students and faculty
   - Competitive compensation and resources

4. **Facility and Equipment Enhancements**
   - State-of-the-art equipment
   - Software training resources
   - Modern research facilities

5. **Research Start-ups**
   - Support for new research initiatives
   - Industry partnerships

6. **Internship Opportunities**
   - Expansion of internship programs
   - Relationship-building with regional engineering companies
   - Student job placement support

*Note: Specific dollar allocations for each initiative were not publicly disclosed. The donation is characterized as a strategic investment to enhance competitiveness and growth.*

### Engineering College Performance Metrics

**FY 2024 Sponsored Research Awards:** Over $70 million (university record)
**Average Starting Salary (2020-21 graduates):** $70,247
**Industry Partnerships:** 38 established partnerships with industry and government agencies
**Student Research Internships:** More than 150 undergraduate students engaged annually

## Key Financial Trends and Notes

### Multi-Year Tuition Policy

USC has frozen in-state undergraduate tuition for:
- Columbia campus: 6 consecutive years (as of FY 2025)
- System institutions: 7 consecutive years (as of FY 2025)

### Revenue Growth Drivers (FY 2024)

1. **Sponsored Research:** 9.6% increase in grants and contracts
   - Federal grants up 16.6%
   - Total sponsored awards: $309M (27% increase)

2. **Auxiliary Services:** 9.0% increase
   - Housing, dining, parking, athletics
   - Campus health services, study abroad, events

3. **State Support:** Increased appropriations
   - Tuition mitigation funding
   - Special purpose appropriations

4. **Investment Performance:** Strong returns
   - Investment income: $19.98M
   - Endowment income: $4.41M
   - Reflects unrealized gains

### Expense Growth Drivers (FY 2024)

1. **Compensation:** $62.5M increase
   - State-mandated cost-of-living adjustment
   - Salary increases for competitive positioning

2. **Inflation Impact:** $36.7M increase in services and supplies
   - General goods and services
   - Supply chain cost increases

3. **Energy Costs:** $3.1M increase in utilities
   - Global energy price increases

4. **Financial Aid:** $5.0M increase in scholarships
   - Need-based student support

5. **Depreciation:** $24.9M increase
   - New building depreciation
   - Public-private partnership arrangements

## Data Limitations and Notes

1. **College-Specific Budgets:** Individual college operating budgets (beyond Engineering's $70M in sponsored awards) are not publicly disclosed in detail. Budget documents show total academic allocations but not itemized college breakdowns.

2. **Travel Expenses:** No separate line item for travel expenditures found in operating budget or ACFR. Travel costs are embedded in "Services and Supplies" category.

3. **Fee Revenue Breakdown:** While student fee amounts are specified, detailed revenue breakdowns by specific fee type are not provided in publicly available documents.

4. **Molinaroli Donation Timeline:** Specific timeline for spending the $30M donation and detailed dollar allocations per initiative have not been publicly disclosed.

5. **Engineering College Operating Budget:** The college's base operating budget (separate from sponsored research) is not separately disclosed in available documents.

6. **PDF Accessibility:** Some budget documents (FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26 operating budgets) were too large or had formatting issues that prevented complete text extraction. Information from these documents was gathered from summaries and news reports.
