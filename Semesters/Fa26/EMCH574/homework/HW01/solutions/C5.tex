The analysis uses a single-degree-of-freedom mass-spring model. The mass is rigid, the spring is massless and linear, the displacement is small, and damping is neglected. The applied force acts in the positive \(x\) direction, and the spring force opposes the displacement.

\[
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
\text{Single degree of freedom, linear spring, massless spring, rigid mass,}\\
\text{small displacement, no damping, and prescribed force }F(t)
\end{gathered}}
\qquad\text{(C.5.1)}
\]

For the mass shown in the free-body diagram, the applied force acts to the right and the spring reaction acts to the left. The force sum in the positive \(x\) direction is therefore
\[
\sum F_x=F(t)-ku(t).
\]

\centering

\caption{C.5. Free-body diagram for the forced spring-mass system.}
\end{figure}

\[
\boxed{\sum F_x=F(t)-ku(t)}
\qquad\text{(C.5.2)}
\]

Newton's law of motion relates this force sum to the mass acceleration. Thus,
\[
\sum F_x=m\ddot u(t)=F(t)-ku(t).
\]

\[
\boxed{\sum F_x=m\ddot u(t)}
\qquad\text{(C.5.3)}
\]

Rearranging the force balance gives the equation of motion,
\[
m\ddot u(t)+ku(t)=F(t).
\]
Dividing by \(m\) and using \(\omega_n^2=k/m\) gives the equivalent normalized form
\[
\ddot u(t)+\omega_n^2u(t)=\frac{F(t)}{m},
\qquad \omega_n=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}.
\]

\[
\boxed{m\ddot u(t)+ku(t)=F(t),\qquad \omega_n=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}}
\qquad\text{(C.5.4)}
\]

The right-hand side is nonzero, so the equation is nonhomogeneous. Its general response is the sum of a complementary solution and a particular solution,
\[
u(t)=u_c(t)+u_p(t),
\]
where \(u_c(t)\) satisfies the homogeneous equation and \(u_p(t)\) satisfies the forced equation.

\[
\boxed{u(t)=u_c(t)+u_p(t)}
\qquad\text{(C.5.5)}
\]

Setting the force to zero gives the homogeneous equation \(m\ddot u_c+ku_c=0\). Its undamped solution is
\[
u_c(t)=A\cos(\omega_nt)+B\sin(\omega_nt).
\]

\[
\boxed{u_c(t)=A\cos(\omega_nt)+B\sin(\omega_nt)}
\qquad\text{(C.5.6)}
\]

For the suddenly applied load, the force is switched on at \(t=0\) and then held constant. Therefore, for \(t>0\),
\[
F(t)=F_0,
\qquad m\ddot u+ku=F_0.
\]

\[
\boxed{F(t)=F_0\ \text{for}\ t>0}
\qquad\text{(C.5.7)}
\]

Because the forcing function is constant, take the particular solution to be a constant \(u_p=C\). Its derivatives vanish, so substitution into the nonhomogeneous equation gives
\[
m(0)+kC=F_0.
\]
Solving for \(C\) gives the particular solution
\[
u_p(t)=\frac{F_0}{k}.
\]

\[
\boxed{u_p(t)=\frac{F_0}{k}}
\qquad\text{(C.5.8)}
\]

Adding the complementary and particular parts gives the complete response,
\[
u(t)=A\cos(\omega_nt)+B\sin(\omega_nt)+\frac{F_0}{k}.
\]

\[
\boxed{u(t)=A\cos(\omega_nt)+B\sin(\omega_nt)+\frac{F_0}{k}}
\qquad\text{(C.5.9)}
\]

The mass starts at the unloaded position and is initially at rest. Hence,
\[
u(0)=0,\qquad \dot u(0)=0.
\]
Evaluating the displacement condition gives
\[
0=A+\frac{F_0}{k},
\qquad A=-\frac{F_0}{k}.
\]
Differentiating the complete response and applying the velocity condition gives
\[
0=\omega_nB,
\qquad B=0.
\]
Substituting these constants into the complete response gives
\[
u(t)=\frac{F_0}{k}\left[1-\cos(\omega_nt)\right],\qquad t>0.
\]

\[
\boxed{A=-\frac{F_0}{k},\qquad B=0,\qquad u(t)=\frac{F_0}{k}\left[1-\cos(\omega_nt)\right]}
\qquad\text{(C.5.10)}
\]

The static displacement is obtained by setting the acceleration to zero and allowing the force to act without vibration. Thus,
\[
u_{\mathrm{st}}=\frac{F_0}{k}.
\]
The dynamic response is largest when \(\cos(\omega_nt)=-1\), which first occurs at \(t=\pi/\omega_n\). At that instant,
\[
u_{\max}=\frac{F_0}{k}[1-(-1)]=\frac{2F_0}{k}=2u_{\mathrm{st}}.
\]
Therefore, the dynamic amplification factor is
\[
\frac{u_{\max}}{u_{\mathrm{st}}}=2.
\]

\[
\boxed{u_{\mathrm{st}}=\frac{F_0}{k},\qquad u_{\max}=u_{\mathrm{dyn}}=\frac{2F_0}{k}=2u_{\mathrm{st}},\qquad \mathrm{DAF}=2}
\qquad\text{(C.5.11)}
\]

The suddenly applied force produces twice the static displacement because the mass has not yet moved when the load is switched on. The mass passes through the static equilibrium position with nonzero velocity, reaches the first turning point at \(\pi/\omega_n\), and then returns. With no damping, this oscillation continues with constant amplitude about the static displacement.
