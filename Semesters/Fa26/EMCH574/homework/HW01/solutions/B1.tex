I take displacement \(u(t)\) as positive to the right and measure it from the equilibrium position. I assume one degree of freedom, a point mass \(m\), a massless linear spring with stiffness \(k\), frictionless horizontal motion, and no damping or external force. Since the spring force opposes the displacement, its horizontal component is

\[\sum F_x=-ku(t)\]

Applying Newton's second law to the mass gives

\[\boxed{\sum F_x=m\ddot u(t)}\qquad\text{(B.1.2)}\]

Equating the spring force and the inertial force produces the equation of motion,

\[\boxed{m\ddot u(t)+ku(t)=0}\qquad\text{(B.1.3)}\]

Since \(m\) is nonzero, division by \(m\) gives the normalized governing equation,

\[\boxed{\ddot u(t)+\frac{k}{m}u(t)=0}\qquad\text{(B.1.4)}\]

Since the equation is linear with constant coefficients, the exponential trial response \(u(t)=e^{st}\) applies. Its derivatives are \(\dot u(t)=se^{st}\) and \(\ddot u(t)=s^2e^{st}\), so substitution gives

\[\boxed{(ms^2+k)e^{st}=0}\qquad\text{(B.1.5)}\]

Because \(e^{st}\) is never zero, the factor multiplying it must vanish. Therefore, the characteristic equation is

\[\boxed{ms^2+k=0}\qquad\text{(B.1.6)}\]

The undamped natural frequency is defined by

\[\boxed{\omega_n=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}}\qquad\text{(B.1.7)}\]

The characteristic equation then becomes \(s^2+\omega_n^2=0\), so its two roots are

\[\boxed{s_{1,2}=\pm i\omega_n}\qquad\text{(B.1.8)}\]

For positive \(m\) and \(k\), these roots have zero real part and nonzero imaginary parts. Thus, the roots are

\[\boxed{\text{Imaginary}}\qquad\text{(B.1.9)}\]

Using the two distinct roots in the exponential solution gives

\[\boxed{u(t)=C_1e^{i\omega_nt}+C_2e^{-i\omega_nt}}\qquad\text{(B.1.10)}\]

Applying Euler's identity converts this response into the equivalent real trigonometric form,

\[\boxed{u(t)=A\cos(\omega_nt)+B\sin(\omega_nt)}\qquad\text{(B.1.11)}\]

Writing \(u_0=u(0)\) and \(\dot u_0=\dot u(0)\), evaluation of the trigonometric solution at \(t=0\) gives \(A=u_0\). Differentiation followed by evaluation at \(t=0\) gives \(\dot u_0=\omega_n B\), so \(B=\dot u_0/\omega_n\). Therefore, the three requested initial-condition cases are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\boxed{u(t)=u_0\cos(\omega_nt)}&\quad\text{(B.1.12.i)}\\[3pt]
\boxed{u(t)=\frac{\dot u_0}{\omega_n}\sin(\omega_nt)}&\quad\text{(B.1.12.ii)}\\[3pt]
\boxed{u(t)=u_0\cos(\omega_nt)+\frac{\dot u_0}{\omega_n}\sin(\omega_nt)}&\quad\text{(B.1.12.iii)}
\end{aligned}
\]
