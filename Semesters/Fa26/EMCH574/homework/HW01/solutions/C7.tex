I follow the flex-beam-pendulum approximation on PDF page 252 of the course notes. The beam is represented by a massless, linearly elastic cantilever and the attached body by a point mass. Motion is small, planar, and undamped; distributed beam inertia and rotary inertia of the attached mass are neglected. The course model uses the unloaded cantilever shape to relate transverse tip displacement \(u\) to tip slope \(\theta\).

For a rectangular cross-section with \(b>h\), the minor and major second moments of area are
\[
\boxed{I_1=\frac{bh^3}{12},\qquad I_2=\frac{hb^3}{12}}
\qquad\text{(C.7.1)}
\]
Minor-axis motion is along the section dimension \(h\), perpendicular to the bending axis. I take this transverse displacement direction as positive \(x\).

For an unloaded cantilever with transverse tip force \(H\), the beam table on course-note PDF page 250 gives
\[
u=\frac{HL^3}{3EI_1},\qquad \theta=\frac{HL^2}{2EI_1}.
\]
Consequently, the elastic tip stiffness and slope relation are
\[
\boxed{k_1=\frac{3EI_1}{L^3},\qquad
\frac{\theta}{u}=\frac{3}{2L}}
\qquad\text{(C.7.2)}
\]
The factor \(3/(2L)\) comes from dividing the tip-slope formula by the tip-displacement formula.

Following the course notes, resolve the restoring terms in the local transverse direction and use \(\sin\theta\approx\theta\). The approximate balance is
\[
\boxed{\sum F_u\approx-k_1u-mg\theta=m\ddot u}
\qquad\text{(C.7.4)}
\]
This is the course's projected-force approximation. The term \(mg\theta\) is not a separate horizontal component of weight in fixed Cartesian coordinates.

Substituting \(\theta=3u/(2L)\) into the balance gives
\[
m\ddot u+k_1u+\frac{3mg}{2L}u=0.
\]
Thus, the effective stiffness and equation of motion are
\[
\boxed{k_{\mathrm{eff},1}=k_1+\frac{3mg}{2L},\qquad
m\ddot u+k_{\mathrm{eff},1}u=0}
\qquad\text{(C.7.5)}
\]
Dividing by mass identifies the course-model natural frequency,
\[
\boxed{\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}=
\sqrt{\frac{k_1}{m}+\frac{3g}{2L}}
=\sqrt{\frac{3EI_1}{mL^3}+\frac{3g}{2L}}}
\qquad\text{(C.7.6)}
\]
The normalized governing equation is
\[
\boxed{\ddot u+\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}^2u=0}
\qquad\text{(C.7.7)}
\]

This has the same mathematical form as B.1, with the spring stiffness replaced by \(k_{\mathrm{eff},1}\). The characteristic roots are \(s_{1,2}=\pm i\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}\), giving
\[
\boxed{u(t)=A\cos(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)
+B\sin(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)}
\qquad\text{(C.7.8)}
\]
The initial conditions \(u(0)=u_0\) and \(\dot u(0)=\dot u_0\) determine \(A=u_0\) and \(B=\dot u_0/\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}\). Hence,
\[
\boxed{u(t)=u_0\cos(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)
+\frac{\dot u_0}{\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}}
\sin(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)}
\qquad\text{(C.7.9)}
\]

For major-axis bending, use \(I_2\) in the elastic stiffness. The course gravity contribution is unchanged,
\[
\boxed{\omega_{n,2}=\sqrt{\frac{3EI_2}{mL^3}+\frac{3g}{2L}},
\qquad \frac{I_2}{I_1}=\left(\frac{b}{h}\right)^2}
\qquad\text{(C.7.10)}
\]
For \(b>h\), major-axis motion has the higher frequency. The frequency ratio approaches \(b/h\) only when the elastic term dominates gravity in both directions.

\subsubsection*{Model qualification.}
The course result retains the unloaded shape and tip-slope approximation. A different model solves the massless beam under its tensile preload \(P=mg\), allowing the deflected shape to change with tension. That model gives
\[
k_T=\frac{P}{L-\tanh(\lambda L)/\lambda},
\qquad \lambda=\sqrt{\frac{P}{EI_1}},\qquad
\omega_T=\sqrt{\frac{k_T}{m}},
\]
as reported by Likins and Barbera~\cite{Likins1971Spacecraft}. It is not algebraically equivalent to the course approximation. The answers and primary plot in C.8 use the course expression from (C.7.6); an explicitly labeled numerical comparison is provided separately.
