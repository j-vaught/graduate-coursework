The beam is uniform and rectangular with \(b>h\). The mass is modeled as a rigid point mass at the beam tip, and the beam is modeled as a massless, linear-elastic cantilever. The displacement \(u(t)\) is measured horizontally from the undeflected vertical position, and positive \(u\) is taken in the \(+x\) direction.

\[
I_1=\frac{bh^3}{12},\qquad I_2=\frac{hb^3}{12}.
\]

Because \(h\) is the smaller cross-section dimension, bending about the \(x\) axis uses the minor second moment \(I_1\), while bending about the \(y\) axis uses the major second moment \(I_2\). Therefore,

\[
\boxed{I_1=\frac{bh^3}{12},\qquad I_2=\frac{hb^3}{12}}
\qquad\text{(C.7.1)}
\]

For the prescribed motion, the beam flexes about \(I_1\). Treat the beam as an equivalent cantilever spring with tip displacement \(u\). A transverse tip force \(P\) produces the static cantilever response

\[
u=\frac{PL^3}{3EI_1},
\qquad
\theta=\frac{PL^2}{2EI_1},
\]

where \(\theta\) is the small tip slope. Eliminating \(P\) gives

\[
k_1=\frac{P}{u}=\frac{3EI_1}{L^3},
\qquad
\theta=\frac{3}{2L}u.
\]

Thus, the elastic restoring force is represented by \(k_1u\), while the tip slope needed for the gravity term is \(3u/(2L)\).

\[
\boxed{k_1=\frac{3EI_1}{L^3},\qquad \theta=\frac{3u}{2L}}
\qquad\text{(C.7.2)}
\]

The free-body diagram contains the elastic restoring force \(k_1u\), the horizontal component of the weight \(mg\sin\theta\), and the positive displacement direction. The left panel shows the cantilever bending shape and defines the tip slope. The right panel reduces the motion to the tip mass and the two restoring forces.

Taking \(+x\) in the direction of \(u\), both restoring forces act in the \(-x\) direction. The force balance is therefore

\[
\sum F_x=-k_1u-mg\sin\theta.
\]

Newton's law of motion relates this force sum to the tip-mass acceleration, so

\[
\boxed{\sum F_x=m\ddot u(t)=-k_1u-mg\sin\theta}
\qquad\text{(C.7.4)}
\]

The analysis assumes small transverse displacement and small slope, so \(\sin\theta\approx\theta\). It also assumes a uniform Euler-Bernoulli beam, linear elastic behavior, negligible beam mass, a rigid tip mass, one horizontal degree of freedom, no damping, and no torsional or axial motion. Because the beam follows the equivalent static tip-load shape, \(\theta=3u/(2L)\). Substituting these two approximations into the force balance gives

\[
m\ddot u+k_1u+mg\theta=0,
\]

\[
m\ddot u+\left(k_1+\frac{3mg}{2L}\right)u=0.
\]

Thus, gravity supplies an additional linear restoring stiffness in this model.

\[
\boxed{m\ddot u+\left(\frac{3EI_1}{L^3}+\frac{3mg}{2L}\right)u=0}
\qquad\text{(C.7.5)}
\]

Divide the linearized equation by \(m\) and identify the coefficient of \(u\) with \(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}^2\). The natural frequency of the flex-beam pendulum is

\[
\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}^2
=\frac{k_1}{m}+\frac{3g}{2L}
=\frac{3EI_1}{mL^3}+\frac{3g}{2L}.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}
=\sqrt{\frac{3EI_1}{mL^3}+\frac{3g}{2L}}}
\qquad\text{(C.7.6)}
\]

Using this definition, the equation of motion takes the normalized governing form

\[
\ddot u+\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}^2u=0.
\]

The equivalent stiffness is \(k_{\mathrm{eff}}=k_1+3mg/(2L)\), so the same equation can also be written as \(m\ddot u+k_{\mathrm{eff}}u=0\).

\[
\boxed{\ddot u+\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}^2u=0,\qquad
k_{\mathrm{eff}}=k_1+\frac{3mg}{2L}}
\qquad\text{(C.7.7)}
\]

Problem B.1 has the form \(m\ddot u+ku=0\) with natural frequency \(\sqrt{k/m}\). The flex-beam pendulum has the same undamped single-degree-of-freedom form after replacing \(k\) by \(k_{\mathrm{eff}}\). The characteristic roots are \(s=\pm i\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}\), so the general real solution is

\[
u(t)=A\cos(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)
 +B\sin(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t).
\]

\[
\boxed{u(t)=A\cos(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)
 +B\sin(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)}
\qquad\text{(C.7.8)}
\]

Let the initial displacement and velocity be

\[
u(0)=u_0,\qquad \dot u(0)=\dot u_0.
\]

Evaluating the general solution at \(t=0\) gives \(A=u_0\). Differentiating the solution gives

\[
\dot u(t)=-A\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}\sin(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)
 +B\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}\cos(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t).
\]

At \(t=0\), this becomes \(\dot u_0=B\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}\), so \(B=\dot u_0/\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}\). Substitution gives the response for simultaneous initial displacement and initial velocity.

\[
\boxed{u(t)=u_0\cos(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)
 +\frac{\dot u_0}{\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}}
 \sin(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}t)}
\qquad\text{(C.7.9)}
\]

If flexure occurs about the major second moment \(I_2\), replace \(I_1\) with \(I_2\) in the elastic stiffness. The new stiffness and natural frequency are

\[
k_2=\frac{3EI_2}{L^3},
\qquad
\omega_{n,2}
=\sqrt{\frac{3EI_2}{mL^3}+\frac{3g}{2L}}.
\]

Since

\[
\frac{I_2}{I_1}=\left(\frac{b}{h}\right)^2,
\]

the major-axis elastic stiffness is larger by \((b/h)^2\). The gravity term is unchanged because it depends on \(m\), \(g\), and \(L\), not on the bending axis. The corresponding initial-value response is obtained by replacing \(\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}\) with \(\omega_{n,2}\).

\[
\boxed{\omega_{n,2}
=\sqrt{\frac{3EI_2}{mL^3}+\frac{3g}{2L}},
\qquad
\frac{I_2}{I_1}=\left(\frac{b}{h}\right)^2}
\qquad\text{(C.7.10)}
\]

For \(b>h\), \(\omega_{n,2}>\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}\), so the major-axis motion oscillates faster and has a shorter period. In the limit that elastic stiffness dominates the gravity contribution, the frequency ratio approaches

\[
\frac{\omega_{n,2}}{\omega_{n,\mathrm{FBP}}}
\longrightarrow \sqrt{\frac{I_2}{I_1}}=\frac{b}{h}.
\]
