## Verified prior work

Panagopoulos and Soraghan, *Small-target detection in sea clutter*, IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing 42(7), 1355–1361, 2004, DOI [10.1109/TGRS.2004.827259](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2004.827259). The primary paper demonstrates temporal and morphological target extraction in real coherent staring-radar data, including a sea-anchored soft-drink can obscured in some wave frames. The main acquisition was 9.75 GHz, HH polarization, sea state 2–3. This establishes that the phenomenon and target extraction are prior art. It does not establish the proposed prospective state-conditioned interruption predictor with fixed false-alarm calibration and distinct-condition holdouts. The proposal makes a bounded increment claim over the cited studies, not a universal priority claim.

Plant and Farquharson, *Wave shadowing and modulation of microwave backscatter from the ocean*, Journal of Geophysical Research. Oceans 117, C08010, 2012, DOI [10.1029/2012JC007912](https://doi.org/10.1029/2012JC007912). [Publisher full text](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2012JC007912). Measured X-band sea-backscatter results challenge a simple binary geometric-shadow explanation for weak deep-water returns. Power modulation, noise-floor effects, look direction, and polarization matter. This source studies ocean backscatter, not small-target detection. Consequently, low radar amplitude must not be relabeled as physical wave occlusion without independent evidence.

Wijaya and van Groesen, *Determination of the significant wave height from shadowing in synthetic radar images*, Ocean Engineering 114, 204–215, 2016, DOI [10.1016/j.oceaneng.2016.01.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2016.01.011). [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0029801816000226) and [author thesis containing the published chapter](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/13493346/thesis_AP_Wijaya.pdf). This work estimates significant wave height from synthetic shadow images for idealized linear seas. Geometric shadowing supplies the synthetic imaging model; tilt and hydrodynamic modulation are outside that model. Its retrieval accuracy is not experimental target-detection accuracy. Crossref metadata confirms author initials A.P. Wijaya and E. van Groesen.

Janssen, Belmont, Christmas, and Ferrier, *Diffraction effects in RADAR illumination of sea waves*, Applied Ocean Research 174, 105183, September 2026, DOI [10.1016/j.apor.2026.105183](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apor.2026.105183). [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S014111872600266X). Its two-dimensional path-sum analysis predicts incident illumination near geometric shadows for a vertically polarized 10 GHz wave-profiling configuration. The article explicitly does not calculate backscatter. Numerical convergence is not measured illumination or target-detection validation. Transferring this mechanism to a target endpoint requires a separate propagation and scattering model. The proposed course study uses an idealized continuous-transmission surrogate first and treats a richer crest model as bounded development, not a completed reproduction of this paper.

## Analytical illustration provenance

[ITU-R P.526-16, *Propagation by diffraction*, November 2025](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.526) supplies the isolated knife-edge parameter and approximate loss in Section 4.1. The [official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.526-16-202511-I!!PDF-E.pdf) was inspected. The obstacle model is highly idealized and assumes isolated-path geometry. A reflective moving sea crest does not satisfy its assumptions automatically. The approximation is used only to illustrate continuous attenuation around geometric clearance and to supply a numerical limiting check.

Figure B1 evaluates the forced-oscillator transfer magnitude at 301 frequency ratios from 0 to 3 with assumed damping ratios 0.12 and 0.30. It is an analytical response, not a fitted hydrodynamic response. Figure B2 evaluates the ITU approximation at 301 normalized heights from −1.5 to 1.5. Loss is set to zero at and below −0.78. The computed grazing-clearance loss is 6.032852209 dB, one-way power transmission is 0.249295695, and reciprocal two-way target-power multiplier is 0.062148344. These numbers do not constitute radar detection results.

The data generator is `src/analytical-data.mjs`; the editable figure sources are under `figures/src/`. `data/analytical.json` stores all curves and `data/checks.json` stores numerical checks. `src/analytical_checks.m` implements equivalent MATLAB calculations and assertions. The JavaScript generator was executed successfully. The MATLAB source was reviewed but not executed because no MATLAB runtime was used in this preparation. The course implementation and benchmark analysis are future deliverables. ## Benchmark contract

The [official IPIX Dartmouth index](https://soma.ece.mcmaster.ca/ipix/dartmouth/index.html) and [downloadable-record table](https://soma.ece.mcmaster.ca/ipix/dartmouth/datasets.html) establish coherent radar records, target primary and secondary bins, and environmental summaries. The target table identifies a one-meter spherical styrofoam block wrapped in wire mesh, with reported average target-to-clutter ratio 0–6 dB. This is a particular test object, not a collection of generic low-profile vessels. The primary source provides 14 target records longer than two minutes. It does not establish time-synchronized local wave surfaces or target-pose truth. No IPIX files were downloaded or processed in this preparation. Any later use must honor the data page's attribution and results-reporting conditions.

The [CANOE data reference](https://github.com/utiasASRL/pycanoe/blob/main/DATA_REFERENCE.md) documents rotating Navtech RAS6 polar-intensity radar scans, cameras, inertial measurements, and navigation data. These public documentation records do not establish complex I/Q, independent wave-probe measurements, or target detection truth. Therefore existing CANOE navigation data are not used as evidence validating wave-shadow target physics in this proposal. A radar operating frequency is not asserted from a data reference that does not specify it.

## Evaluation controls and unresolved risks

The three propagation arms are generative ablations. The prospective forecasting comparison is distinct. Within each held-out reference world, all predictors receive the same radar returns, detector decisions, and truth. Development fitting and calibration use separate entire realizations. The history logistic reference uses detection history and clutter power; the geometry predictor adds projected clearance and heave; the continuous-transmission predictor adds diffraction power to the same features. Freeze coefficients before testing. Compare every predictor across geometric and continuous-transmission reference worlds and assumed state-measurement perturbations. A predictor should not generate its own favorable test truth.

Simulation results can identify sensitivity, model differences, and conditional forecast skill. They cannot prove which propagation model describes real sea conditions. The go/no-go endpoint requires improved held-out Brier score across multiple reference worlds and wave regimes, a realization-level confidence interval excluding zero, and acceptable common-detector calibration. A negative result remains informative. Field validation requires independent synchronized wave profiles, known target motion and geometry, calibrated coherent radar, receiver settings, and distinct sea-state acquisitions. Multipath, scattering fluctuations, polarization mismatch, three-dimensional seas, phase error, and target dynamics remain material risks.
