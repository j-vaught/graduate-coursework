\subsection{Revisit phase and elapsed outage duration}

With a uniform starting phase, every independently scheduled visit samples the same marginal wave-phase distribution. Expected usability is therefore independent of the clock in a regular wave with exact spoke registration and fixed margin. The sequence of failures still depends on the phase increment. For a 0.42 Hz wave giving $3^\circ$ peak pitch we simulated 256 starting phases per schedule over 480 s. Gaps intersecting a record boundary contribute their observed duration. An entirely missed record counts as 480 s.

Nominal period (s) & Constant (s) & $\pm5\%$ (s) & $\pm10\%$ (s) \\
\midrule
1.25   & 2.5 & 2.6 & 2.7 \\

Mean usability ranged from 64.6 to 65.3\% across Table~\ref{tab:outage-clock}. At exact lock, 90 of 256 constant-period records produced no update. Interval variation reduced those outages but lengthened the upper-tail gaps for the 2.50 s clock. Replacing the regular wave by a carrier and two sidebands at $\pm2\%$ with matched elevation variance increased usable fractions to about 72\%. At periods of 1.25, 2.50, and 3.75 s, the gap percentiles increased to 3.75, 27.5, and 11.25 s. A better average fraction thus coexisted with longer outages.
