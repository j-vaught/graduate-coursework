\subsection{Revisit phase and elapsed outage duration}

For a regular wave, let $A(\varphi)$ indicate acquisition at encounter phase $\varphi$.
With exact per-spoke registration, acquisition also determines usability.
A uniformly distributed initial phase makes each visit phase $\varphi_j=(\varphi_0+2\pi f t_j)\bmod 2\pi$ uniform for any independently prescribed revisit schedule.
The expected usable fraction therefore equals the fraction of one cycle for which $A(\varphi)=1$.
Successive outcomes still depend on the phase increment between visits.
At exact synchronization, each realization repeatedly samples the same part of the wave cycle.

To compare outage durations at fixed wave forcing, we held the $0.42$ Hz wave and $3^\circ$ peak pitch fixed and simulated 256 uniformly spaced initial phases over $480$ s each.
We compared constant scan periods with independently perturbed intervals uniformly distributed within $\pm5\%$ and $\pm10\%$ of the nominal period.
These interval bounds are prescribed sensitivity inputs.
An outage ends at the next usable update, and gaps intersecting the start or end of a record contribute only their duration inside the window.
A record with no usable update consequently contributes $480$ s.

The mean usable fraction remained within $64.6$--$65.3\%$ across the twelve schedules, while the longest update gaps changed substantially (Table~\ref{tab:outage-clock}).
At exact synchronization, 90 of 256 constant-period records had no usable update, so the reported percentile reached the window boundary.
Interval variation released those records from a fixed wave phase.
At $2.50$ s, the constant schedule advances $0.05$ cycles past one full wave cycle per visit.
Here interval variation increased the longest gaps, consistent with individual sequences advancing more slowly through the phases where echoes were below threshold.

To test finite wave bandwidth at constant scan intervals, we redistributed the regular wave's elevation variance across a $0.42$ Hz carrier and two sidebands at $\pm2\%$ of the carrier frequency, with weights $0.84$, $0.08$, and $0.08$. We paired the carrier phases across 256 records and drew independent sideband phases. Both spectra had $0.131$ m root-mean-square elevation and $2.12^\circ$ root-mean-square pitch, and the summed surface slope was bounded by $0.195$. The mean usable fraction rose from $64.9$--$65.1\%$ to $71.9$--$72.0\%$. The 95th-percentile longest gaps at scan periods of $1.25$, $2.50$, and $3.75$ s increased from $2.5$, $20.0$, and $7.5$ s to $3.75$, $27.5$, and $11.25$ s, respectively. Refining the path from 301 to 601 samples preserved all six gap percentiles. The $2.50$ s scan produced the longest gaps under both spectra.
