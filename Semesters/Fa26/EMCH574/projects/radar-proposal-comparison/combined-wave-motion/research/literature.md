# Literature and evidence record

Prepared 6 October 2026 by J.C. Vaught for the proposed EMCH 574 study, *Predicting Small-Target Radar Reliability Under Wave-Driven Vessel Motion*. The report supports a coupled, prospective chain. A shared directional wave realization drives vessel and target motion, a rotating radar pose and beam-coverage calculation, and a wave-path transmission model. The resulting detector outage and conditional localization error are evaluated on held-out realizations. No synchronized wave, vessel-pose, target-truth, and coherent-radar acquisition has been completed for this proposal.

## Proposed coupling and restrictions

For each directional component, the wave phase is advanced at encounter frequency $omega_e=omega+kUcoseta$ using the convention in the cited course appendix. A linear response operator $H_q(omega_e,eta)$ maps wave amplitude to vessel mode $q\in\{3,4,5\}$ for heave, roll, and pitch. The target heave is generated from the same wave realization but is assigned a separate transfer operator. This shared forcing is the causal link required for evaluating whether vessel attitude, target motion, and path clearance create correlated outages.

The radar arm maps pose at each ray timestamp into azimuth, elevation, and beam gain. The propagation arm compares an isolated-edge attenuation calculation with a continuous incident-field surrogate. The detector arm reports outage probability and conditional localization error under common detector calibration. Existing records establish pieces of this chain, but no cited source supplies synchronized local wave geometry, vessel six-degree-of-freedom truth, target truth, and calibrated coherent radar. Independent synchronized measurement remains an experimental requirement. Public navigation or radar archives therefore support feasibility checks and model stress tests only; they do not validate the full coupling.

The novelty claim is bounded. Prior detection, backscatter, diffraction, motion-distortion, registration, and wave-compensation studies motivate the components and constrain interpretation. The proposed contribution is their prospective state-conditioned comparison under common wave forcing, with held-out realizations and separate propagation arms. No universal first claim is made.
