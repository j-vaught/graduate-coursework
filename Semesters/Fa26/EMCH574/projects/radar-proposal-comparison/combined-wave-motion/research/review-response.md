# Internal review and revision of the IDETC manuscript

The reviewers read the complete eight-page draft at commit `b1ac6c9`. The review brief gave each reviewer the author's four concerns about prior work, nonlinear modeling, the basis for the assumptions, and the engineering purpose. It also asked for independent findings. Claude Opus 5.5 at xhigh recommended major revision. Gemini 4 Hydrogen at XHigh recommended rejection of that draft. Two Sol xhigh reviewers separately evaluated the vibration analysis and marine-radar methods. The recommendations apply to the draft before the changes below.

| Review finding | Revision and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Prior work did not establish what was already feasible or what remained open. | The related-work section connects pulse-time navigation, shipborne radar registration, pointing control, low-elevation small-craft returns, radar diffraction, clutter gating, nonlinear waves, and measured vessel response. The introduction identifies the engineering decision and the observed quantity that distinguishes each intervention. |
| Numerical agreement with a linear oscillator did not establish a plausible response for a small hull. | The AutoNaut comparison evaluates an independently field-fitted response at two study conditions. Its predicted peak pitches are within 12% of the prescribed values. The source does not identify the required local crest-to-pitch phase. |
| The largest reported gain depended on a linear mode and linear wave profile. | Separate controlled tests add rate-dependent quadratic pitch damping and a bound second-harmonic crest. Each recomputes the observations on matched visits. Both change the registration gain. The damping sweep can eliminate it. |
| At the forward bearing, a pitch mismatch alone cannot explain an out-of-tolerance horizontal position. | The paper derives the forward-bearing cancellation and separately changes the timestamps assigned to the antenna origin and attitude, the surge and roll response, yaw, target bearing, mount lever arm, scan offset, and synthetic measurement noise. An independent bearing-plane/range-circle solution checks the inverse geometry. |
| A 0.50 m horizontal gate is tight compared with the radar's bearing specification. | At 100 m it allocates at most 0.29 degrees to bearing error alone. Noise tests at the largest-gain cell quantify how quickly the ideal gain declines. Manufacturer accuracy is distinguished from measurement-error standard deviation. |
| Level pointing was easy to misread as an acquisition ceiling, and a deterministic threshold amplified small margin changes. | Nominal depression and zero angular loss supply separate comparators. Ray visibility, effective-edge diffraction, and Gaussian margin perturbations give materially different gains. Refinement at both negative-gain cells checks the sign and quantifies classifications sensitive to path resolution. |
| Equal visit counts compared different durations, and a regular wave can synchronize with the scan. | The elapsed-time experiment uses 480 s windows and 256 initial phases. It gives the distribution of time between usable updates under constant and perturbed intervals, including records with no update. An equal-variance three-component control retains the largest gaps at the 2.5 s scan among the three clock periods tested. |
| Several study inputs and verification outputs were hard to reproduce from the text. | The paper states the phase seed, clock offset, wave-component frequencies and weights, geometry, beam convention, path resolution, and scope of the sampling intervals. The project README links the data and provides the reproduction commands. |

The revised contribution is a simulation-based way to separate failures at acquisition, registration, and revisit. A field calibration of the local sea-to-hull phase, return margin, target height, clutter, and navigation errors remains necessary to estimate the benefit on a particular vessel. Neither the independent hull comparison nor solver convergence supplies end-to-end sea-trial validation.
