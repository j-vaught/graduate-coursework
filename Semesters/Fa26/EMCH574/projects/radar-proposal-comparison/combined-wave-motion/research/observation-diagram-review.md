## Wave obstruction

The direct path is the exact straight line from radar phase center (24,53) to target front face (149,28). The target center is (151,28). The sea surface at crest position 92 is 47.8; the direct ray there is 39.4. Therefore crest intrusion is 8.4 drawing units. Both witness marks for d_c use these computed elevations. The crest is a local maximum of the smooth surface. The buoy symbol connects to its local sea surface. White masking interrupts the geometric direct ray below the wave, so the drawing does not imply an unobstructed transmitted path. This is the obstruction geometry in Appendix B; it does not depict a calculated diffracted field.

## Beam misalignment

The centerline is at zero angle, the two half-power directions are exactly plus and minus 9 degrees, and the target direction is minus 14 degrees. Full half-power width is consequently 18 degrees. The beta and epsilon arcs use these same angles. Appendix B's Gaussian power-gain expression gives B approximately 0.187 at this target direction, hence B is below one half but remains positive. Half-power directions describe attenuation, not a hard edge of coverage. Angular values set the schematic geometry and are not measured antenna specifications. A label initially intersected the lower half-power ray; it was moved after independent review.

## Scan-time position error

This diagram is explicitly a plan view for acquisition before scan midpoint with constant heading and translation. Both range-bearing displacement vectors are (70,32), with length sqrt(5924), approximately 76.9675. Sensor translation and target mapping error are both (42,0). Thus the reconstruction shifts the one physical target by the wrongly assigned sensor displacement. The outlined crossed symbol denotes a reconstructed point, not a second target. The constant-heading case supports E_h = norm(Delta p_i) = U abs(t_i - t_mid) used in Appendix C. Heading changes and lever-arm rotation are excluded from this particular geometric example. Independent review consolidated the top equations to restore whitespace and replaced absolute-value bars with the Euclidean norm for the error vector.
