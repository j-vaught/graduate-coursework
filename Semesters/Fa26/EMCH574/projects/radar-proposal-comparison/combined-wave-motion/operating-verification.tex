\section{Model Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

The analytical response of a damped pitch mode provides an independent check on the numerical integration. For a $2.00^{\circ}$ static forcing amplitude, natural frequency $f_n=0.35$ Hz, and damping ratio $\zeta=0.15$, we integrated the forced oscillator from rest and fitted a sinusoid after 60 forcing periods. Table~\ref{tab:verification} compares the recovered amplitude and phase lag with the closed-form response below, at, and above resonance. Across the 19 ratios in Fig.~\ref{fig:verification}, the largest relative amplitude difference is $5.50\times10^{-12}$ and the largest absolute phase difference is $3.47\times10^{-10}$ degrees.

The production model uses wave slope to prescribe the forcing amplitude. At $f=0.35$ Hz and wave amplitude $0.15$ m, its assumed hull gain gives a $3.200^{\circ}$ peak pitch. An independent evaluation of the same complex response agrees with the modeled pitch time history within $1.04\times10^{-16}$ rad. The Gaussian antenna check gives a one-way power ratio of $0.5$ at half the $25^{\circ}$ beamwidth and a two-way loss of $6.021$ dB. For a constant translation of $2.00$ m/s over a $2.50$ s scan, the midpoint position error reaches the expected bound $vT/2=2.50$ m. That bound applies to the translation-only check.

For three operating cells, 32 starting phases, and 192 scheduled visits per phase, refining the line-of-sight evaluation from 301 to 2,401 points changes no acquisition or usable-visit decisions. The largest change in two-way power margin is $0.0844$ dB. Increasing the path resolution to 601 points reduces that maximum difference to $0.0211$ dB. A separate 40 s reference interval sampled at 512 and 2,048 points gives the same usable-visit fractions. These checks assess numerical resolution at the selected cases.

The scan schedule can remain synchronized with periodic waves, so we also compared uniformly spaced starting phases. At a 2.50 s scan period, 32 versus 256 phases changes the usable fraction by at most 1.17 percentage points for the reference and 0.55 percentage points for the selected matched-pitch cases. Across the three tested scan periods the largest discrepancy is 3.13 percentage points. At exact scan--wave commensurability, the longest miss run also depends on the finite observation horizon, which is reported with the operating results.
