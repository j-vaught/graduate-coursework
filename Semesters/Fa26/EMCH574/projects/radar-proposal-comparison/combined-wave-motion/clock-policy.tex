\subsection{Selecting a fixed rotation speed}

We selected among 24, 36, and 48 revolutions per minute by minimizing the predicted 95th-percentile longest update gap. The predictor rounded the encounter frequency to the nearest 120 s Fourier bin (0.00833 Hz spacing) and used a fixed half-cycle visible sector with 256 evenly spaced starting phases. Ties favored the fastest speed. We scored each speed over 480 s using 256 shared random phases for each of 40 held-out regular-wave cells. The slope limit screened 25 frequencies from 0.173 to 0.613 Hz and amplitudes of 0.17, 0.34, and 0.56 m. For rotation period $T$, the margin was $M_0+c\log_{10}(T/2.5\,\mathrm{s})$, with $M_0$ of 5 or 10 dB and $c$ of 0, 5, or 10 dB.

The rule chose 48 revolutions per minute in 38 cells. Its two switches lengthened gaps at 10 dB. At 5 dB and 0.3013 Hz, switching to 36 shortened the gap percentile by 0.42 s at 0.17 m amplitude and lengthened it by 95--129 s at 0.34 m. With 5 dB and full dwell scaling, fixed 36 outperformed fixed 48 in 19 cells and fixed 24 outperformed it in three. The remaining echo margin can therefore change the speed ranking at the same encounter frequency.
