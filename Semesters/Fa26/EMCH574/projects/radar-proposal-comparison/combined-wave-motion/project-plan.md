# Wave-driven limits of radar motion correction on small vessels

## Final deliverable

The EMCH 574 final report and the ASME IDETC manuscript are the same paper. The intended reader is a designer deciding whether time-resolved attitude correction will keep buoy observations usable on a moving vessel, or whether antenna pointing warrants a hardware change. The study uses simulation, published specifications, and existing public evidence. It requires no boat, radar purchase, or field campaign.

The paper asks one question. **Across what wave and scan conditions does correcting each radar spoke recover usable buoy positions, and how much further improvement could ideal antenna stabilization provide?** A spoke is the measurement acquired at one antenna azimuth during a rotation. Wave forcing and the vessel’s damped pitch response connect the radar design question to EMCH 574. The proposed venue is IDETC’s Mechanical Vibration and Noise conference.

## Primary outcome and checks

A scheduled visit is usable if the modeled echo clears the frozen power threshold and its mapped position is within the declared horizontal tolerance. Divide usable visits by **all scheduled visits**. Also report the below-threshold fraction, the fraction acquired but outside tolerance, and the distribution of consecutive unusable visits. The first two processing cases must have identical acquired-return counts. Any difference reveals an implementation error because both process the same echoes. The stabilized case changes acquisition and therefore requires a fresh echo calculation.

The primary comparison is the gain in usable visits from per-spoke correction. The second comparison estimates the additional gain available under ideal pointing. Give both in percentage points with uncertainty and retain the separate failure counts so that a gain can be traced to its cause. The resulting bounds apply to the stated vessel model, threshold, antenna pattern, and wave conditions. A physical stabilizer would introduce bandwidth, travel, and latency limits.
