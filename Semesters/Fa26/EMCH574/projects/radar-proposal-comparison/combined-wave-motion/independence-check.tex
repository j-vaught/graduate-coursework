We multiplied the acquisition and position-pass fractions over all scheduled visits to test independence. Only 11 of 111 noiseless cells yielded a registration gain. The product matched midpoint usability in the other 100. In the 11 informative cells it underestimated midpoint usability in seven and overestimated it in four by up to 3.75 points. At 0.30375 Hz and 0.488 m, underestimating midpoint usability overstated the registration gain by 9.11 points (4.39 paired versus 13.51 under independence, rounded separately). Resampling whole phase records gave a 95\% overstatement interval of 9.02--9.20 points. Both noiseless spoke-time arms factorized exactly because registration always passed.

With 0.25 m range and $0.25^\circ$ bearing noise, the product minus joint registration gain for correcting delay $d$ is $\operatorname{Cov}(A,M_d)-\operatorname{Cov}(A,M_0)$, with acquisition $A$ and position pass $M$ evaluated on the same visits across all phase records. For body pointing, the median, 90th percentile, and maximum absolute differences were 0.12, 0.95, and 2.84 points at 625 ms and 0.01, 0.11, and 0.38 at 50 ms.
