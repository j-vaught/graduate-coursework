\section*{Abstract}

The ultimate goal of this work is to predict whether the next radar scan will detect a small maritime target (within an acceptable position error) and identify the physical 'blockage?' when detectoin fails.


\section{Introduction}

In oceans, lakes, and other bodies of water(henceforth referred to as maritime), small targets can disappear or `fade' in and out of radar returns. behind wave geometry, move outside a pitching radar beam, or remain detectable while its mapped position is distorted by motion during the scan. Those failures share a forcing source. Water waves move both the radar vessel and the target while changing the path between them. Treating propagation and scan correction separately therefore leaves a sponsor without a complete explanation of when an observation is usable. This project will connect the mechanisms in one predictive model and produce an operating envelope for small-target sensing.

\section{The hypothesis and contribution}

The hypothesis is that a shared estimate of wave phase, vessel response, and target motion improves one-scan-ahead forecasts of usable observations beyond detection history, motion-only features, or propagation-only features. The contribution is a controlled test of their interaction, followed by a decision map linking failure regimes to corrective action. Existing work establishes small-target detection in sea clutter, diffraction in wave illumination, and motion compensation for rotating radar. \cite{Panagopoulos2004SmallTarget,Janssen2026Diffraction,Lund2018SeaIceDrift,Burnett2021MotionDistortion} The proposed increment is to evaluate detection interruptions and conditional position quality together under the same evolving physical state and the same held-out observations.

\section{The coupled approach}

A MATLAB model will propagate prescribed linear waves and drive damped roll, pitch, and heave modes of the radar vessel and a separate target-heave mode. Shared spatial phase will determine the forcing at each body. Vessel heading and speed will change encounter frequency, while sensor separation will move the radar origin as the vessel rotates. At each azimuth timestamp, the model will calculate pointing, vertical beam gain, wave-path clearance, target illumination, and the resulting two-way echo with clutter and receiver noise. A fixed detector will supply misses; acquisition-time geometry will supply position error when a return is available.

This chain gives the project a direct connection to vibration response, resonance, damping, phase, wave propagation, and sampling. The initial scope uses linear modes, nonbreaking waves, known synthetic target geometry, and a transparent diffraction surrogate. A geometric visibility calculation and an ideal knife-edge limit will precede a richer two-dimensional crest model. Ray-by-ray pose correction will be evaluated separately from beam and propagation loss, because coordinate correction cannot recreate an echo that was never acquired.

\section{The sponsor's outcome}

The sponsor will receive a reproducible way to distinguish three engineering responses. Scan distortion can justify time-resolved pose correction. Beam loss can justify a different mounting height, vertical coverage, or scan rate. Propagation loss can justify wave-state sensing or more conservative target tracking. The project will characterize these choices before considering a controller, creating a practical result from the course mechanics rather than expanding immediately into autonomous control.

\section{The benchmark that decides the hypothesis}

The primary study will separate physical ablations from forecasting methods. Four prescribed truth conditions will retain neither vessel-motion nor wave-path modulation, motion alone, propagation alone, or their shared forcing. Within every held-out condition, all predictors will receive identical radar observations and usable-observation labels. A history reference, a motion-feature predictor, a propagation-feature predictor, and a coupled predictor will share the same fitting and calibration procedure. Complete wave realizations will be split into development and test sets before fitting, preventing adjacent samples from appearing in both sets.

The primary score will be the Brier score for the next scan's usable-observation probability, supported by reliability diagrams and outage forecasts. Detection probability, empirical false alarms, consecutive misses, outage duration, and position-error distributions will show the physical consequences. A declared operational correction and position tolerance will define common usable-observation labels; a separate paired comparison will test midpoint, per-azimuth horizontal-motion, and per-azimuth attitude correction. Timing, wave-state, beam-pattern, and target-height errors will test whether an apparent gain survives realistic uncertainty.

\section{Data and resources}

MATLAB and a documented synthetic scene are sufficient for the semester's central experiment. CANOE provides timestamped rotating-radar intensity scans, inertial data, and calibration for a selective motion-correction pilot. IPIX provides coherent target and sea-clutter records for detector calibration and measured interruption statistics. \cite{UTIAS2026CANOE,McMaster1993IPIX} These pilots will test separate components. A complete field test requires synchronized wave profiles along the path, vessel and target motion, known target geometry, radar calibration, and acquisition timing. The public records do not supply that combined measurement set.

Preparation already includes reproduced published-model calculations and a processed IPIX target record, documented in Appendix~\ref{app:published}. The sponsor's immediate support is project selection, review of the acceptable position tolerance, and identification of available radar or wave measurements. The course endpoint does not depend on procuring hardware. A later campaign will target conditions that distinguish the competing mechanisms.

\section{Shared wave forcing and mechanical response}

\label{app:forcing}

The main proposal relies on a common state rather than independently sampled sea conditions. With surface elevation $\eta$, component amplitude $a_j$, propagation vector $\mathbf{k}_j$, angular frequency $\omega_j$, phase $\phi_j$, water depth $h$, and gravity $g$, the first wave field is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:1}
\eta(\mathbf{x},t)=\sum_j a_j\cos(\mathbf{k}_j\cdot\mathbf{x}-\omega_j t+\phi_j),\quad\omega_j^2=g\lVert\mathbf{k}_j\rVert\tanh(\lVert\mathbf{k}_j\rVert h).
\end{equation}

Deep water gives $\omega_j^2=g\lVert\mathbf{k}_j\rVert$. For a vessel moving at constant velocity $\mathbf{U}$, substitution of its path into the phase gives $\omega_{e,j}=\omega_j-\mathbf{k}_j\cdot\mathbf{U}$. Here $\mathbf{k}_j$ points in the propagation direction, so head seas increase the encountered frequency. Speed changes forcing frequency; it does not change a mode's natural frequency in this fixed-parameter model. Linear wave-body response is the extension connecting these waves to course oscillators. \cite{MITOCW2002WaveBody}

For vessel mode $q$ representing roll or pitch, inertia $I_q$, damping $c_q$, restoring stiffness $k_q$, and wave moment $M_q(t)$ give

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:2}
I_q\ddot\theta_q+c_q\dot\theta_q+k_q\theta_q=M_q(t),\quad\omega_{n,q}=\sqrt{k_q/I_q},\quad\zeta_q=\frac{c_q}{2\sqrt{k_q I_q}}.
\end{equation}

Vessel and target heave use $m\ddot z+c\dot z+kz=F_w(t)$. The first forcing rules are linear projections of local elevation and slope, with declared amplitude and phase. Each body has its own response parameters. Harmonic forcing at frequency ratio $r=\omega_e/\omega_n$ has gain and lag

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:3}
\lvert H(r)\rvert=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1-r^2)^2+(2\zeta r)^2}},\qquad\delta(r)=\operatorname{atan2}(2\zeta r,1-r^2).
\end{equation}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{mechanism.pdf}
\caption{Shared forcing connects vessel motion and target state to acquisition-time geometry, propagation, and radar reliability. The same realization and clock must drive both branches. Radar cross section (RCS) specifies the target scattering term; position quality is assessed when the detector supplies a return.}
\label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure}

For the semester model, effective mode parameters are prescribed and swept. A measured extension can replace them with identified response-amplitude operators. Breaking waves, target wetting, nonlinear buoyancy, and large-angle coupled hydrodynamics require separate models. The state manifest will retain frame definitions, forcing phase, sign conventions, natural frequencies, damping, and all sensor offsets so these extensions can be added without changing the benchmark contract.

\section{From ray-time geometry to a usable observation}

\label{app:observation}

Mechanical response determines the radar origin and rotation at acquisition time $t_i$. With vessel reference position $\mathbf{p}_i$, body rotation $\mathbf{R}_{b,i}$, lever arm $\boldsymbol{\ell}$, and radar extrinsic rotation $\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{br}}$, use $\mathbf{s}_i=\mathbf{p}_i+\mathbf{R}_{b,i}\boldsymbol{\ell}$ and $\mathbf{R}_i=\mathbf{R}_{b,i}\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{br}}$. Slant range $\rho_i$ and azimuth $\alpha_i$ define a family indexed by unmeasured elevation $\epsilon$,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:4}
\mathbf{q}_i(\epsilon)=\mathbf{s}_i+\rho_i\mathbf{R}_i\begin{pmatrix}\cos\epsilon\cos\alpha_i\\\cos\epsilon\sin\alpha_i\\\sin\epsilon\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

A known synthetic target-height plane closes this inverse. Public two-dimensional returns retain an elevation interval or target-height assumption. A general angular displacement bound cannot substitute for the horizontal error of this geometry.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{geometry.pdf}
\caption{A moving radar and target change beam offset, direct-path clearance, and the pose used for mapping. Geometric obstruction, beam loss, and position distortion are distinct outcomes of the same state.}
\label{fig:geometry}
\end{figure}

Let $B_i$ be one-way antenna power gain at the target direction and $G_i$ the one-way propagation power factor. The illustrative Gaussian beam uses full half-power width $\beta$ and $B_i=\exp[-4\ln(2)(\epsilon_i/\beta)^2]$. For an obstructing edge of height $d_c$ above the direct ray, wavelength $\lambda$, and path distances $d_1,d_2$, the limiting diffraction parameter is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:5}
\nu=d_c\sqrt{\frac{2(d_1+d_2)}{\lambda d_1d_2}},\quad J(\nu)=6.9+20\log_{10}\!\left(\sqrt{(\nu-0.1)^2+1}+\nu-0.1\right).
\end{equation}

The illustration sets $J=0$ for $\nu\leq-0.78$ and $G=10^{-J/10}$. The maximum normalized intrusion along the path represents one dominant obstacle. This ITU-R approximation supplies a check and sensitivity arm; a reflective smooth sea crest requires richer propagation treatment. \cite{ITUR2025P526}

For baseline target power $P_{0,i}$ and reciprocal complex propagation $q_i$ with $\lvert q_i\rvert^2=G_i$, a monostatic return is $Y_i=\sqrt{P_{0,i}}B_iq_i^2 e^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{T,i}}+C_i+N_i$. Thus target power scales as $P_{0,i}B_i^2G_i^2$. Target phase, clutter $C_i$, noise $N_i$, range loss, polarization, and integration are explicit terms. Incident illumination alone is not a detection. \cite{Janssen2026Diffraction}

For detector decision $D_i$, correction method $\mathcal{C}$, horizontal error $E_i(\mathcal{C})$, and tolerance $E_{\max}$, define usable observation $U_i(\mathcal{C})=D_i\mathbf{1}\{E_i(\mathcal{C})\leq E_{\max}\}$. The probability forecast uses one frozen $\mathcal{C}$ so all predictors share labels. Separate correction experiments compare $U_i$ across mapping methods. Missing returns remain misses; they do not become zero-error positions.

\section{Published-model calculations}

\label{app:published}

The coupled illustration now has two independent literature baselines. Wijaya and van Groesen construct surface visibility from geometric obstruction. Their published harmonic case uses period $T=9$ s, depth $h=50$ m, and radar-height ratios $H_r/A=5$ and $10$. \cite{Wijaya2016Shadowing} The published chapter gives the obstruction indicator and its image average as Equations 3.4 and 3.7. \cite{Wijaya2017Reconstruction} With a surface point $\eta(r,t)$ and radar height $H_r$, the equivalent skyline test is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:6}
\begin{aligned}\chi(r,t)&=\mathbf{1}\!\left\{\frac{\eta(r,t)-H_r}{r}\geq\sup_{0<x<r}\frac{\eta(x,t)-H_r}{x}\right\},\\V(r)&=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\chi(r,t_j).\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

For $\eta=A\cos(kr-\omega t)$, exact finite-depth dispersion gives $\lambda=124.829$ m. The reproduction averages $M=1400$ uniform phases on a grid of $\lambda/400$. It reproduces the paper's numerical visibility construction rather than digitizing its graph.

Use logistic models with regularization selected only on development realizations. Predict $\Pr(U_{i+1}=1\mid\mathcal{F}_i)$ for the target's next scheduled scan, where $\mathcal{F}_i$ contains only available history and estimated state. A separate forecast predicts three consecutive missed target visits. Present-state estimates are propagated with uncertainty; future truth is excluded from predictor features. The default synthetic detector operates at $P_{\mathrm{FA}}=10^{-2}$ per declared target decision, calibrated before evaluation. Lower operating rates require enough independent clutter trials. Report the empirical rate with uncertainty rather than equating it with a nominal threshold.

Brier score and reliability are primary. Report detection rate, missed fraction, outage lengths, conditional median and 95th-percentile localization error, and processing time. Compare paired score differences with realization-level bootstrap intervals. An interaction contrast $S_M+S_W-S_C-S_H$, where $S$ is a lower-is-better score and subscripts denote motion, wave, coupled, and history predictors, quantifies nonadditive gain in this design. Direct superiority over both single-group models remains the main gate.

Hold out complete wave realizations, period/damping regimes, and acquired files. Evaluate alternate geometric and continuous-transmission truth worlds, phase-shuffled controls, perturbed timestamps, unknown elevation, and target-aspect variation. Keep one correction method and tolerance for common forecast labels; separately compare correction methods on paired retained scenes. Report misses alongside localization to prevent selective target loss from appearing as improved accuracy.

Select 32 development configurations, 8 probability-calibration configurations, and 16 held-out configurations, each with 8 independent initial-phase and clutter/noise realizations. Expand replication only when score intervals or convergence checks remain unresolved.

\subsection{Implementation and scientific risks}

The principal technical risk is interpreting an illumination surrogate as a sea-target return. The course study addresses it with explicit echo formation, geometric and continuous-transmission alternatives, and matched downstream processing. The next risk is overstating exact geometry from two-dimensional radar; target-height uncertainty remains part of the result. Finally, shared forcing can produce optimistic synthetic forecasts if state estimates are treated as perfect. Measurement errors, phase controls, and alternate truth models directly test that dependence.

These controls make the course result reviewable even when the hypothesis fails. The publication path adds independently measured joint state, characterized antenna patterns, sufficient sea-condition holdouts, and a full-text review of related compensation and target-detection work. The final research claim will follow the evidence produced by those tests.
