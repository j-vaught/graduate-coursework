\subsection{Comparison with a field-fitted pitch response}

We compared the prescribed peak pitch with a published model fitted to inertial measurements from the 5 m AutoNaut. Mounet et al. fitted a linear transfer function using measured motion spectra and modeled directional waves from a coastal deployment \cite{Mounet2024Hydrodynamic}. We evaluated their weighted-average parameter set at zero forward speed and a wave propagation angle of $25^\circ$ to the bow. Multiplying the response amplitude by the regular-wave amplitude gives $3.47^\circ$ at $0.350$ Hz and $0.150$ m, and $9.21^\circ$ at $0.3325$ Hz and $0.432$ m. The prescribed oscillator gives $3.20^\circ$ and $8.28^\circ$ at those two points.

The two models give peak pitches within $12\%$ under these conditions. This comparison establishes the scale of the prescribed amplitudes relative to a field-fitted transfer function for another hull. The calibrated spectrum magnitudes leave the phase between pitch and the local crest unresolved, and the radar study retains its prescribed frequency response and phase.

\subsection{Bounding a pointing benefit from measured margins}
A small change in angular loss can recover only visits sufficiently close to threshold. Let $m_i$ be the measured body-pointed power margin on scheduled visit $i$. If leveling can increase this margin by at most $b$ dB, the net improvement in acquisition fraction is bounded above by
\begin{equation}
 U_b=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}\{-b\le m_i<0\}.
\end{equation}
The bound counts every potentially recoverable visit. Losses on previously acquired visits can only lower the net gain. Its application requires comparable margins on all attempts, including misses.

We evaluated this count on held-out recorded returns in Dartmouth IPIX acquisition 17, a shore-mounted 9.39 GHz radar observing a 1 m wire-wrapped sphere \cite{McMaster1993IPIX}. A 99th-percentile threshold fitted to 5,120 development clutter windows gave 0.99\% crossings on 15,360 held-out clutter windows. In the primary target bin, 1,023 of 1,536 held-out 64-sweep power windows fell below threshold. Only 38 were within 0.81 dB below it. Even adding 0.81 dB to every measured window could therefore recover at most 2.47 percentage points in this record. The increment equals the reference simulation's upper bound on angular loss. IPIX measured a different target, height, and range. Its margin distribution illustrates a test a vessel designer can apply to raw radar video before investing in stabilization. It cannot calibrate wave-dependent acquisition on the modeled vessel.
