\subsection{Registration with recorded radar and vessel motion}
\label{sec:canoe-registration}

The Canadian Aquatic Navigation for Observation of the Environment (CANOE) inland-water records pair Navtech radar spokes with encoder angles and calibrated radar poses derived from satellite and inertial navigation \cite{UTIAS2026CANOE}. The pose product is interpolated on a 1 ms grid. Rotations took about 0.25 s. Calibrated range-bin spacing was 0.044 or 0.292 m, depending on mode. Sub-bin mean changes aggregate repeated registrations of the same bins. They do not establish sub-bin absolute precision. The initial virtual-point replay used the central 120 scans of two available sequences covering both range modes. At 100 m and eight bearings, 41 of 960 midpoint fixes exceeded 0.50 m in the August 21 19:16 sequence and zero in the August 28 14:50 sequence. The 95th-percentile errors were 0.470 and 0.178 m, respectively. Exact spoke-time inputs reconstructed the virtual points to numerical precision.

We compared recorded return repeatability in 22 disjoint windows across all eight public CANOE sessions. Each window contained 120 scans centered at the 25th, 50th, or 75th percentile of scan index. The 288-scan session used only its central window. We retained the strongest bin within 20--110~m on each spoke if its intensity code was at least 100 and its 2~m horizontal cell was occupied in at least 30 scans. Four rotations apart, we matched mutual nearest neighbors within 1.5~m. Cell assignment and matching used the mean of both plotted positions to apply the same rule to each registration.

Across 55,411 matched returns, the equal-session mean reduction in separation was 0.22~mm, with a paired 95\% interval of $-0.50$ to 0.94~mm across sessions. Session means ranged from $-1.20$ to 1.59~mm, and the interval grouped by recording date also spanned zero. Changing the distance gate or requiring agreement on matches gave mean reductions from $-0.05$ to 0.31~mm.

Across windows, median antenna speed was 0.62--1.53~m/s and the 95th-percentile absolute antenna heading rate was 0.24--9.94~deg/s. Using the same 112 anchor scans per window, the mean separation reductions for one-, four-, and eight-rotation lags were $-0.24$, 0.29, and 1.14~mm. At the eight-rotation lag and 80--110~m range, the session-averaged 99th percentile fell from 1.327 to 1.297~m.

As a positive control, we applied the same four-rotation matching to exact virtual echoes in all 22 windows. At 100~m, the window-averaged 95th-percentile midpoint error was 0.312~m against known positions and 0.186~m between repeated fixes. In the window with the highest 95th-percentile heading rate, those values were 1.045 and 0.438~m. Both fell to numerical zero at spoke time. A similar pose error in both scans cancels in their separation, which reduces sensitivity to absolute position error. The measured echoes therefore assess repeatability at 4~Hz. Absolute buoy position and detection performance require independent target observations.
