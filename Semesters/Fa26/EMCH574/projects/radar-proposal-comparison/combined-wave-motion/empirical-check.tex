\subsection{Registration with recorded radar and vessel motion}
\label{sec:canoe-registration}

We applied midpoint and spoke-time registration to two 30~s windows from the Canadian Aquatic Navigation for Observation of the Environment (CANOE) dataset \cite{UTIAS2026CANOE}. The windows comprise the central 120 scans of each published radar pose file for sequences \texttt{2025-08-21-19-16} and \texttt{2025-08-28-14-50}. Together they contain 96,000 radar spokes with recorded times and encoder angles. We used the calibrated radar pose from the post-processed global navigation satellite system and inertial navigation records. The recordings come from inland water and use a Navtech radar. Both windows had a median rotation time of 0.250~s and maximum spoke-to-midpoint offsets of about 0.125~s.

To isolate the timing error under this measured motion, we placed virtual stationary points at eight evenly spaced directions and at 30 and 100~m from the antenna at the center of each window. Each point was 1~m below that antenna position. For each point and scan, the recorded encoder angles identified the closest spoke. Both registrations received the same exact slant range, bearing, and point height at that spoke's time. At 100~m the 95th-percentile midpoint error was 0.470~m on August~21 and 0.178~m on August~28. The midpoint error exceeded 0.50~m in 41 of 960 visits on August~21 and zero of 960 on August~28. Across the eight 100~m points on August~21, the longest interval between consecutive usable visits fell from 2.756~s to 0.251~s with spoke-time registration. Errors at 30~m stayed below 0.50~m in both windows. Spoke-time registration reconstructed the virtual points to numerical precision, as expected from the exact inputs.

We separately compared the repeatability of recorded radar returns. From each spoke we retained the strongest range bin between 20 and 110~m with an intensity code of at least 100 on the stored 8-bit scale. We projected each bin onto the antenna scan plane and retained returns in 2~m horizontal cells occupied in at least 30 scans. For scan pairs four rotations apart, we selected mutual nearest neighbors within 1.5~m. Cell assignment and matching used the mean of the two plotted positions so that both registrations used the same return pairs. The median separation fell from 0.364 to 0.358~m across 4,516 matched returns on August~21 and rounded to 0.305~m under both registrations across 4,196 matches on August~28. These small changes measure the repeatability of persistent returns in these two windows. Absolute buoy position and detection performance require independent target observations.
